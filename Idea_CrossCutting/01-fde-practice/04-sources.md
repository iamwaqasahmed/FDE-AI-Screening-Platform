## Opened and used

### OpenAI — forward deployed engineering

**Emerging. Primary.**

- Colin Jarvis, “What FDE means at OpenAI,” 22 October 2026. https://openai.com/index/forward-deployed-engineering-means
- “Forward deployed engineering for enterprise AI,” product and FAQ page. https://openai.com/business/forward-deployed-engineering/

The essay says the team was founded in early 2025 to close a “deployment gap,” states the value “Start with the outcome, do what it takes to succeed, then follow with scale,” and describes semiconductor verification (about 15 FDEs, 10 or more use cases, production in 3–6 months, later removal of roughly 90 percent of custom code), a telecom voice effort that moved from about 60 percent to above 90 percent on the evals that mattered only after Research joined, John Deere guidance around See & Spray, and a tax pilot (7,000 returns, about 50 percent throughput, six weeks to 90 percent precision and recall on rental-property returns). The FAQ says FDEs are not professional services, are unnecessary when existing capabilities suffice, work with Product and Research, define a measurable operational outcome, and after launch leave the customer team owning the capability. DeployCo is described there as a separate subsidiary for implementation capacity.

Conflict with a16z, below: OpenAI rejects the “this is professional services” label that Schmidt uses.

### United States — digital services playbook

**Proven as the text on the live page. Branding note included.**

- Digital Services Playbook. https://playbook.usds.gov/

Fetched text shows the 13 plays and, on the same page, a logo line for the United States DOGE Service. This research does not reconstruct the organizational rename. It uses the plays as written on that URL:

1. Understand what people need
2. Address the whole experience
3. Make it simple and intuitive
4. Build with agile and iterative practices, including an MVP in no more than three months
5. Structure budgets and contracts to support delivery
6. Assign one leader and hold that person accountable
7. Bring in experienced teams
8. Choose a modern stack
9. Deploy in a flexible hosting environment
10. Automate testing and deployments
11. Manage security and privacy through reusable processes
12. Use data to drive decisions, including tail latency, alerts, and post-mortems

## Conflicts worth keeping

1. **Services or product.** Schmidt (opinion, June 2025) says FDEs are often rebranded services staff. OpenAI’s FAQ (opened) says no, and that an FDE is the wrong tool when the capability already exists. Anthropic staffs both a builder and a statement-of-work owner.
2. **Custom or reusable.** Ramp prefers a generalized feature and admits the tension with scoping. OpenAI describes deleting most custom code once the product catches up. Palantir’s opened page emphasizes implementing the customer’s solution. The unopened blogs may say more; they are not cited.
3. **When building starts.** GDS: not in discovery. US playbook: an MVP within three months. OpenAI: a useful system in production, then scale. These are sequenced phases, not synonyms.
4. **Who codes.** Anthropic’s deployment lead does not. Anthropic’s FDE does. Government manuals center a product owner who may not be the engineer.
5. **Title history.** Hollands says Palantir coined the title in 2011. The opened Palantir page confirms Delta, Echo, and Dev. It does not confirm 2011.
6. **DGA version line.** WCAG 2.2 Level AA in the body; one step title says 2.3.
