# Cross-cutting research: what FDEs actually do

This folder is an independent research pack for a curriculum redesign. It does not change the Al-Waha course. It asks what people called forward deployed engineers actually do in 2025–2026, and what a self-paced program for government employees must therefore contain.

The existing course is a rehearsal. Four modules, twenty lessons, Python, a fictional Saudi health cluster. Participants discover a problem, architect, implement, integrate, evaluate, operate, and present. They are not meant to become specialists in every field. A later real client project sits outside this repository.

## Labels

- **Proven.** Stated in a page or standard opened for this research, by the organization that does the work or by the standards body.
- **Emerging.** Stated by a company about its own 2025–2026 field practice (an essay, a product page, or a live job description). Real, but not yet a settled profession.
- **Speculative.** An opinion essay, a historical claim this research could not verify on the company’s own site, or an inference about curriculum design.

Opinion is marked as opinion. If two sources disagree, both are kept.

## Bottom line

Vendor FDEs in 2025–2026 are customer-facing engineers who start from an operational outcome, learn the domain on the engagement, connect ugly systems, evaluate against that outcome, put something in production, and then try to make the custom work unnecessary. Government delivery standards describe a different seat: do not build until the problem is understood, name one owner, shape the contract so iteration is legal, serve people who are not digital, and keep the service running.

The Al-Waha arc already rehearses the technical middle of the vendor job: approach choice, workflows, agents, sign-in, feeds, tests, deploy, data, retrieval, a measured fix, guardrails, an incident, a surge, failover, and a proposal. It is thin where the job is political, commercial, linguistic, and statistical, and where a correct answer is to stop.

The ten capabilities are in [03-recommended-capabilities.md](03-recommended-capabilities.md). A 500-word brief of those ten, with source URLs, is the companion note returned with this pack.
