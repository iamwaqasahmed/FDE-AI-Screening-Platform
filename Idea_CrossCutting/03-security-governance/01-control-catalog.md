# Control catalog

Overview comparison only. Cluster rules are fictional. Real frameworks below were read from the public pages listed in [04-sources.md](04-sources.md). ISO text is the public overview, not the paid clauses.

## Disagreements to teach in the open

**Prompt-injection filters.** OWASP LLM01:2025 says prompt injection comes from how models take input, that RAG and fine-tuning do not fully mitigate it, and that fool-proof prevention is unclear. It still lists input and output filtering as one mitigation, beside least privilege, human approval, and segregating untrusted content. The OWASP prompt-injection cheat sheet is sharper: input screening is not a security boundary, because a deny list does not define instruction versus data. NIST AI 600-1 describes direct and indirect prompt injection as information-security risks and says ordinary cybersecurity may need to adapt. Suggested actions in that profile include filtering inputs and outputs and red-teaming. Filtering appears as one action, not as the control that closes the risk. ISO/IEC 42001 and ISO/IEC 27001 do not name a prompt filter. A vendor checklist that says "the filter blocks injection" disagrees with LLM01 and with the cheat sheet. The course already sides with the stronger view for grading: direct-phrase coverage is not done.

**When the 72 hours start.** The cluster clock starts at the notice timestamp. The PDPL implementing regulation starts when the controller becomes aware, and only for incidents expected to harm personal data or data subjects or to conflict with their rights. The regulation also asks for a data-subject notice in that harm case, and it does not remove NCA reporting. Teaching the cluster clock is fine if the page says it is the case rule.

**Residency versus transfer.** The cluster forbids PHI on `ext-large`. The PDPL does not impose that product ban. It permits cross-border transfer for listed purposes when conditions, including adequate protection and no prejudice to national security, are met. Health data has further duties in the implementing regulation. A hard zone deny is a legitimate fictional control. It is not a summary of Art. 29.

**Erasure versus logs.** The lesson requires that a permitted log no longer return the person. PDPL Art. 18 requires destruction when the purpose ends, and it also requires retention for a legal period or a judicial matter. ECC event-log expectations and an ISO/IEC 27001 logging practice pull the other way. The learner should record the hold, not pretend one sentence satisfies both.

**`phi` versus statutory categories.** The catalog class `phi` includes national id, date of birth, name, and phone. PDPL "Sensitive Data" and "Health Data" are legal categories with extra rules. Do not tell learners that a field tagged `phi` is, by that tag, sensitive data under the statute.

**Lists that moved.** OWASP's 2025 top 10 matches the course family map, including system-prompt leakage (LLM07) and vector weaknesses (LLM08). The 2023 edition used a different seventh item (insecure plugin design). Older write-ups will not match `atk-prompt`.

**One certificate does not cover the others.** NIST is voluntary guidance and, as of the page read for this note, under revision. ISO/IEC 42001 can be certified by a certification body and still says nothing about a particular guardrail. ISO/IEC 27001 certifies an information-security management system. The ECC is a national cybersecurity baseline for entities in its scope, not a model-evaluation standard. The cluster stage gates G0–G5 are a fictional delivery policy.
