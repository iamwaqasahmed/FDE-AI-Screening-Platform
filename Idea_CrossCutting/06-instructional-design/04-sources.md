## Cognitive load, examples, and guidance

**Sweller, J., van Merriënboer, J. J. G., & Paas, F. (2019).** Cognitive architecture and instructional design: 20 years later. *Educational Psychology Review, 31*(2), 261–292. [https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09465-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09465-5). Open PDF: [https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10648-019-09465-5.pdf](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10648-019-09465-5.pdf).

Read. Supports: working memory is limited for novel information; element interactivity depends on the material and on expertise; worked examples direct attention to states and steps and were first reported by Sweller and Cooper (1985) in algebra; learners must study the example; completion problems (van Merriënboer & Krammer, 1987, introductory programming) force that study; the self-explanation effect is the reason a scanned example fails; expertise reversal and guidance fading mean full examples should be withdrawn as expertise grows; 4C/ID is the long-program form of the same architecture, with learning tasks, supportive information, just-in-time procedural information, and part-task practice only for automaticity. Sweller and Cooper (1985) and van Merriënboer and Krammer (1987) are used here only as that review states them. Their original pages were not opened.

**Kalyuga, S., Ayres, P., Chandler, P., & Sweller, J. (2003).** The expertise reversal effect. *Educational Psychologist, 38*(1), 23–31. [https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep3801_4](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep3801_4). Author copy: [https://ro.uow.edu.au/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1141&context=edupapers](https://ro.uow.edu.au/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1141&context=edupapers).

Opened far enough to confirm the claim: guidance that helps novices, including worked examples, can become redundant and then harmful as domain schemas form. The mechanical-trade sequence described there is the pattern used for the lab pretest.

**Pollock, E., Chandler, P., & Sweller, J. (2002).** Assimilating complex information. *Learning and Instruction, 12*(1), 61–86. [https://doi.org/10.1016/S0959-4752(01)00016-0](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0959-4752(01)00016-0).

Abstract and ERIC record opened. Supports: for novices, presenting isolated elements and only later the interacting whole improved learning of complex material; the advantage was tied to low prior knowledge and was absent when prior knowledge was higher. Does not support leaving the elements isolated. The module gate and the capstone are the recombination.

**Chi, M. T. H., Bassok, M., Lewis, M. W., Reimann, P., & Glaser, R. (1989).** Self-explanations: How students study and use examples in learning to solve problems. *Cognitive Science, 13*(2), 145–182. [https://doi.org/10.1016/0364-0213(89)90002-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/0364-0213(89)90002-5). Alternate resolver: [https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1302_1](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1302_1).

Abstract opened. Supports: while studying mechanics examples, learners who explained the conditions of steps and tied them to principles developed knowledge they could use without the example; learners who did not explain leaned on the example afterward. Supports a written four-beat note. Does not support a question answered only in the head.

**Kirschner, P. A., Sweller, J., & Clark, R. E. (2006).** Why minimal guidance during instruction does not work. *Educational Psychologist, 41*(2), 75–86. [https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_1](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_1). PDF consulted: [https://itgs.ict.usc.edu/papers/Constructivism_KirschnerEtAl_EP_06.pdf](https://itgs.ict.usc.edu/papers/Constructivism_KirschnerEtAl_EP_06.pdf).

Opened. Supports: for novices and intermediates, minimally guided discovery is less effective than guidance that explains the concepts and procedures; the advantage of external guidance shrinks when prior knowledge can guide. Use this against an unguided cluster, not against a short conceptual prediction that is followed by a written consolidation.

## Productive failure

**Sinha, T., & Kapur, M. (2021).** When problem solving followed by instruction works: Evidence for productive failure. *Review of Educational Research, 91*(5), 761–798. [https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211019105](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211019105). PDF consulted: [https://janfasen.nl/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Sinha-and-Kapur-PS-I.pdf](https://janfasen.nl/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Sinha-and-Kapur-PS-I.pdf).

Read for the estimates. Primary result for conceptual knowledge and transfer: Hedge's g = 0.36, 95% CI [0.20, 0.51], 166 comparisons. High-fidelity productive-failure designs: about g = 0.37 to 0.58. Procedural knowledge: g = −0.03, 95% CI [−0.20, 0.15], 51 comparisons. Instruction that builds on student solutions was a stronger moderator than instruction that does not. The bias-adjusted g = 0.87 is the authors' sensitivity estimate. The evidence is concentrated in school and undergraduate mathematics. A self-paced professional course can run a low-fidelity version: one committed prediction, then a consolidation written against a planted fork. It cannot claim the meta-analytic effect for procedures, for domain-general skills, or for a gold file revealed after a paste.

## Curriculum structure

**Four-component instructional design.** [https://www.4cid.org/about/](https://www.4cid.org/about/).

Page opened. Supports the four components and the rule that part-task practice is for automaticity, not the default. The practical book cited there is van Merriënboer, Kirschner, and Frèrejean, *Ten Steps to Complex Learning* (4th ed., 2024). That book was not read. Design claims about task classes are taken from this page together with the 2019 review above.

**Mayer, R. E. (2009).** Segmenting principle. In *Multimedia learning* (2nd ed.). Cambridge University Press. [https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511811678.013](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511811678.013).

Chapter abstract opened. Supports: learner-paced segments beat a continuous multimedia message for transfer in the lightning-animation studies, d = 0.98, when material is complex, fast, and the learner is inexperienced. Does not set a minute length for a text chapter.

## Time, completion, and mastery

**Guo, P. J., Kim, J., & Rubin, R. (2014).** How video production affects student engagement. *Proceedings of the First ACM Conference on Learning at Scale*, 41–50. [https://doi.org/10.1145/2556325.2566239](https://doi.org/10.1145/2556325.2566239). PDF consulted: [https://www.cs.rochester.edu/hci/pubs/pdfs/edX-MOOC-video-production-and-engagement_LAS-2014.pdf](https://www.cs.rochester.edu/hci/pubs/pdfs/edX-MOOC-video-production-and-engagement_LAS-2014.pdf).

Opened. 6.9 million sessions, four edX courses. Median engagement at most six minutes at 1x. Videos longer than nine minutes often watched less than halfway. Problem-attempt rates after videos fell from 56% in the shortest length bucket to 31% in the longest. This is watch time. It is not a lesson-length rule for this course.

**Jordan, K. (2015).** Massive open online course completion rates revisited: Assessment, length and attrition. *International Review of Research in Open and Distributed Learning, 16*(3). [https://doi.org/10.19173/irrodl.v16i3.2112](https://doi.org/10.19173/irrodl.v16i3.2112). [https://www.irrodl.org/index.php/irrodl/article/view/2112](https://www.irrodl.org/index.php/irrodl/article/view/2112).

Opened. 221 MOOCs; completion figures for 129; median completion 12.6%, range 0.7% to 52.1%. Longer courses and peer grading were associated with lower completion in the reported regression (adjusted R² about 0.59). Jordan reads this as an argument for shorter modular courses. The week-by-week curves are dominated by four-week Open2Study offerings. Jordan notes that Perna et al. (2014) did not find a length relationship. Perna et al. was not opened. These are open enrolments, not this cohort.

**Reich, J., & Ruipérez-Valiente, J. A. (2019).** The MOOC pivot. *Science, 363*(6423), 130–131. [https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aav7958](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aav7958). Supplement figures read from the author postprint: [https://webs.um.es/jruiperez/papers/journals/2019_Science_MOOCPivot_postprint.pdf](https://webs.um.es/jruiperez/papers/journals/2019_Science_MOOCPivot_postprint.pdf).

Postprint opened. HarvardX and MITx, through May 2018. Completion did not rise over six years. In 2017–18 the postprint table gives 3.13% of participants, 15.42% of those intending to complete, and 46.02% of verified-track learners. Same boundary as Jordan: not a forecast for a selected professional group.

**Bloom, B. S. (1968).** Learning for mastery. *Evaluation Comment, 1*(2). ERIC ED053419. [https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED053419](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED053419).

Record opened. Supports: define mastery, then vary instruction and time, including corrective feedback, so aptitude shows up as time rather than as a failed seat. Does not supply a percentage cut for this course.

**Kulik, C.-L. C., Kulik, J. A., & Bangert-Drowns, R. L. (1990).** Effectiveness of mastery learning programs: A meta-analysis. *Review of Educational Research, 60*(2), 265–299. [https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543060002265](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543060002265).

Abstract opened. 108 controlled evaluations. Positive exam effects, stronger for weaker students; attitudes improve; time on task may increase; self-paced mastery programs often reduce college completion. The abstract's completion warning is why core gates have to stay small. A mean effect size is not quoted here because the full tables were not opened.

**Nilson, L. B. (2015).** *Specifications grading: Restoring rigor, motivating students, and saving faculty time.* Stylus. Record of the argument: [https://doi.org/10.4324/9781003447061](https://doi.org/10.4324/9781003447061).

Publisher abstract opened. Used for the mechanism: pass/fail against one published level, revision, higher standing as a bundle of further work, no partial credit. The book is not an evaluation of this course.

**Howitz, W. J., McKnelly, K. J., & Link, R. D. (2025).** Delving into the design and implementation of specifications grading systems in higher education. *Education Sciences, 15*(1), 83. [https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci15010083](https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci15010083). [https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7102/15/1/83](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7102/15/1/83).

Opened. Review of 90 implementations. Supports the structural description (bundles, binary specs, revision) and the caution that published outcomes are still mostly instructor reports of time, performance, and student reaction.

**Fernandez, T. M., Martin, K. M., Mangum, R. T., & Bell-Huff, C. L. (2020).** Whose grade is it anyway?: Transitioning engineering courses to an evidence-based specifications grading system. ASEE, paper 28899. [https://peer.asee.org/whose-grade-is-it-anyway-transitioning-engineering-courses-to-an-evidence-based-specifications-grading-system.pdf](https://peer.asee.org/whose-grade-is-it-anyway-transitioning-engineering-courses-to-an-evidence-based-specifications-grading-system.pdf).

Opened. Faculty experience in engineering courses. Evidence that the practice is used in engineering. Not a controlled estimate of learning gain.

**Carnegie Mellon University, Eberly Center.** Specifications grading at scale, fall 2023–spring 2025. [https://www.cmu.edu/teaching/teaching-as-research/atscale/specgrading/index.html](https://www.cmu.edu/teaching/teaching-as-research/atscale/specgrading/index.html).

Page summary used. Institutional report, not a peer-reviewed meta-analysis. No difference found in performance or grade distribution. Four post-survey items, including grade clarity, favored the traditionally graded sections. Used only as a warning that binary grading does not, by itself, make the standard clearer.

## Judgment, feedback, and score use

**Kane, M. T. (2013).** Validating the interpretations and uses of test scores. *Journal of Educational Measurement, 50*(1), 1–73. [https://doi.org/10.1111/jedm.12000](https://doi.org/10.1111/jedm.12000).

Abstract opened. Supports: validate the interpretation and the use; more ambitious claims need more evidence; a rejected use does not erase a narrower true claim. This is why a green file supports "the file behaved" and does not support "the person can defend the architecture."

**Sadler, D. R. (1989).** Formative assessment and the design of instructional systems. *Instructional Science, 18*(2), 119–144. [https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00117714](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00117714). PDF consulted: [https://www.ualberta.ca/centre-for-teaching-and-learning/media-library/symposium/less-teaching-more-learning-2009/royce-sadler/articles/symposiumltmlroyce-sadlerbformative-assessment-and-the-design-of-instructional-systems.pdf](https://www.ualberta.ca/centre-for-teaching-and-learning/media-library/symposium/less-teaching-more-learning-2009/royce-sadler/articles/symposiumltmlroyce-sadlerbformative-assessment-and-the-design-of-instructional-systems.pdf).

Opened. For multi-criteria qualitative work, the learner needs a concept of the standard, the skill to compare, and a move that closes the gap. Keyword coverage is the wrong instrument for a design, a DPIA, or a readout. Exemplars are the instrument.

**Shute, V. J. (2008).** Focus on formative feedback. *Review of Educational Research, 78*(1), 153–189. [https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654307313795](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654307313795).

Opened through the guideline table. Supports feedback that is about the task, specific, elaborated, and small enough to use. Supports one-unit hint rungs and a not-yet message that names the unmet specification. Does not support dumping the gold solution as the first response.

**Akimov, A., & Malin, M. (2020).** When old becomes new: A case study of oral examination as an online assessment tool. *Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education, 45*(8), 1095–1107. [https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2020.1730301](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2020.1730301).

Opened. Live orals online. Validity is the stronger claim; reliability is the weaker one (question, occasion, and examiner can change the result). Follow-up questions are what distinguish a surface answer. Recordings were moderated on a sample of high, middle, and low marks. This is why the module gate uses a second, scripted recording and a delayed human score, and why a monologue is not treated as a viva.

**Asynchronous oral assessments.** [https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10677-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10677-0).

Abstract-level only, from the publisher record retrieved in this search. Early evidence that recorded orals change preparation and are not interchangeable with multiple-choice scores. Not used as a validity proof, and not used to justify automatic scoring.

## Shared artifacts

**Star, S. L., & Griesemer, J. R. (1989).** Institutional ecology, 'translations' and boundary objects. *Social Studies of Science, 19*(3), 387–420. [https://doi.org/10.1177/030631289019003001](https://doi.org/10.1177/030631289019003001).

Paper opened. Supports the design claim that one case record can be stable across a manager and an engineer while each group uses it differently. It is an analytic concept from museum work, not an experiment on adult technical training. It justifies a shared decision record. It does not justify a shared implementation quota.
