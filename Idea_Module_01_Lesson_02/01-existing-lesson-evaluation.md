# 01 — Evaluation of the lesson as it stands

Release `rel-m01-l02`, student title "The approach," facilitator title "Applied ML and LLM foundations." Purpose, from the curriculum understanding map: match each problem id to an approach family, including the problems that need no model. Concepts named there: baseline, row split versus person split, classification, extraction, retrieval, agents, forecast, `no-model`, `blocked`, fine-tuning versus retrieval, cost keys. Activities: read nine problems and the extracts, run four baseline scripts, write `approach.md` and a draft `brief.md`. Checks: `chk-approach`, `chk-brief`. Knowledge file is trainer-read. Traps that must survive any edit: a language model for `p-clock` and `p-slots`; ranking `p-triage` first; a row split that looks stronger because the same person sits on both sides.

This evaluation judges the release against that contract and against the syllabus sentence in `course_design/module-01.md`: compare prompting, RAG, fine-tuning, agents, and traditional ML, and select an architecture. The release teaches the vocabulary of that sentence. It does not make the learner select under cost, under a split they have to interpret, or under a 2026 default that postdates the menu.

## What the day actually is

The learner is one forward deployed engineer holding five roles. Lesson 1 has already fixed claim versus fact, the three zones, and the assistant-use rule. Full history is not granted. Today's file drop is leaders' numbers: a wait chart, a vendor note, an access-cost note, a sponsor ask, a price sheet, and four small samples. The assistant is not built. Intake, in the next release, will only build paths the memo did not block. `p-triage` stays blocked into the readout.

The four scripts are one-feature baselines, copied into `work/baselines` and not rewritten.

| Script | Sample | What it prints | What the memo is told to keep |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `dna.py` | `ds-dna.csv` | `row` and `person` accuracy of `prior_dna >= 1` | `person` |
| `messages.py` | `ds-messages.csv` | share of texts containing "status" | `keyword` |
| `letters.py` | `ds-letters.md` | count of bodies containing a six-word specialty list | `filled` |
| `demand.py` | `ds-demand.csv` | mean absolute error of 2026 versus the same month in 2025, and versus the mean of earlier years | `seasonal`, the smaller error |

On the practice profile the solution copy records `row 0.7692`, `person 0.0000`, `keyword 0.3125`, `filled 5`, `seasonal 2.1667`, `mean 12.0324`. Those six lines are the only measurements the day produces. Five of the nine problems have no sample. Their "measured" cell is the word `claim` plus a figure from an extract: `510000`, `230000`, `14`, `430`, `3000000`.

Family gold (`alwaha/samples/gold.py`) is a required set and a forbidden set. `p-clock` and `p-slots` require `no-model` and forbid every model family. `p-triage` requires `blocked` and forbids every other allowed family. `p-bounce` requires both `extract` and `retrieve`. The checker test `test_all_model_memo_hides_the_family` asserts that an all-agent memo fails on `p-clock` and does not fail on `p-status`, and that the failure text does not contain `no-model`, `classical-ml`, `blocked`, or `retrieve`. That is good assessment design. The student chapter undoes it.

## What is worth keeping

**The no-model rows are the intellectual center.** `p-clock` is a wrong date column on the wait-time mart. `p-slots` is a workflow on the session book, and a sentence in a letter must not hold a slot. `p-waitlist` is matching, with an explicit harm if twins or a mother and newborn are merged. Design doc `02-real-problems.md` says the clock is cheap and changes the value of every other problem because it fixes the baseline. That dependency is the right product lesson. A day that only ranked model families would have taught the sponsor's sentence.

**The blocked row is a safety lesson, not a leftover.** The sponsor ask states the program as "the virtual front desk, including triage" and claims 3,000,000 SAR a year. `pol-stage-gate` says a clinician assigns urgency. The medical director card says the same. Gold forbids every buildable family on that id. Later lessons still fail a design that promises triage. The trap is aligned across the map, the design doc, the gold, and the module 5 readout.

**Claim versus fact is practiced on real extracts.** `booking_date` is a fact because the dashboard names it. The median of 14 days stays a claim. The vendor's 70 percent is 640 chats on a national pilot that the note itself says is not Al-Waha. Banned sentences include "Waiting time fell last year," "Al-Waha containment is about 70 percent," and "The assistant may set clinical urgency." The brief may record those wishes inside a claim row and must not promote them into a heading or a rank line. That is the Lesson 1 rule applied to numbers, and it is the right level for this day.

**The baselines are the right shape for a first pipeline.** Zinkevich's Rule 4 is "keep the first model simple and get the infrastructure right." These scripts are that rule, written as courseware. They use the standard library only. They print a line a heavier method has to beat. `dna.py` leaves `appointment_status_final` unread and documents why: the column is filled after the visit and repeats the label. `letters.py` misses "retina" and "sugar clinic" on purpose. `messages.py` misses "Where is my referral?" on purpose. `demand.py` prints both a seasonal naive error and a mean error so the learner can see which is smaller. The comments tell the reader what the function does without requiring a statistics course. Proven pedagogy.

**The leakage demo is visible in code.** `split_mask` keeps every fifth row, or the last 30 percent of sorted `person_id` values. `shared` counts person ids on both sides of the row split and is deliberately not printed. The facilitator slide says: show the split, do not announce the leak. The understanding map names the trap in one sentence. Davis, Matheny, Balu, and Sendak, writing in JAMIA, call the same failure aggregation-based patient-level leakage: the same patient in train and validation, the model associating the label with the person. Roberts and colleagues, in the 2021 Nature Machine Intelligence review of COVID imaging models, tell authors to state that images from the same patient did not cross partitions. The course's row-versus-person pair is a legitimate operational illustration, not a Kaggle tip bolted on.

**Assistant-use still binds.** The letter file, the message file, and the DNA file stay out of prompts. A watermark in `work/` fails both checks, and the failure does not echo the watermark. The solution folder does not contain the letter file. That constraint is doing real work.

**Cost keys exist.** `prices.csv` carries slot missed (598 SAR), return cycle (244), coordinator hour (55), call minute (3), SMS (1), WhatsApp template (1), label (3), rented GPU hour (42), bought GPU (23,520), `price.ext_large_per_1k` (20), `price.local_per_1k` (3), and a teaching harm weight per week waiting (150). The solution's optional section counts 84 words in the letter file and prices them at 1.68 SAR and 0.252 SAR. The keys are the right objects. The exercise is not yet a decision.

## Where the memo becomes static

Chapter 07 is titled as the learner's memo. It then says to replace each empty row with a matching line from a block, and it prints the block. The block contains the family, the practice-file measurement, the citation, the system of record, and a reason sentence for all nine ids. It prints the six claim rows, the nine size rows, and nine numbered rank lines beginning with `p-clock` and ending with `p-triage`. It also explains the mis-park in advance: `510000` is the director's money for returned referrals, `p-bounce` already uses `filled 5`, so the figure is parked on `p-waitlist` and marked `claim`. A learner who is confused is told the confusion is the answer.

Chapter 04, which is supposed to be a glossary, already states that the person line on the practice file is `person 0.0000`, names `p16` through `p23`, and explains that every held-out row disagrees with the rule while `p00` through `p15` agree. Chapter 06 repeats the same numbers before the command is run. The facilitator instruction "do not announce the leak" is overruled by the morning reading. The split is still worth running, because the two commands produce different lines and `shared` is still unprinted. The interpretation has been handed over.

The knowledge questions are similarly pre-answered. Chapter 08 says question 1 is `p-info` and question 2 is `p-clock` (or `p-slots` if the learner prefers). The checker does not read `knowledge.md`. The trainer guide grades it. A self-paced learner who follows the chapter produces the trainer's expected ids without choosing them.

This is the sense in which the memo is multiple choice. The allowed tokens are the seven family words. The correct token for each id is printed. Joining two words is permitted only where the chapter says so (`extract+retrieve`). A second family word on `p-slots`, `p-clock`, or `p-triage` fails, and the chapter says so. Prompting versus retrieval versus fine-tuning is a glossary paragraph plus a two-line knowledge file, not a comparison with a baseline and a price. The syllabus verb "select" is performed by the author of chapter 07.

The solution copy is honest about the consequence: another ranking order can pass, and a reason can use different words when family, measurement, and citation stay the same. Wording freedom is not architectural freedom.

## Where the traps are softer than the map

**Triage first.** `test_triage_first_needs_the_urgency_rule` expects a failure when `p-triage` leads with a plain note, and expects a pass when the same ranking adds "a clinician assigns urgency" on line 1. Chapter 07 says any order passes, and that moving triage to line 1 is fixed by that phrase. The map's trap is "ranking `p-triage` first." The check implements "ranking `p-triage` first without a disclaimer." A roadmap that leads with the blocked program, disclaimer attached, is the sponsor pattern the design doc describes: the largest gross saving, the blocker unaddressed. `chk-brief` is documented as failing a blocked problem ranked first with the block unaddressed. The implemented check treats a clause on the same line as addressing the block. That is the weakest safety test on the day.

**Row split.** The check rejects the row-line number in the DNA measured cell and does not print that number. Good. The teaching split itself is a constructed toy. Holding out the last 30 percent of sorted ids yields accuracy 0 because those rows were built to miss `prior_dna`. A learner can leave thinking that person-level splits "cause" a score of zero, or that accuracy is the clinical metric. Wilimitis and Walsh, in a 2023 JMIR AI tutorial, distinguish subject-wise from record-wise cross-validation and report a case where the two estimates barely differed, which they attribute to few repeats and low within-person correlation. They still tie the split to the unit of the decision: a prognosis over time wants the person held out; a single-encounter diagnosis may not. Davis and colleagues add timing-based leakage, which `appointment_status_final` illustrates, and warn that some apparent leaks are clinically appropriate when the feature would exist at decision time. The course shows one vivid pair. It does not teach the cadence question: what is known when the reminder is sent, as against what is known after the clinic closes. The design doc's preferred DNA split is a person-grouped time split. The script implements a person holdout on sorted ids, not time. Proven failure mode, Experimental stand-in for the split the design doc named.

**Fine-tuning versus retrieval.** The glossary says fine-tuning changes weights and retrieval leaves them alone, and that retrieval wins when the document changes by site or version. The knowledge answer is `p-info`. Nothing is measured: no superseded circular is retrieved, no fine-tune is attempted, no token price is compared with `cost.label` or a coordinator hour. The advanced layer in the module trace asks for a cost column of `ext-large` versus `local-small` and for near-duplicate referrals by embedding. Both are optional sections the check ignores (`## Cost`, `## Overlap`). The overlap task (name `letter-01` and `letter-06`, do not copy the watermark line) is a good instinct for duplicate letters. It is not retrieval evaluation.

**The parked 510,000.** Returned referrals are 510,000 SAR on the director's ranked note, second after no-shows at 910,000 and ahead of empty sessions at 230,000. The director's opening claim is that no-shows dominate. The design doc says returns and duplicate waitlist rows cost more once identity is counted. The lesson never measures that. It stores the return SAR on the waitlist row so that `p-bounce` can keep `filled 5`. The brief then looks quantified. The sizes are incommensurable: a keyword rate, a letter count, a forecast error in visits, a median in days, and several SAR claims. Ranking them as if the size column were one scale is the sort of dashboard error `p-clock` exists to stop. The example ranking in chapter 07 happens to put the clock first, for the right reason, and then says any other order is also a pass. The dependency diagram in the design doc (identity before waitlist, DNA, and status; criteria before bounce; clock before honest value; reliable reminders before DNA) is not graded.

**Tools section.** Four sentences are requested: the chart is a query, empty chairs are a workflow, status is a tool call, urgency is not a model call. The check does not read them. They are the application engineer's only product, and they are the sentences that would survive a reasoning-model pitch. Leaving them unscored teaches that the boundary is decorative.

## What 2025–2026 makes stale, without throwing out the floor

The family floor is not stale. A wrong date column is still SQL. A slot write is still a transaction. Urgency is still not a generation target. A changing document is still retrieval. A monthly series still needs a later window. Status still lives in a record.

What has moved is the default a learner brings into the room. File 02 has the sources. The evaluation consequence is short. Hosted fine-tuning is no longer the obvious next step: OpenAI's model-optimization page, fetched 30 October 2026, says the platform is winding down for new users, pushes reasoning controls, and still lists "medical diagnoses" as a reinforcement example. That example is a vendor claim and a trap if copied onto `p-triage`. Reasoning-model demos make one agent look universal. Anthropic's agent note says many jobs are a workflow or a single call. Chapter 07 assigns `p-status`, `p-slots`, and `p-clock` the right tokens and never asks the learner to reject the demo or to price it. Small on-prem models revive fine-tuning for a narrow schema inside the zone, which Module 3 already plans as a LoRA on bounce letters. Lesson 2 mentions weights once and never prices `cost.label` or the six-word list that misses everyday clinic names. Forecasting is the sharpest stale default. Tan and colleagues (arXiv 2406.16964) found that removing the language model from three popular time-series methods did not hurt accuracy and cut compute sharply. Their scope is not outpatient sessions. The course script already keeps `seasonal` and then grades only the family word `forecast`.

## What must not be "fixed" by helpful editing

The understanding map says traps are copied so a later editor does not remove them. Do not delete `no-model` for the clock and the slots. Do not make `p-triage` a supervised urgency model with a human "in the loop" after the grade is written. Do not replace the person line with the row line because 0.7692 looks like a success. Do not let the vendor percent become Al-Waha's containment. Do not put the letter file into `work/`. Do not announce, in a new chapter, a hidden gold family the checker currently refuses to echo. The repair is to remove the answer key, keep the checker strict, and add the costed comparison the advanced layer already named.

## Gaps that are design debt, not learner error

The person split is not the person-grouped time split the design doc promised. The waitlist size is an accounting patch. Ranking has no dependency edge. Tools and knowledge are unscored. Cost is optional. Near-duplicate embeddings are named in the trace and implemented only as "write two headings." The five roles are a table in chapter 00 and a single author in chapter 07. A cohort that rotates roles still submits one transcribed memo. Self-paced learners, who are the audience of the student chapters, are given the key because otherwise the check is a wall. That tradeoff is real. It is resolved in file 04 by a worked toy that is not one of the nine, not by leaving the nine in the chapter.
