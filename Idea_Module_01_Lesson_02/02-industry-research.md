# 02 — Industry research for approach selection

Research date 30 October 2026. The client in the course is fictional. External sources are used as analogues and as method evidence. They are not findings about Al-Waha, and they are not permission to build `p-triage` or to overbook a district. Vendor pages are treated as vendor pages. Preprints are labeled Experimental. No benchmark number below was invented for this pack. If a figure comes from an abstract returned by search and the full PDF was not re-scored, the sentence says so.

## 1. Decision frameworks: rules, classical ML, prompts, RAG, fine-tune, agents

**Proven.** Martin Zinkevich, *Rules of Machine Learning* (`https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/guides/rules-of-ml`): Rule 1 says to launch without machine learning when a heuristic will do, because borrowed data usually loses to a simple rule. Rule 4 keeps the first model simple so the pipeline and the definition of a good outcome are trustworthy. Rule 33 trains through a date and tests on later days. The four scripts are Rule 4. `p-clock` and `p-slots` are Rule 1. `appointment_status_final` breaks Rule 33: it exists only after the outcome.

**Proven as practitioner judgment, not as a controlled trial.** Hamel Husain, "Is Fine-Tuning Still Valuable?" (`https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/fine_tuning_valuable.html`): fine-tuning still fits a narrow task, a prompt that has become a pile of conditionals, a latency or size constraint, or data that must stay inside a boundary. Fine-tuning learns syntax, style, and rules. Retrieval supplies facts that change. Prompting comes first because it stress-tests the evaluation; if the measured prompt meets the bar, stop. Declaring fine-tuning dead is common on general coding tools, which is a different product from a letter schema. His eval FAQ says retrieval is not dead, because the model still needs the right context (`https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals-faq/is-rag-dead.html`).

**Proven as vendor pedagogy, skeptical as evidence.** OpenAI's accuracy guide (`https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/optimizing-llm-accuracy`) rejects a single ladder of prompt, then RAG, then fine-tune. It splits failures into in-context memory (RAG puts the textbook in the prompt) and learned memory (fine-tuning internalizes a behavior). It says many of the vendor's largest deployments used prompting and RAG only. That is a vendor claim, not an audit. For the lesson: `p-info` and the clause half of `p-bounce` are in-context; a stable specialty schema could later be learned memory; `p-clock` is neither, because the defect is a column.

The same vendor's model-optimization page, fetched 30 October 2026 (`https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/model-optimization`), says the hosted fine-tuning platform is no longer open to new users, that existing users may create jobs for a limited period, and that new prompting should start from a frontier model using reasoning-effort controls. The page still documents supervised fine-tuning, direct preference optimization, and reinforcement fine-tuning, and it lists "medical diagnoses based on history and diagnostic guidelines" as a reinforcement example. **Emerging** as a market fact: hosted fine-tuning is being withdrawn while reasoning controls are being pushed forward. **Experimental** as advice to a health cluster: copying that medical example onto `p-triage` collides with the cluster rule and with the device guidance in section 5. The page can change. Teach the date of the fetch, not a permanent platform map.

**Proven as engineering guidance from a model vendor.** Anthropic, "Building Effective AI Agents" (`https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents`): start from the simplest design, including no agent. Workflows suit a path you can specify. Agents suit open-ended tasks, at higher cost, and they compound errors. For many applications one call plus retrieval is enough. Simon Willison's 20 December 2024 note quotes the same warning. Status is one read of the referral record. Empty chairs are a workflow with a write lock. An autonomous loop that "handles access" is the sponsor sentence, and it is the wrong grain.

**Emerging, narrow.** The Alan Turing Institute's essay on small language models (`https://www.turing.ac.uk/blog/why-we-still-need-small-language-models-even-age-frontier-ai`) argues that frontier benchmark gains do not remove small models where consistency, speed, privacy, or unit cost dominate. That matches the course zones: identified data stays off `ext-large`, and `price.local_per_1k` is 3 SAR per 1,000 tokens against 20. It does not say a small model should forecast sessions or grade urgency. An on-prem break-even preprint (arXiv 2509.18101) talks about very high volume or a residency constraint. Do not drop its token threshold into the cluster's business case. Residency can justify `local-small` when the token math does not. A test-time-scaling preprint (arXiv 2509.19645) says reasoning traces should be judged on latency and cost. No stable multiplier for thinking tokens was verified, so the lesson leaves that line unpriced.

The stack to teach is a failure diagnosis. A wrong definition is a query. A slot write is a workflow. Status is one tool call. A changing document is filtered retrieval. A stable letter schema may later be a small local fine-tune, only after it beats the keyword list and still cites a clause. A seasonal series stays with seasonal naive until a statistical or tree model wins on a later window. A future miss, if modeled, is classical ML on a person-and-time split, and the action is a reminder. Urgency is routed to a human. File 03 turns that list into architectures. It is not a published bake-off.

## 2. Leakage and person-level splits in operations

**Proven as a taxonomy.** Davis, Matheny, Balu, and Sendak, JAMIA (`https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10746313/`): leakage is outcome information in the inputs. They separate aggregation leakage (the same patient on both sides of a split), timing leakage (a feature that does not exist yet), and association leakage (a feature entangled with the label through care). Some future-looking features are legitimate if they are plans known at decision time. `appointment_status_final` is the clear timing case. `prior_dna` is legitimate only if it was known when the reminder was sent. The script hides the final status and still has no as-of timestamp.

**Proven as reporting practice.** Roberts and colleagues, Nature Machine Intelligence, 2021 (`https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-021-00307-0`): of 2,212 COVID imaging papers and preprints in their window, 62 survived quality screening, and none were judged of potential clinical use, for methodological flaws and bias. One recommendation is explicit: authors should state how they kept images from the same patient out of more than one partition. The paper is about chest radiographs and CT, not appointment attendance. The transfer that is fair: a row split on repeated measures from one person is a known way to publish a number that will not survive the next person. The transfer that is not fair: DNA prediction is not diagnostic imaging, and this citation is not a clinical ban on classical attendance models.

**Proven as a tutorial, with a result that complicates the demo.** Wilimitis and Walsh, JMIR AI, 18 December 2023 (`https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11041453/`): subject-wise splits keep a person's events on one side; record-wise splits can put the same person in train and test. In their case study the estimates barely differed, which they suspect came from few repeats and weak within-person correlation. They still tie the split to the unit of the decision. The practice file's drop from `row 0.7692` to `person 0.0000` is constructed. The command should reveal the arithmetic. The debrief should say a real gap can be small and the split can still be mandatory.

**Proven as a reporting checklist, not as a methods manual.** TRIPOD+AI, BMJ, 16 April 2024 (`https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-078378`), asks reports to say how data were partitioned, how performance was measured, and what is known about inequalities. It does not prescribe patient-level splits, and it is not a quality score (that role sits with PROBAST). The lesson's measured cells are the same habit: name the split and the file, and do not let a leader's sentence wear the word "measured."

**Operational reading for DNA.** Decision time is when the reminder is sent, not when the clinic marks the outcome. A person-grouped time split scores later visits for people absent from the fit window, using only features that existed then. The current script holds out the highest sorted ids. Both reject the row split. Only the first is the design doc's split. File 03 specifies the data change.

## 3. Forecasting clinic demand

**Proven textbook baseline.** Hyndman and Athanasopoulos, *Forecasting: Principles and Practice* (`https://otexts.robjhyndman.com/fpp2/toolbox.html`): the seasonal naive forecast sets each future season equal to the last observed value of that season. `demand.py` is that method. On the practice file the seasonal error is 2.1667 visits and the mean error is 12.0324. The memo keeps the smaller one. ETS is the standard statistical next family in the same book. It is Proven as a class. It is not a fitted Al-Waha result.

**Emerging as a holiday-aware candidate, unproven as a winner.** Taylor and Letham's Prophet model, as presented beside ARIMA and ETS in *Forecasting: Principles and Practice* (`https://otexts.com/fpptr/prophet.html`), is a regression with a piecewise trend, Fourier seasonality, and holiday indicators, aimed at series with several seasons of history. Ramadan, Eid, and a campaign are the story a vendor will tell. This pack extracted no win-rate from that chapter and claims none. A learner may name Prophet-class only if it beats `seasonal` on a later window. Fitting it on the scored months is the design doc's forecast trap.

**Proven on retail hierarchies, not on clinics.** The M5 accuracy competition (Makridakis, Spiliotis, and Assimakopoulos, International Journal of Forecasting, 2022, `https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijforecast.2021.11.013`) forecast 42,840 hierarchical Walmart series. LightGBM was the workhorse of the winning entries the organizers could inspect. The first-place method averaged several LightGBM models (In and Jung, same issue). That supports gradient boosting as a classical candidate when the unit is a count, the drivers are calendar and related series, and the score is a future window. It does not show that boosting beats ETS in a clinic.

**Proven on one medical center, old, and specific.** DeLurgio and colleagues, 2009 (`https://btdenton.engin.umich.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/138/2015/08/DeLurgio-2009.pdf`): weekly outpatient-visit forecasts for 23 clinics, 12-week horizon. A combination of univariate and cross-clinic regression had the lowest RMSE in 43.5 percent of clinics, against 30.4 percent multivariate and 26.1 percent univariate. Symmetric median absolute percent error medians were about 6.8, 7.1, and 7.9 percent. They name season, holidays, and referral flow. Use that to justify a seasonal baseline plus a cross-clinic signal. Do not quote 6.8 percent as Al-Waha's target.

**Proven as a negative result on LLM forecasters, limited scope.** Tan, Merrill, Gupta, Althoff, and Hartvigsen (arXiv 2406.16964): across 13 datasets, ablations beat Time-LLM in 26 of 26 dataset-metric cells, CALF in 22 of 26, and OneFitsAll in 19 of 26. Time-LLM was 6,642 million parameters and 3,003 minutes on Weather, against an ablation near 0.245 million parameters and 2.17 minutes. Inference averaged about 28.2, 2.3, and 1.2 times the ablations. Shuffling the series, and a 10 percent few-shot, did not show that text sequence skill had transferred. Later foundation models, and a model that only adjusts a statistical forecast after reading a campaign note, sit outside that ablation. No outpatient paper retrieved here shows a language model beating seasonal naive on a later window. Until one does, LLM-as-forecaster is Experimental, and the graded act is to refuse it when it cannot beat `seasonal`.

## 4. DNA prediction: methods at a high level, and the ethical stop

Did-not-attend prediction estimates whether a booked patient will be absent. It is not a diagnosis. The course's causes are fictional truth. The literature, at the altitude this lesson needs, argues about the action more than about another algorithm.

**Secondary summary, not re-audited.** The abstract of a 2025 review (DOI `10.1016/j.ibmed.2025.100229`), as returned in search and not re-scored from the publisher PDF, covers 52 papers and says logistic regression was the most common model (68 percent), with later trees, ensembles, and deep learning, best reported AUCs between 0.75 and 0.95, and accuracy from 52 percent to 99.44 percent. That accuracy span is a warning, not a target. A 2024 conference review (DOI `10.1109/ictmod63116.2024.10878195`) prefers regression, trees, and ensembles among 51 studies. Neither abstract is permission to ship a model. Both support `classical-ml` over an LLM that reads the row.

**Proven on interventions, which is the part the memo skips.** Oikonomidi and colleagues, JAMIA, 2023 (`https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9933067/`): eight studies of actions, not of algorithms alone. Targeted text reminders: one trial, high certainty, median risk ratio 0.91 (interquartile range 0.90 to 0.92). Targeted phone reminders: three trials, moderate certainty, median risk ratio 0.61 (0.49 to 0.68). A navigator trial: risk ratio 0.55 (95 percent confidence interval 0.46 to 0.67). Overbooking: very low certainty, three studies, 31,766 appointments, outcomes not poolable. Equity and cost were thinly reported. The lesson's baseline is therefore also "remind everyone," priced at `cost.sms` and `cost.call_minute`, before a model earns its keep.

**Proven as a fairness failure of the objective, on someone else's data.** Samorani and colleagues (`https://doi.org/10.1287/msom.2021.0999`) show that placing the highest predicted no-show risk into overbooked slots makes the higher-risk group wait longer. In their specialty-clinic data, a standard objective left Black patients waiting about 30 percent longer. Do not carry 30 percent into the Kingdom or into `d-rim`. Do carry the mechanism: minimizing empty chairs by overbooking predicted misses dumps waiting onto whatever group the features pick out. The course already names that harm for `d-rim` and residents. The lesson stops at that sentence. It does not teach a race-aware objective, a feature list, or an overbook program.

**Ethical limit.** A DNA score may target a reminder or a human call. It may not silently overbook, use the final attendance column, or be validated on a row split. District and insurance are not innocent features once the action is a worse slot. This pack is not a legal opinion on device status. The teaching line is the action boundary the design doc already drew.

## 5. Triage as a blocked, safety-critical decision

The in-world rule remains `pol-stage-gate`: a clinician assigns every clinical urgency; the assistant routes emergency wording to a human; it does not write a grade. External texts explain why that rule is the ordinary shape of a boundary, not a local quirk.

**Proven US device policy, not Saudi law.** FDA's Clinical Decision Support Software guidance, Federal Register 28 September 2022 (`https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/clinical-decision-support-software`), says section 520(o)(1)(E) excludes CDS from the device definition only when all four criteria hold: no image or signal acquisition, medical information as the input, recommendations to a health professional rather than a specific directive, and enough of the basis that the professional does not rely primarily on the software. A single diagnostic output, or software meant to replace the clinician's judgment, fails the third criterion. The non-device path is for a professional, not a patient-facing bot that writes an urgency grade. A later human visit does not repair a grade the software has already stored.

**Proven Saudi device guidance, narrower than "any AI in a hospital."** SFDA MDS-G010, version 1.0, 29 November 2022 (`https://www.sfda.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2023-01/MDS-G010ML.pdf`), covers marketing authorization for AI and ML that diagnose, manage, or predict diseases by analyzing medical data. Intended use decides device status. Investigation, detection, diagnosis, monitoring, treatment, or management of a condition makes the product a device under that guidance. **Interpretation, not a ruling:** fixing `booking_date`, retrieving hours, and booking a slot are administrative. Writing a clinical urgency grade matches "manage or predict." An attendance model sits in between and depends on intended use and action. Learners follow `pol-stage-gate`, which is stricter than a guessed exemption and is the rule the checker knows.

**Proven as ethics principles.** WHO, 28 June 2021 (`https://www.who.int/news/item/28-06-2021-who-issues-first-global-report-on-ai-in-health-and-six-guiding-principles-for-its-design-and-use`; report ISBN 9789240029200): humans remain in control of medical decisions. A system that transfers the decision to the machine undermines autonomy. That sentence sits beside the 3,000,000 SAR claim. WHO is not the SFDA and not the medical director. It is why a disclaimer on rank line 1 is not a control. No red-flag phrase list is specified here. The course already routes emergency wording to a human.

## 6. Build versus buy before any access model

A forward deployed engineer who opens a notebook before asking who already owns the appointment will build a second system of record. The race condition in `p-slots` is that bug.

**Proven for the national appointment layer in the Kingdom.** The Ministry described Mawid on 10 September 2020 as the Central Appointment System for booking, canceling, and rescheduling at primary care and for referral appointments at hospitals, with reminders (`https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/ministry/mediacenter/news/pages/news-2020-09-10-010.aspx`). On 26 January 2022 it merged Sehha, Tetamman, and Mawid into Sehhaty (`https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/ministry/mediacenter/news/pages/news-2022-01-26-006.aspx`). The Sehhaty booking page still describes reserve, modify, and cancel, with Absher, no fee, and 937 as the helpline (`https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/eservices/sehhaty/pages/appointment-booking-on-sehhaty.aspx`). A 6 February 2022 Makkah cluster note links a local e-health rollout to Mawid, Wasfaty, and other national systems (`https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/ministry/mediacenter/news/pages/news-2022-02-06-004.aspx`). **Uncertainty:** that note is one cluster's phase. It does not say which hospital system any cluster runs, and Al-Waha is invented. The engineer still asks whether the slot is mastered in the national service, the hospital scheduler, or both, and which writer wins a conflict.

**Proven that major EHR products already are the scheduler.** Epic Cadence is documented by operational training sites as the module that schedules, cancels, reschedules, holds the wait list, and tracks check-in (`https://epicsupport.sites.uiowa.edu/epic-resources/scheduling`). The US Department of Veterans Affairs technology catalog describes Cadence as enterprise scheduling used by staff, affiliated providers, or patients from the portal (`https://www.oit.va.gov/Services/TRM/ToolPage.aspx?tid=10776`). Epic's open specification for incoming scheduling (`https://open.epic.com/Scheduling/HL7v2`) receives new, rescheduled, updated, and canceled appointments, and a notification that the patient did not show. DNA is already an event the scheduling interface knows. Oracle Health's patient-administration guide (`https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/health/health-patient-administration/health-patient-admin-userguide/schedule-appointment1.html`) shows scheduling creating or matching an encounter, rejecting a slot that collides with another appointment, and warning when a selection would overbook. The write path is the product. An LLM "judgment" beside it is a second writer.

**What the engineer asks first.** Who masters the slot, the status, the wait clock, and the in-force circular. Whether two systems accept writes. Whether did-not-show is already a message. Whether the vendor rate is this cluster's calls. The course note fails that last test: 70 percent of 640 chats, national pilot, not Al-Waha. Configure first. Model only where the system of record does not already define the decision. No head-to-head accuracy for those products was retrieved. **Emerging:** front-door products will keep bundling triage with containment. The answer is the questionnaire, not a classroom bake-off.
