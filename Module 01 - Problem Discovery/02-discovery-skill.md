# The discovery skill

A forward deployed engineer is hired when a customer says "build us a platform." That sentence is a request. The job is to find the problem underneath it.

In a health service the cost of skipping that step is concrete. A model can look strong on a slide and still fill a specialist clinic with the wrong people, miss people who never appear in the list, or sit for a year waiting on a security review. Discovery is how you find that out while the change is still small.

Hartland is the practice case. The moves stay with you after Hartland is gone.

1. Write the ask down in their words. Leave your improvements out of it.
2. Name the sponsor's goal and the operator's pain as two different things.
3. Walk the workflow as it is. Include the workaround and the queue where work waits.
4. Find the scarce person and the costly delay.
5. Separate data that exists from data people wish existed.
6. Name the decision a human still has to own.
7. Choose one outcome an operator would recognize, and a metric for it.
8. Describe the smallest slice that can move that metric.
9. State what you are leaving out of the first release, and why.
10. List what you still do not know.

The brief you will write has ten parts: the ask, the client, who feels the pain, the current workflow, the job to be done, the outcome and metrics, the constraints, the first slice, the later work, and the open questions.

By the end of the module you can take a vague executive request and turn it into a problem a team can build against.

- Restate the ask without improving it.
- Separate the sponsor's goal from the operator's pain.
- Reconstruct the workflow from interviews, extracts, and written rules, including where they disagree.
- Name one outcome, one first slice, and the work you are leaving for later.
- Say what is still unknown, and proceed anyway.
