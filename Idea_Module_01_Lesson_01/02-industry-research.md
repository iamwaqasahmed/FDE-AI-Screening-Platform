# Industry research for an arrival lesson

Sources below were opened for this review unless a paragraph says otherwise. **Proven** means a fetched primary source states it. **Emerging** means the source is real and the setting is narrow, or a follow-up weakened the number. **Experimental** means a course-design inference. Vendor impact percentages inside documents that call their own figures notional are not repeated.

On 30 October 2026 a direct fetch of the Palantir Medium posts returned HTTP 403, and `guides.18f.gov/product/discover/technical/` returned HTTP 500. Those URLs are not quoted as full pages. Fetched: the Palantir architecture page and 2022 partnership PDF, the USDS sprint pages, the GDS service manual, the UK coding-assistant trial, the NIST PDFs named below, the SDAIA and NCA and DGA PDFs named below, the HHS privacy summary, the WHO ICD page, the ISO 27799 catalogue page, and the Synthea README (used in file 03).

## Palantir: pre-pilot, then a data foundation

**Proven** as Palantir's account of its own method. It is not an independent study of the first five days.

The [Architecture Center overview](https://palantir.com/docs/foundry/architecture-center/overview/) says the platforms are developed by forward deployed engineering: engineers get close to a problem and work with core engineering so field feedback becomes product. It names AIP, Foundry, and Apollo, and puts an Ontology at the center. The Ontology joins data, logic, action, and security policy into objects and actions that people and agents both use. Security sits on those objects. Data services include connectivity, transformation, and health of the data. The first representational act in the supply-chain example is the operational noun, not a model choice. Counts on that page ("50+ verticals," "300+ microservices," release volume) are Palantir's marketing. They are not treated as measured adoption.

The 2022 PDF *Getting Started with Palantir*, fetched from an Azure Marketplace artifact, says engagements usually start as a joint pilot. Pre-pilot work identifies the business problem and does setup. The diagram marks that stretch as one to two weeks. The tasks printed are: name a business project lead, identify a key business problem, scope the pilot, and finish business and technical setup. Client roles printed are one executive sponsor, one business project lead, one technical lead, and four to eight business users. The pilot is one month in three phases: a data foundation (deploy Foundry, ingest, transform, model an ontology), workflow refinement with users, then operationalise, measure, and train. The PDF says its data are notional. See [Getting Started with Palantir (2022)](https://catalogartifact.azureedge.net/publicartifacts/palantirtechnologies1585670280063.palantir_foundry-be480bc7-d758-4a25-a60b-3e29b911ac31/Artifacts/Documents/Whitepaper_GettingStarted.pdf).

**Emerging.** Search extracts of posts that returned 403 match that picture and add no timetable: [Dev versus Delta](https://blog.palantir.com/dev-versus-delta-demystifying-engineering-roles-at-palantir-ad44c2a6e87), [Who wants to be a Delta](https://blog.palantir.com/who-wants-to-be-a-delta-8d2ea948035), [A day in the life](https://blog.palantir.com/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-palantir-forward-deployed-software-engineer-45ef2de257b1), and [How we do agile](https://blog.palantir.com/how-we-do-agile-1-35fa76e48cad). The extracts say the forward deployed engineer configures an existing platform for one customer, scopes with the users, and spends time on site. None is an hour-by-hour of week one.

**Unverified, and unused.** Secondary blogs prescribe a Friday query and a "healthy" access delay. No Palantir page opened here states those timings. Foundry's product named Pilot builds from a prompt on seed data so the model does not see enterprise rows. It is a builder feature, not the 2022 engagement. See [Pilot overview](https://palantir.com/docs/foundry/pilot/overview/). Teaching the builder feature as week one reverses the partnership note. The documents support naming the problem and a business lead before any ontology, and putting access control on the objects. They do not support a health URL as proof the engagement started.

## USDS, 18F, and GDS

**Proven** as published method. "More than 160 successful digital products" is USDS's own sentence on the guide and was not audited.

The [Discovery Sprint Guide](https://sprint.usds.gov/) says a sprint is usually two to four weeks. The team interviews every level and end users, reads available data, looks at production code, and observes processes, to find causes and next steps. The worked scenario is an office about to buy servers because filing is "slow." The finding is duplicate filings and applicants who cannot see status. The report may say the servers will not fix it.

[Conducting the Discovery Sprint](https://sprint.usds.gov/conducting/) is the week-one page. Interviews should already be booked, including with call-center operators. By midweek, observe, write a hypothesis, and name who else to see. By Friday, check findings with stakeholders. The answer is not assumed to be technology. Another catalogue of problems is called a failure. The [interview page](https://sprint.usds.gov/interview/) says the team writes five to ten core questions before anyone is interviewed.

18F's fetched [De-risking Government Technology Guide 2.0](https://guides.18f.gov/assets/derisking-government-tech/dist/18f-derisking-guide.pdf) is about buying and overseeing custom software, including an in-house product owner. A success-rate figure in its introduction depends on a footnote this review did not open, so the figure is omitted. The 18F technical-discovery URL was unreadable and is not a source.

**Proven.** [How the discovery phase works](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/how-the-discovery-phase-works) (page updated 21 June 2021) says the team learns users, constraints, policy intent, and opportunities such as shared data before it commits to build. The page says not to start building the service in discovery. Four to eight weeks is typical, and the purpose sets the length. A handed solution ("build a map") is reframed as a problem ("how do people find the nearest centre?"). Scope includes what is out. The team should learn what the problem costs. A hard constraint that blocks improvement is a reason to stop, and stopping is described as saving money. Continuing means ideas to test, a first idea, a team, and a measure. A content page, a campaign, a partner, or an API can be the better response. [Start by learning user needs](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/start-by-learning-user-needs) includes call-centre data and says non-user opinions are assumptions. [Set up a service team](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/the-team/set-up-a-service-team) makes a developer optional in discovery. Product, user research, and service design are the core. A lesson whose graded center is a Python process is aimed at the optional role.

## AI deployment teams, as far as public pages go

No industry-wide "AI week one" was opened that this review could treat as standard. OpenAI and Anthropic deployment manuals were not opened.

**Proven** as a voluntary framework. NIST AI RMF 1.0 (26 January 2023) uses Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage. Govern runs the whole time. The functions are not a checklist and not a waterfall. Map checks context, including when a system will not work as intended and what happens if use exceeds the intent. After Map, there should be enough context for a go or no-go on designing, developing, or deploying at all. Measure is for before deployment and during operation. See [NIST.AI.100-1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf).

**Emerging.** Teresa Torres argues that generating an opportunity tree from a button skips the alignment the tree is for, and that assumption tests include ethical assumptions. See [generative AI and discovery](https://www.producttalk.org/generative-ai-discovery/) and [opportunity solution trees](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/).

## Saudi institutions, at the level a learner needs

The case keeps fictional gates, a fictional personal-data assessment, and `public` / `internal` / `phi`. The pages below are the real map. They are not Al-Waha's law, and they should not become quiz items on decree numbers.

**Proven** from fetched pages. The national portal dates the Personal Data Protection Law to Royal Decree M/19 (16 September 2021) and the amendment M/148 (27 March 2023). It places SDAIA over data and AI, NDMO under SDAIA for data-management standards, and the National Cybersecurity Authority (from 2017) as the cybersecurity reference, with each entity still responsible for its own compliance. See [Data Regulation and Cybersecurity](https://my.gov.sa/en/content/legal-regulatory-framework).

The English PDPL defines Health Data as personal data about a person's health condition, including physical, mental, or psychological condition, or about health services they received. Sensitive Data includes Health Data. The fetched text bars legitimate-interest processing of sensitive data. See [the English PDPL PDF](https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/about/Documents/PersonalDataProtectionLaw.pdf). SDAIA's controller guide says it is not binding; the law is. The teaching point is narrow: health data is a named category, and a convenience basis is a weak story for it.

Two classification PDFs on the same authority do not share one ladder. The standards document prints "Version 1.5, January 2021" on the cover and "1.5, June 2021" in the revision table. This review does not decide which date is the typo. Its domain DC maps impact to class: high to Top Secret, medium to Secret, low to Confidential, none or insignificant to Public, with a further test that may release some low-impact data as Public. The glossary still says public information is what is not confidential or higher. See [the standards PDF](https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/about/Documents/DataManagementPersonalDataProtectionStandards.pdf). The classification policy, headed Version 1, 5 May 2020, uses Top Secret, Secret, Restricted, and Public. Principles include open-by-default in the development sector, proportionality, classify on receipt, the highest class wins for a mixed set, segregation of duties, need-to-know, and least privilege. Restricted splits into A, B, and C by how wide the impact is. See [Data Classification Policy](https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/about/Documents/DataClassificationPolicy.pdf). **Proven:** do not teach "the four Saudi levels" as a single list. **Experimental** and false: equating the course's three labels with either PDF.

The [data-sharing policy](https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/about/Documents/DataSharingPolicyEN.pdf) says a request with no class is classified by the sharing office, and that secret or top-secret sharing is signed higher than restricted sharing. That is the shape of an access negotiation.

SDAIA's [AI ethics principles](https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/about/Documents/ai-principles.pdf), principle 2, say design should classify data, limit personal data to what the system needs, and plan de-identification. The regulations index still showed a draft mark when fetched. Treat the PDF as published principles, not as a statute.

NCA ECC–2:2024 requires assets to be classified, labeled, and handled, protection based on that class, and cybersecurity requirements inside the project method rather than at the end. Encryption follows class. The Arabic text governs if the English diverges. See the [ECC page](https://nca.gov.sa/en/regulatory-documents/controls-list/ecc/) and the [2024 English PDF](https://cdn.nca.gov.sa/api/files/public/upload/86e09090-44e4-481f-bc28-355673607654_ECC--2024-EN.pdf). The case CISO who says security reviews come at the end is in conflict with that control. Good fiction. Not a statement that ECC binds the case.

DGA's IT-governance controls, version 3.0, June 2026, document DGA-1-2-5-108, say the Authority sets digital-transformation standards and checks compliance, and that entities adopt IT policies covering at least cybersecurity, risk and continuity, and human resources, reviewed at least yearly. See the [version 3.0 PDF](https://dga.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2026-06/Controls%20for%20Information%20Technology%20Governance%20in%20Digital%20Government-V3.0_0.pdf).

A real cluster arrival would touch DGA, SDAIA/NDMO, NCA, and health-sector rules this review did not open. The lesson needs a one-page "real institutions, fictional rules" so `internal` is not remembered as a national class.

## Health-data classification against the three zones

Zones are places. NDMO class is an impact judgment. The US term PHI is an identifiability judgment. ICD is a code system. They answer different questions about the same letter.

**Proven.** HHS says protected health information is individually identifiable health information held or transmitted by a covered entity or business associate, in any form, about condition, care, or payment. De-identified data, by a statistician's determination or by stripping listed identifiers, falls outside. See the [privacy-rule summary](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/ocr/privacy/hipaa/understanding/summary/privacysummary.pdf) and [de-identification guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/de-identification/index.html). HIPAA does not govern the fictional cluster. The course borrowed the word. A watermark is not de-identification. [NIST SP 800-66r2](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-66r2.pdf) ties electronic-PHI safeguards to a risk assessment. Useful as "security follows the data." Useless as the client's statute.

NIST Privacy Framework 1.0 (16 January 2020) runs Identify-P, Govern-P, Control-P, Communicate-P, and Protect-P together. It does not name PHI or a national four-level scheme. See [NIST.CSWP.01162020](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/CSWP/NIST.CSWP.01162020.pdf). A 1.1 draft exists; 1.0 is the text used here.

ISO/IEC 27002:2022 control 5.12, paraphrased from a public mirror and not quoted at length, classifies information for confidentiality, integrity, availability, and other parties' requirements. Names are local. The same fact can be classed differently in two organisations. The ISO text is the authority and is copyrighted. [ISO 27799:2025](https://www.iso.org/standard/84647.html) applies 27002-style controls to health organisations, including records and personal health information on premises or in the cloud, and remote care. **Proven:** class is not "which building." **Experimental:** teaching the control wording in this lesson.

WHO says ICD-11 was adopted in 2019 and took effect on 1 January 2022, for mortality, morbidity, payment, and planning. The page also calls it a foundation for AI in health, which is WHO's claim about its terminology. See [ICD](https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/classification-of-diseases/international-classification-of-diseases). `DM fu` on the sample letter is a shorthand for a diabetes follow-up, not an ICD code. One audit question belongs in arrival. An ICD chapter does not.

The zone sketch can stay as a deployment map. Using it as the classification is **Experimental** and wrong. A field can be `phi` in a hospital data center, `phi` on the private cloud, and forbidden on `ext-large`. The class did not change when the disk did. A public leaflet on a hospital website can still be `public`.

## Gates, trees, and dual-track

**Proven** as Cooper's 2026 description of Stage-Gate. Discovery sits in front of five stages: concept, business case, development, test, launch. Gates are decisions. Stage-Gate International calls them Go/Kill and draws a funnel, not a tunnel. See [Cooper, 3 February 2026](https://community.pdma.org/knowledgehub/blogs/robert-cooper/2026/02/03/stage-gate-the-official-2026-version) and [discovery-to-launch](https://www.stage-gate.com/about/stage-gate-innovation-performance-framework/discovery-to-launch-process/). The course ladder (name, class the risk, accept a data assessment, limited pilot, go live, review) has no kill and no split between work and decision. Obey it inside the case. Do not call it Stage-Gate. **Experimental** as a method for choosing an AI system. The missing piece to teach is the stop.

**Proven** as product practice. Cagan's [dual-track page](https://www.svpg.com/dual-track-agile/) says much validation happens before production code, and that he dropped the term after *Inspired*'s second edition because teams fetishised the process. He now says continuous discovery and continuous delivery. Torres's tree is an outcome, opportunities from research, a few solutions for one opportunity, and tests of the risky assumptions. A 2×2 of importance against evidence is widely credited to Bland and Osterwalder, *Testing Business Ideas* (2019). The book was not re-read, so the named workshop stays **Emerging**. The kill-the-fatal-assumption move is on Torres's pages and is **Proven** there.

The order that fits, without replacing the client's page: reframe the sentence and mark what is out (GDS); hang opportunities under an outcome (Torres); test the assumption that would forbid the build, such as urgency, residency, the date, or a missing system of record; write "stay at G0" or "stop." Walking the ladder because three names were typed is a tunnel. The ladder prevents skips. It is a weak discovery method. The hidden world already leaves the data assessment open at the end of Module 1.

## Assistants in 2025–2026, for people who are not all specialists

**Emerging**, with GDS's caveats attached. The [UK coding-assistant trial](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-coding-assistant-trial/ai-coding-assistant-trial-uk-public-sector-findings-report) ran from November 2024 to February 2025. 2,500 licences were offered and 1,900 assigned across more than 50 organisations. 1,100 Copilot business licences and 173 Gemini Code Assist licences were redeemed. Analysis used 424 survey responses from 31 departments. 73 percent of respondents reported five or more years of coding. Average self-reported saving was 56 minutes a working day, 24 of them on creating code or analysis. 67 percent reported less time searching. Copilot telemetry showed about 15.8 percent of suggested lines accepted. 39 percent said they committed suggested code. 58 percent would not go back. Mean satisfaction was 6.6 out of 10. Eight percent reported no meaningful saving. GDS records a missing telemetry month, the festive period, possible double-counting, and optimism bias, and says the trial will not drive procurement. Studies GDS cites and this review did not open are not repeated.

**Emerging**, and the authors have already qualified it. METR's early-2025 trial, 16 experienced developers and 246 tasks on repositories they knew, found a 19 percent slowdown with early-2025 tools (interval +2 to +39 percent), against a forecast of 24 percent faster and a post-hoc belief of 20 percent faster. Tools were mainly Cursor with Claude 3.5 or 3.7 Sonnet. See [the July 2025 write-up](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/). The [24 February 2026 update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/) reports raw follow-up estimates of an 18 percent speedup for returners (interval −38 to +9) and 4 percent for new recruits (interval −15 to +9), and says selection (people refusing AI-free work) and concurrent agents make the size unreliable, likely too small. METR's belief is that early-2026 tools help this population more than early-2025 tools did. That belief is not a number to teach. A [17 February 2026 note](https://metr.org/notes/2026-02-17-exploratory-transcript-analysis-for-estimating-time-savings-from-coding-agents/) treats a large time-savings factor on agent transcripts as a soft upper bound, because people pick tasks the agent suits and do not spend the whole day there.

The result worth teaching is the gap between felt speed and a clock, plus the refusal to export either study onto non-specialists in an unfamiliar client. Quoting 56 minutes or 19 percent as this cohort's outcome is **Experimental**.

**Proven** limit. GitHub's docs say content exclusion does not apply to Copilot Chat Edit or Agent modes, or to Copilot CLI; it is not guaranteed for third-party MCP servers; the IDE can still pass semantic hints from an excluded file. See [content exclusion](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/security-governance-and-network-settings/content-exclusion). A repository scan has the same shape: it sees files that landed, not the prompt and not an agent session that never wrote the letter down.

Useful, given those limits: explanation and search over a file already opened (**Emerging**, and exposed to the optimism bias GDS names); a draft question list the learner edits, which the interview tree punishes if it is lazy (**Experimental**, and checkable); a test for a health response whose fields the learner named (**Proven** as a small habit, and not the arrival). Theater: agent mode over a packet that holds the sample letter (**Proven** hole); a model that labels facts, classifies a field, or writes the refusal (**Emerging** ban, following Torres); a green workflow and an unbuilt container as proof of progress (**Proven** mismatch with the discovery sources above).

## What the sources jointly allow

The fetched pre-pilot, USDS week one, GDS discovery, and NIST Map put a problem, the people in it, the constraints, and a possible refusal in front of a build. Palantir's next phase is a data foundation on a scoped problem. GDS says the service waits. This lesson can carry a discovery packet and a short repository drill. It cannot carry a starter product. Use two classification tools on one object. Keep zones as computers and the client's labels as the case rule. Show the national impact scale once, and do not merge the lists.
