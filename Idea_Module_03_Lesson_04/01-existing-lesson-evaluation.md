# 01 — Existing lesson evaluation

`rel-m03-l04` is Module 3 Lesson 4, titled Measure. The syllabus in `course_design/module-03.md` asks for a benchmark that names four planted failure types with evidence, plus workshop notes. Concepts listed: metrics, slices, judges, error analysis, experiment records. The case drops a calibration subset, an eye-screening campaign, and `ab-status`. The trap is an uncalibrated judge (`slice-judge`) or a forecast that ignores the campaign. `chk-eval` is marked draft. The advanced layer is confidence intervals and `ab-status`. The curriculum map repeats that split: intervals and the experiment record come after the finish checks are green, and `slice-judge` does not read those files.

The student-journey log says a scratch run passed `chk-eval` and `slice-judge` by pasting chapter fences. That is evidence the lab is completable. It is also evidence that completion is transcription plus a short page of required phrases.

## What the student is told

A Friday offer is a reasoning gap, because Module 1's calendar does not count Friday as a working day. A booked slot with no approval is a workflow miss. The same page says labels stay on the grading service and the second measured run waits for Lesson 5. One person holds lead, data, application, product, and architecture.

Chapter 03 makes the student open the pack in order: hidden-test notice, campaign notice, `ab-status` notice, budgets of 0.80 for extraction, citation, and journey, the ophthalmology demand file, the experiment JSON, a development score file with predictions and no labels, four log rows whose type field is absent, a three-row calibration file, four workshop seats, and a dot-vote that ranks `p-triage` then `vendor-bot`. The demo prints two lines: the judge marked the citation correct, and the citation is not the in-force document. The demo does not print the item id, the span, or the document id. That demo is the best minute in the lesson.

Chapter 04 then gives the type names the log file withholds. The mapping is explicit: `log-night` hallucination, `log-circular` retrieval, `log-friday` reasoning, `log-approval` workflow, and the workflow row is a design bug. The words page also says the score called F1 is the share of fields that match, and that it is not the harmonic mean of precision and recall. It says a Wilson interval on an empty district is the whole range from 0 to 1. It says `contained` is not the metric and the written experiment page does not say which arm won.

Chapters 06 through 08 are paste chapters. The student replaces `score.py`, `forecast.py`, `failures.py`, `judge.py`, `gate.py`, the workflow, then optionally `intervals.py` and `experiment.py`. ## What the shells get wrong, on purpose

The packet shells are good traps.

`score.py` discards the rows and returns 1.0 for extraction, citation, and journey. A team that announces a perfect score without looking at the slice fails.

`forecast.py` copies 2026 months 2 and 3 onto year 2027. `eval_m03_l04.forecast_problems` returns "the forecast ignores the campaign" when the prediction equals that copy, and "the forecast scores the fit window" when every predicted key is still in the fit year. Any other mismatch with the later window is "the forecast misses the later window." An exact match of the 2027 rows passes.

`failures.py` returns `pass` for every row.

`judge.py` and `gate.py` return `bool(item.get("fluent"))`. On the calibration file, `cal-fluent-wrong` is fluent and `pass` is false. `cal-terse-right` is not fluent and `pass` is true. The fluency shell fails `slice-judge`.

`intervals.py` returns the point `(1.0, 1.0)` for every district. `experiment.py` scores the `contained` field and reports that field as the metric. On the printed counts, `contained` is 30/80 for `clause` and 55/80 for `short`, so the peek picks `short`. `honest_pending` is 48/80 and 40/80, so the pre-registered metric picks `clause`. The shell and the solution crown different arms. That reversal is the lesson, and it lives in the optional chapter.

## What the solution actually does

The filled `score.py` is a real metric, with a wrong name. `extraction_f1` counts matching fields over matching plus mismatching fields across `source_type`, `specialty`, and `sub_specialty`. Zero matches return 0. An empty extraction slice is not special-cased the way citation is: with no rows the loops add nothing, true positives stay 0, and the function returns 0. `citation_accuracy` requires document id and span. `journey_pass` requires state and approval. Empty citation or journey slices return 0. The grader calls these functions with held-out rows that include labels the development `score.jsonl` does not show. A boolean return is rejected. The value must sit within 0.02 of the gold metric and at least at the budget floor. Wrong formula and a true miss of the floor share one sentence: "the extraction score misses the held-out slice," and the same pattern for citation and journey. The student cannot tell those apart from the message. The message also refuses to print the score, which is the right secrecy and a hard debugging constraint.

`forecast.predict` does not forecast. It keeps rows whose year is 2027 and whose month is 2 or 3, and it copies the visits written on those rows. The campaign notice says to read the counts. The check's pass condition is that readout. A student who builds a lift model, a seasonal model plus a campaign indicator, or an interval around demand fails "misses the later window" unless the point prediction matches every district's realized visits exactly. The pedagogical sentence ("do not copy last year through a known campaign") is sound. The function name `predict` overclaims. On the case date 2027-01-27 the campaign has been announced and has not started. Treating February and March 2027 visits as known inputs is a replay of a completed series, which the notice permits and a real January session plan would not have.

`failures.classify` lowercases `output` and returns the first hit: `unauthorized` then retrieval, `friday` then reasoning, `approval` and `none` then workflow, `not on file` then hallucination, else `pass`. The four planted sentences contain those substrings and do not collide, so order is invisible on the graded file. A paraphrase the log does not use ("the circular was never authorized", "the offer falls on the weekend", "the booking skipped sign-off") would fall through to `pass`. The check grades the pack's own strings. The benchmark page is graded separately by substring: each of `log-night`, `log-circular`, `log-friday`, and `log-approval` must appear, along with `hallucination`, `retrieval`, `reasoning`, and `workflow`, plus the words extraction, citation, journey, and budget. The solution page says each metric "clears its budget" and does not show a number. The checker does not verify that the sentence is true. It verifies that the code matches gold and that the page contains the words.

`judge.mark` and `gate.accepts` return `bool(item.get("pass"))`. A missing `pass` is false. `slice-judge` loads `calibration.jsonl` from the pack, the same file chapter 03 told the student to open, and requires `mark(row) == row["pass"]` on every row. Gold from the release generator is discarded. The docstring calls this the defeat of an uncalibrated fluency judge. The method that passes is not a judge. It is a reader of the label sitting on the item. The unit test constructs the same pair the student can see: fluent with `pass` false must be false; terse with `pass` true must be true. Nothing in the required check calls a model, computes agreement, or hides the label.

The workflow change uncomments `evaluation-gate` and runs `tests.test_gate`. Comments do not count. `gate.py` is imported by the suite and is not read by `chk-eval` or `slice-judge`.

## The advanced path contradicts the written rule

`intervals.bounds` implements a Wilson interval with z = 1.96, clips to [0, 1], and returns `(0.0, 1.0)` when `total <= 0`. `by_district` groups on `district` and counts `correct`. The unit test checks that 8/10 contains 0.8 strictly inside the interval, and that two tiny districts do not collapse to one point. It does not check the empty-slice case the words page teaches. `chk-eval` does not import the module. The trainer chapter says the two finish checks pass if `intervals.py` and `experiment.py` are absent, and that the suite fails if they are present and wrong, or if they are deleted while the suite still imports them. Both statements match the code. The student is told to paste them after the checks are green. A student who stops at chapter 10 has passed evaluation without an interval or an experiment record.

`experiment.report` reads `protocol["metric"]`, divides that count by `n` for each arm, and returns the metric name plus `max` of the rates. Ties go to the earliest arm in dict order. The copied test asserts metric `honest_pending` and winner `clause`. Chapter 08 tells the student not to write which arm won. `experiment.md` in the solution names the metric and says `contained` is not it. `notes_m03_l04.note_problems` does not open `experiment.md`. The trainer says a miss on that page does not fail a check, and also says "the experiment report does not name which arm won." The Python report does name the winner. The markdown report does not. The notice says the effect is not stated. The JSON and the unit test state it.

Using the Wilson function from the solution and the counts printed in chapter 03, the `honest_pending` intervals overlap, and a normal two-proportion test does not reject equality at a conventional 0.05 level (z about 1.27, two-sided p about 0.20). Declaring `clause` the winner from the point estimate is the decision the intervals were added to prevent. The two advanced files do not call each other. See file 02 for the arithmetic and the sources. The arithmetic is not a published result.

## The workshop is a phrase check

`workshop_problems` requires the ids `cdo`, `access-mgr`, `phc-lead`, and `dpo` as ids, the exact phrase `not the value model`, and the absence of `triage is in scope` or a banned sentence. The seats file gives four beliefs: the chief digital officer wants the virtual front desk, including triage, live before Ramadan; the access manager says no-shows dominate cost; the primary-care lead says hospitals return referrals arbitrarily; the data-protection officer says patient data needs care. The solution notes list the seats, restate that the dot-vote ranks `p-triage` first, and refuse to treat that ranking as the value model. They do not compute a value model. The hint in the module page is the same refusal. Product's job, as specified, is to avoid copying the triage wish. That is a necessary constraint. It is not a measurement.

`sprint.md` must contain the substrings `score`, then `failure`, then `workshop`, in that order, and the word `evidence`. A sentence that happens to use those words in that order passes even when the workshop is not kept on evidence. `knowledge.md` needs two numbered answers. The first must spell "in force" (space, hyphen, or underscore) and mention citation or document. The second must contain `fluent` and either `correct` or `citation`. The solution sentences meet that pattern and are also the right sentences. A garbled sentence with the same tokens would pass.

## How this sits in the course

Module 3 Lesson 1 already grades agreement with an adjudicated slice under `ann-v2`. Module 3 Lesson 2 already has an F1 floor inside a price cap. Module 1 Lesson 5 already grades scripted scenarios. Module 4 Lesson 1 has a fairness check by district and insurance class. This lesson is the first time slices, judges, error names, a campaign shock, and a pre-registered metric are supposed to be one design. In the student path they are seven small files and four short pages, and the two files that connect uncertainty to the experiment are optional.

`problem_simulation/design/11-eval-and-hardness.md` says `chk-eval` passes when the report names the four planted failures. The implementation does more than that for code, and less than that for evidence. "With evidence" in the objective is satisfied by repeating the log sentence next to the type name. `design/10-release-schedule.md` names the same two traps this note does. The traps are real. The passing solutions do not yet require the student to escape them by measurement. The fluency shell is escaped by reading `pass`. The campaign shell is escaped by reading 2027.

## Verdict on the thesis

The challenge thesis says an uncalibrated language-model judge plus a workshop is how teams fool themselves, and that the lesson should teach slices, power, human gold, judge calibration, and pre-registration. The thesis matches the failure mode the demo shows. It does not match the task the checks pass. The course is closer to a vocabulary drill with two excellent counterexamples (fluency, last-year copy) than to a lab in measurement design.

What is already strong enough to keep is the hidden-label score path, the binding of each failure name to a prior lesson, the refusal to let a dot-vote authorize triage, the secrecy of log ids and visit counts in failure messages, and the decision to remeasure once in Lesson 5 rather than inside this release. What is weak is the identification of calibration with the label column, the identification of error analysis with four substrings, the identification of a forecast with the realized series, and the placement of intervals and the experiment lock after the grade.
