# Gate model

A mastery gate is a blocking specification. It is met or not yet. The learner may revise it until it is met. Meeting it opens the next gate. An extension never sits on that path.

A distinction lab is a second bundle, in the sense Oregon State's specifications-grading note uses: more advanced work, satisfactory or unsatisfactory, skipped without closing the next mastery gate. The capstone may claim a lab only when that lab's check is met.

Practice chapters still carry the twenty traps in `Idea_CrossCutting/06-instructional-design/03-proposed-progression.md`. A trap is not deleted. It is absorbed into the gate that owns that kind of decision. The gate's specification lists the decision lines from those chapters, so the reading is part of the lock. There is not a second lock per chapter.

No duration is set. The Government Digital Service publishes typical lengths of about 4 to 8 weeks for discovery and about 6 to 8 weeks for alpha. Those figures belong to a multidisciplinary team on a live public service. Copying them onto a lesson would invent the timetable Phase 1 refused.

## How many gates

**Blocking mastery gates a strong mixed learner can finish alone: 9 to 12. Center: 10.**

**Distinction labs the course should offer: 6 to 9. Center: 7.**

This is a design judgment, not a completion study. Nothing opened for this note measured this cohort.

The reasons for the mastery range:

- The program already has four modules and twenty lessons. Phase 1 would lock the path about twenty times, once per decision. Bloom (1968) treats aptitude as time and cites homework-time ratios on the order of six to one among school pupils. He also writes that a task painful enough is given up. Employed staff working alone have less protected time than that school sample, and they do not have the tutor his mastery picture depends on. Twenty blocking revision cycles, even small ones, are more locks than this mix can be asked to survive. Four module memos, the other obvious cut, can all be met in prose. Zinkevich's Rule 2 says to implement metrics. The ML Test Score gives the half point only when a test was executed, and the final score is the minimum of four sections. Four memos leave that minimum at 0.
- A strong member of this mix can read a Python file and can revise one completion in one file. They are not a specialist in retrieval, adapters, or cloud operations. One new running behavior per gate matches Phase 1's element-interactivity rule and still gives the brief a local implementation. Seven running behaviors plus three written decisions is the center.
- The floor is 9: fold the no-model table into the reframe gate when the approach families are short enough to sit on that same specification. The ceiling is 12: the only additions that earn a lock are a deletion that actually reaches the stores the lab has, and a cost gate whose total includes staff time and the cost of being wrong, not only tokens. A thirteenth lock would be a second subsystem wearing a specification.
- Above 12, the course is asking for more blocking cycles than it has new task classes. Below 9, the credential can be earned without a running boundary, a measurement, and a response to a bad signal. That is the commentator's credential. It may still exist as a case-judgment bundle for a learner who does not write code. It is not forward-deployed engineering competence.

The reasons for the lab range:

- Specifications grading puts the higher standing in a separate bundle. Phase 1 already did this with extension labs. The brief does not promote those labs onto the only path.
- Six labs is one extra mechanism for each module that has one, plus a live-account lab, plus one access slice. Nine adds a generated client and a running handoff. Past nine, the optional pile repeats a mechanism: a second index, a second messenger, a graph index beside fusion. That pile becomes a second course that authors must keep green.
- The range is how many labs to offer. It is not a prediction that every strong learner finishes all of them. After ten blocking gates, each lab is a new task class and another revision cycle. Some strong learners will finish none. A persistent engineer may finish a few. Requiring the mastery set and the full lab set is the platform-company end of the conflict.

Someone who can meet the written gates and cannot meet the running gates has case judgment on Al-Waha. The forward-deployed claim requires the running gates as well. The two claims stay separate so the course does not hand the job title to a memo, and does not bar a non-engineer from the decisions.

## The ten mastery gates

| Id | Gate | Production | Lessons whose decision lines it absorbs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MG-1 | Reframe | Written. The sponsor sentence is restated as a problem. The cost of the status quo points at a file in the packet. A recommendation to stop is a pass. | Module 1, Lesson 1, and the sponsor-sentence line of Lesson 2 |
| MG-2 | No-model | Written table. Every problem id has a family. Triage stays blocked. The clock and the missing slots are not assigned to a language model. Two person ids are not merged, as a refusal traced to those ids. | Module 1, Lessons 2 and 5; the identity refusal in Module 3, Lesson 1 |
| MG-3 | One refusal | One completion in one file. A tool does not book from a sentence inside a referral. | Module 1, Lesson 4 |
| MG-4 | Degrade | One local module. A nightly or partial file is accepted once. The booking does not wait on the live API the other side does not have. The mart's wait number stays a claim, in a sentence on the same spec. | Module 2, Lessons 2 and 5 |
| MG-5 | Metrics before a model | One automated check on a fixture. A schema or expectation holds, and one metric is not a model loss. The check runs with the heuristic or with the model still blocked. | The red-test idea in Module 2, Lesson 3, stripped to this one check |
| MG-6 | Go, hold, or remove | One local eval. An uncalibrated judge does not pass. One slice fails a metric the sponsor likes. The function returns go, hold, or remove. One later run shows the effect of one change. | Module 3, Lessons 4 and 5 |
| MG-7 | Outside the prompt | One function. Candidates are filtered by audience and by status before a score. A staff leaflet and an unauthorized circular fail. Privilege sits in that function, not in a system prompt. | Module 3, Lesson 3, at the core size already split in `05-core-versus-extension.md` |
| MG-8 | Local incident | One fixture of spans or a stale cache. The passing edit is a runbook action. Editing the model fails the check. A short line names who on the phone is affected. | Module 4, Lesson 2 |
| MG-9 | Cloud policy | One infrastructure template. A local policy check fails it closed. No account. | The five deploy decisions in Module 2, Lesson 4, as rules in the template |
| MG-10 | Recombine | Written. Earlier refusals still hold. Triage is a non-goal. A skipped lab is not claimed. Each claim points at a file, a persona, or a measurement that exists. | Module 4, Lesson 5, without its integration lab |

MG-1, MG-2, and MG-10 are design gates. They are blocking because the brief's first verb is investigate, and because the Government Digital Service treats a stop, a cost, and a definition of success as the product of discovery. They are not deployments.

MG-3 through MG-9 are the running gates. Each adds one behavior. MG-5 is allowed to be a test file rather than an application. That is still implementation: the test executes.

Floor, 9 gates: merge MG-2 into MG-1. Ceiling, 12 gates: add MG-11, a deletion call that reaches the stores this lab actually has, and MG-12, a cost total that includes people and failure. Both stay off the center unless a later edit shows the absorbed lines in MG-1 and MG-7 are too thin to carry them.

## Distinction labs

A lab opens after the mastery gate it depends on. It is one mechanism and one check. Skipping it leaves MG-10 open and strikes the matching sentence from the proposal.

| Id | Lab | Depends on | What the capstone may then say |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DL-1 | Apply the MG-9 template in a real account, smoke-check it, destroy it | MG-9 | That this template was applied and destroyed |
| DL-2 | Reciprocal-rank fusion on a public in-force pair, after the audience filter | MG-7 | That a fused index was built |
| DL-3 | A registered adapter inside the packet's quota and price, against the prompt baseline | MG-5 and MG-6 | That a registered adapter beat the baseline inside the cap |
| DL-4 | `site-a` fails, in-flight approvals survive, insurance is off the booking path | MG-4 and MG-8 | That a site was failed |
| DL-5 | Chat keeps a reserve while a batch runs, at the packet's current price | MG-5 | That chat and batch shared a quota in the run |
| DL-6 | One patient-visible slice in Arabic and English, language declared per part | MG-7 | That this slice was tried in both languages |
| DL-7 | The hidden attack set, or a supply and poisoning fixture, beyond the single MG-7 control | MG-3 and MG-7 | That the hidden set was run |

Center is these seven. Floor is six: drop DL-7, because MG-3 and MG-7 already contain one control each. Ceiling is nine: add DL-8, a generated client against the removed FHIR field, and DL-9, the contact-center handoff and the HR leave cancellation as running code. Graph retrieval, a contextual prefix, a second channel, and a labeling vendor do not get a lab slot. They may be named, with the limit attached, in the way Phase 1 already named them for the retrieval lesson.

## Skills that must be implemented

To claim forward-deployed competence on this case, the following must run. A description does not substitute. The ML Test Score half point is not awarded for a paragraph that names a test. The test has to have been executed.

1. **A refusal in code** (MG-3). One file. The tool does not book from untrusted text.
2. **A degrading integration** (MG-4). One module. A partial or nightly file is accepted once, and the booking does not block on the API that is absent.
3. **A data check and a metric** (MG-5). Automated, on a fixture, with the model still simple or still blocked. This is Zinkevich's Rule 2 and Rule 5, and it is one data test in the ML Test Score, not seven.
4. **A go, hold, or remove** (MG-6). The numbers come from a frozen set. An uncalibrated judge fails. One slice can veto a flattering aggregate. One change is remeasured.
5. **A control outside the prompt** (MG-7). Audience and status are filtered before score. The test calls the function. The prompt is not the control.
6. **An operational response** (MG-8). Given a bad signal, the learner changes a flag, a cache, or a route. The model file is the wrong fix. The outcome line names the person on the phone, because point 14, as updated 29 January 2026, requires user outcomes and bias to be watched, and it forbids leaving assurance entirely to an automated tool. The checker grades the action. The outcome line is a short specification on the same gate.
7. **A cloud control a pipeline can reject** (MG-9). The template is the implementation. The claim stops where the next section stops.

One automated test in each of data, model, infrastructure, and monitoring is a section score of 1 and a minimum of 1. On Table V that still means serious holes are likely. The capstone may say the four sections were each touched with one executed test. It may not say the system is production-ready, mission-critical, or at an exceptional ML Test Score.

## Skills that stay design-only

These are real forward-deployed skills. On this path they are memos, tables, or named rejections. Building them is how the course becomes a platform company.

- **Whether the service should exist**, and the current cost of the problem (MG-1). Discovery's product is that decision. Alpha's prototype, if any, is the single risky behavior in a later running gate. The rejected service is not built.
- **Which problems get no model**, including triage, the lying clock, and the refusal to merge two people (MG-2). A linker, a forecast model, and a custom classifier are not installed.
- **Procurement.** Objectives, acceptance, who may change priority, data and code rights, and how the agency exits. Point 11 asks the team to show build versus buy and to avoid a lock-in. The showing is the memo inside MG-10. There is no vendor sandbox.
- **A disagreement between owners.** Who can say no, and what a legitimate party loses. One recorded decision inside MG-10.
- **The integration that was rejected.** Change-data capture, a broker, or a live slot API can be the wrong tool. The rejection is a sentence on MG-4. The rejected system is not stood up.
- **Everything past the first simple pipeline.** Adapters, fusion, multi-region failover, and a surge harness are DL-2 through DL-5. Zinkevich's Phase II starts after the pipeline is trusted. Naming them in MG-2 or MG-10 is the design skill.
- **ML Test Score items with no executed test.** Canary releases, automatic rollback, a training-serving skew harness, feature ownership, and a full fairness audit are listed as unmet. A documented wish is not the half point.
- **The DPIA as a legal judgment.** Risk tier, residual risk, and the 72-hour clock are sentences on MG-10. Proportionality is a qualitative judgment. The running control is MG-7, not the essay.
- **Accessibility as a program.** Alpha asks for a plan and for inclusion in research, not for a production audit. A full WCAG audit is out of scope. DL-6 is the one slice a learner may implement.
- **The next month.** On-call, a support queue, and a standing team are a handover note on MG-10. Point 8 says a live service is never finished and does not require a full team all the time. This course cannot staff the following month.

## AWS without a budget

Two different claims, two different labs.

**Mastery, MG-9.** The learner writes one template, in one dialect chosen once for the course. CloudFormation or Terraform, not both. The template states the deploy decisions the case already cares about: encryption, a block on public access, no wildcard administration, an explicit region or residency constraint, a pinned model digest, and logs that do not carry a national id. A local checker the course ships fails the template when any of those rules is broken. No account, no card, no region quota.

The sentence this supports is: "I specified a cloud control that a pipeline can reject." Point 11 is the warrant for doing it this way. The tool has to be cheap, and the team has to be able to change it later. One dialect is that choice.

The sentence this does not support is: "I operated this on AWS." Point 14 requires a test environment as close to live as the team can make it, a response plan, and human oversight of quality. A policy check is the control specification. MG-8 is the response. Together they are still a rehearsal. They are not a live service. The January 2026 line on point 14 also means MG-9 cannot be the only operate evidence. An automated policy result with no MG-8 action is not yet met as an operate pair.

**Distinction, DL-1.** The same template is applied in an account the learner already has, smoke-checked, and destroyed in that lab. Destroy is part of met. If there is no account, DL-1 stays unmet and MG-10 stays silent on AWS operation. The mastery path does not wait.

An emulator or a compose file may sit under MG-4 as a local process. It is not DL-1. Point 14's "as similar to live as possible" is the reason. An emulator does not settle identity and access, quotas, or billing, and grading it as AWS teaches a different system than the one named in the brief. A free tier is still an account and a payment instrument. It is a budget gate. It belongs on DL-1, for a learner who already has it.

## If every lesson is a full local build and a full AWS build

That pattern collapses both sides into the worse version of each.

The lesson now holds two task classes. Phase 1's progression rule says a second subsystem is another lesson or a lab. The decision stops being the object of study. The learner maintains two environments, and the drift between them becomes the work: credentials, image pulls, region errors, and a bill. People who understood the refusal fail the lesson on the account. People who can finish both stacks paste them, because two gold trees are faster than studying either. The checker reports that the files behave. It does not report that the refusal was chosen. The assessment dossier already names that validity error for one pasted file. Two pasted stacks double it.

Bloom's perseverance point lands here. A dual build is painful in a way that has little to do with the case. A mixed solo learner gives it up, or gives up the chapters that follow. Specifications grading makes the failure conjunctive: one missing piece fails the whole specification. Doing that twice per lesson, twenty times, with no instructor and no token budget, is how a self-paced mastery path empties out.

The Government Digital Service phases get flattened. Discovery was not supposed to start the build. Alpha was supposed to prototype the riskiest assumption and throw code away. Live operation is a later definition of done. A lesson that always does all three at full size never discovers, never throws anything away, and still does not operate a service, because nothing lives past the lesson.

The ML Test Score gets gamed in the expensive direction. Twenty templates raise an infrastructure theater score and leave data and monitoring thin. The final score is the minimum, so the theater does not move the number that matters. It does spend the author's time, twice, on every lesson: forty stacks to keep green.

## What to cut first when the course bloats

1. **The second environment.** If a lesson grows a full local build and a full AWS build, delete the AWS build from that lesson. MG-9 remains the only cloud-policy gate. DL-1 remains the only live account. A second infrastructure dialect goes at the same time. One template language is enough.
2. **Duplicate distinction labs.** Any lab that repeats a mechanism already graded: a second index, a second messenger, a graph beside DL-2, a second failover beside DL-4. Named reading can stay. The check goes.
3. **Blocking gates above 12,** starting with any new memo that does not change a later running check. MG-11 and MG-12 are the first of the center-ten to leave if even the ceiling feels heavy. They are the optional extras, not the core seven behaviors.

What stays when those cuts are made: the refusal that is the trap, the single local behavior that makes the refusal executable, and the rule that a skipped lab cannot be claimed in MG-10.
