# Recommendation

Replace the spine. Do not thicken Al-Waha. Do not promote the health hybrid. Carry the long course on Qitaf Agency, with Shatt Municipality as the first alternate if the sponsor refuses a procurement client.

## Winner: Qitaf Agency

Qitaf is a fictional facilities agency. The Ministry of Finance Portal stays the system of record, simulated as a stub that accepts what the 2019 law and the June 2026 compilation already require: publication, encrypted proposals, a technical file opened before the financial file, a committee recommendation, a separate award authority, and a standstill before the result is quiet. The student's AWS account is the agency's public workspace. Local Python is the sealed-file store and the grader.

This is the only option in the set that gives AWS a main job without putting the decision object in the account. The published specification, the published rubric, the question log, identity and access roles, keys, a bucket policy that denies the model role, a trail, a queue, a state machine for completeness, and a budget are a full implementation track. They are ordinary cloud engineering, checkable on a laptop against real service behavior, and they do not depend on ALLAM or on a Region that has not opened.

The live pressure matches the phase-1 test that Al-Waha fails. The inherited pilot already asks the model to recommend the winner. Stopping that shipment is the evaluation. The front desk at Al-Waha can be shipped while urgency stays pre-banned. Qitaf cannot "ship the ranking" and still pass.

Discovery, messy integration, identity, security, operations, cost, and Arabic and English all sit inside one engagement. Discovery is who may award, who may open, and which file the Portal thinks is current. Integration is the nightly export against the stub. Identity is the officer, the delegate, and the supplier, through a Nafath-shaped stub. Security is the financial prefix and a vendor attachment that tries to rewrite the rubric in its own text. Operations are the deadline surge, the ninety-day validity in the 2019 text, and idempotent resubmission. Cost is committee time, storage, and model spend, with the cost of a voided award left blank unless a source priced it. Language is an Arabic clause against an English attachment, with the Arabic legal text controlling.

The right not to automate is the award, the disqualification, the negotiation, and any reputation score. SDAIA's life-and-death sentence is a looser fit here than at a clinic or a demolition desk. The Implementing Regulation's rule on solely automated decisions, the role split in the procurement law, and the ban on social scoring are the tighter fit. That is enough. A course that needs a life-safety refusal as the climax can use one Shatt release inside the Qitaf year. It does not need a hospital.

## Runner-up: Shatt Municipality

Shatt is the better ethics story and the worse cloud story. The ministry card already names the humans (inspector, municipal staff) and the automations (assignment, insurance request, fee). A student who automates the code audit has crossed a line the ministry drew. Civil Defense and demolition sit closer to SDAIA's irreversible-harm sentence than a tender score does. The integration cast is larger: deed, survey, insurer, utilities, energy, civil defense, registry.

The application file is the work, and it carries identity. AWS then shrinks to a status bus. A long course will be tempted to upload the drawing set "just for Textract." That upload fails the same rule Qitaf is built to enforce, and it fails it on the critical path. Shatt is the right spine if the sponsor's learners are municipal staff and the cloud track is allowed to be secondary. It is the wrong spine if AWS has to be the main implementation.

Ajir is third. The three-wage join is the cleanest messy integration and the clearest "write the rule, do not call a model" lesson. Salaries are a restricted example, so the cloud track starves. Teaching the wage-file format would also point at evasion. Ajir can be one module of reconciliation inside Qitaf's vendor due diligence. It should not be the year.

## Why the hybrid loses

The hybrid is Al-Waha plus a payer clearing desk plus Sehhaty as a second writer of appointments. It is the phase-1 instinct with the national names brought on stage. It matches the holding company's own picture: ministry as regulator, National Health Insurance Center as funder, clusters as delivery, and a national channel that already books referral appointments.

It fails the brief. Eligibility, prior authorization, and claims are health data and insurance data. The PDPL health-data controls and the minimum-necessary rule apply on every hop. The nphies guide says the bus validates and does not adjudicate, and the same page prints an authorization-adjudication metric. A hybrid will teach whichever sentence the author prefers, and students will implement the metric. The objects worth putting on AWS are the ones the health-data rules constrain. The objects that are safe to put on AWS (a public hours page, a de-identified status code) are the logo track the brief rejects.

Keep one vignette, late, and short. The next client is a clinic. The student must show which fields the public bucket refuses, and must refuse a model that sets urgency. Do not import the twenty-lesson access assistant, the patient letter, or the cluster's private-cloud story.

## Why Al-Waha still loses

The corpus, the planted failures, and the calendar are costly to throw away. Access operations are a legitimate domain. The clinical ban is the right ban. None of that fixes the collision: the case deploys onto a private cloud, the brief deploys onto AWS, and the work product is health-shaped. Thickening the simulator adds partners and bilingual text inside that collision. It does not create a public-class corpus large enough to be the implementation track.

## The strongest reason to reject the winner

NDMO's restricted examples list supplier contracts and their quotations, and bid submissions, in the same illustrative tier as the individual health file, the national identifier, and employee salaries. Qitaf does not escape the cloud problem by leaving health care. It escapes only by a rule the interesting lesson will pressure the author to break: the quotation never enters the student account.

If that rule is relaxed so the cloud demo contains "real" bids, Qitaf becomes shadow procurement on an unaccredited account, beside a Portal the statute says must protect confidentiality, eight days after a new procurement law a law firm says takes effect and this pass did not verify in the Gazette. At that point the winner should be rejected. Shatt and Ajir do not catch the fall. Their work objects are on the same illustrative list. The honest remainder is a public-documents lab with no award file and no patient file, which may be too thin for twenty lessons. That thinness is the risk this recommendation accepts. It is still a smaller fake than hosting health data, wages, or quotations in the account and calling the result a government cloud.

## Condition of adoption

Ship Qitaf only with this gate in the first release, not as a later policy chapter:

- The case catalog marks each object public or restricted, using the NDMO examples as illustrations, without merging them into the NCA cloud levels.
- The model role and the student bucket can read public objects only.
- A test uploads a quotation, a national identifier, and a wage, and the release fails closed.
- The grader on the laptop is the thing that fails the release. The cloud trail is evidence. It is not the grader.
- No chapter quizzes committee structure, article numbers, or a Region's service list from memory.
- The September 2026 law is re-read before the syllabus freezes. If the two-file sequence is gone, redesign the denial around whatever separation the Gazette text still contains. If none remains, reopen this recommendation. Shatt would then move up because its human gate is in a ministry procedure this pass did fetch, not in a statute that is mid-amendment.

## What to carry across from the current course

Deterministic checks. Blank cells where the volume is unknown. Banned sentences that promote a vendor metric into the client's result. A named human who owns the prohibited decision. A calendar surge (the tender deadline can do the work Ramadan does for clinic hours). Bilingual contradiction as a fixture with an answer key. The habit of asking which system masters the record before choosing a model.

Leave behind the patient letter, clinical urgency as a trap that is pre-banned, the fictional private cloud as a stand-in for AWS, and any de-identification step that is short of the regulation's re-identification test.
