I am Harper Singh. I am the integration engineer at Northline Access Collaborative. I am writing on Thursday, 19 January 2023. The office week is Monday through Friday. This Thursday is an office day. The wall clock is America/Vancouver. This page is a field map. It says which spreadsheet column I am placing on which of the three logs when a workbook row is copied forward. The three logs are the hospital log, the channel log, and the payer log. They are three logs. They are not one table. A placement of a column is not a shared write, and it is not a picture of a screen.

I want the September refusal in the same breath as the map, so nobody can skim the columns and call them the table that was declined. On 14 September 2022 I filed the letter of that refusal. Lena Brooks declined for Harbor Coverage. Jordan Hale declined for Cascade Seattle. Harbor will write Harbor's book. Cascade will keep Cascade's charts in Cascade's book. They will not write one table together. A code copied from one header onto another is text. It is not a shared table. I am not reopening that letter by drawing this map. The map does not install a pen that both of them hold. It says, when Northline takes a workbook it has already been sent and appends from it, which log receives which column. Harbor's appends are payer appends. Cascade's appends are hospital appends. The bay's own working sheet, when the bay appends from it, is a channel append. Each log receives the rows of its own kind. I do not stand in a common place and mark all three with one stroke.

The samples under this map are the workbooks the bay already knows. Harbor's workbook arrives from Ruth Alvarez, or from the mailbox Lena Brooks's office uses. Cascade's workbook arrives from Jordan Hale, or from the night tray when Omar Farouk's shift has put a file on the wire. The bay's working sheet is the sheet June Lau keeps because member services cannot hold every workbook in its head. I have the headers in front of me. I am mapping headers. I am not inventing a header that is not on the paper in order to give the logs a neater key.

Phone is digits. The telephone column on Harbor's sheet, the telephone column on Cascade's sheet, and the telephone column on the bay's working sheet are the same shape in this map, and the shape is digits. There is no separator I am being asked to preserve, because the cells I am looking at do not contain a separator. A Vancouver number is ten digits in a row. A Washington number is ten digits in a row. I will give the shape and not a member's life. A cell that reads 6045550136 is copied as 6045550136. A cell that reads 2065550148 is copied as 2065550148. I do not insert a dash after the third digit. I do not insert a dash after the sixth. I do not leave a space where speech would pause. Noel Park's handwritten gap is not the column. When the source cell is digits, the log receives digits. The phone column from a Harbor row is placed on the payer log, because that row is a payer row. The phone column from a Cascade row is placed on the hospital log, because that row is a hospital row. The phone column from the bay's working sheet is placed on the channel log, because that row is a channel row. Placing the same shape of digits on more than one log, when more than one workbook carried them, is three copies of text. It is not a rule that the three logs must agree, and it is not a correction of one copy by another.

I am calling out two columns that do not match. One column is member id. It is a header on Harbor's workbook, and it appears on the bay's working sheet when June has copied it. The other column is MRN. It is a header on Cascade's workbook. I have laid a Harbor row and a Cascade row beside each other when both sheets carried the same referral string in the reference column, and the member id cell and the MRN cell were not the same string. They do not match. A reader who hopes the two headers are two names for one identifier will not get that hope from me. Member id is one column. MRN is another column. The characters in one are not the characters in the other on the pairs I have actually looked at. I am placing the member id column, when the source row is Harbor's row or the bay's copy of Harbor's member id, onto the payer log and onto the channel log as the member id those sources already had. I am placing the MRN column, when the source row is Cascade's row, onto the hospital log as the MRN that source already had. Placement is not a claim that either column reaches across and finds the other.

I do not say which id joins. I do not say that member id joins the payer log to the hospital log. I do not say that MRN joins the hospital log to the payer log. I do not say that member id joins the channel log to either of the others. I do not say that MRN joins the channel log to either of the others. I do not elect one of them as the identifier a later reader should follow when the two columns disagree, because on the samples they do disagree. The map records the disagreement. It does not cure it by choosing. If a person builds a sheet that matches member id to MRN and calls the match a join, that sheet is a sheet that person built. It is not this map. I have been asked, in the room where I pinned the headers, which of the two ids is the one that joins. I am not answering. The question can stay asked. This page leaves it asked.

The reference column is on all three workbooks under headers that are not identical and that I am still calling the reference column because that is the work the cell does. A referral string typed on Harbor's sheet is placed on the payer log as text in that row's payload. A referral string typed on Cascade's sheet is placed on the hospital log as text in that row's payload. A referral string typed on the bay's working sheet is placed on the channel log as text in that row's payload. I am saying "placed" and "text" on purpose. Jordan's sentence from September still binds me. A copied code is not a shared table. I am not saying the reference column is the id that joins, and I am not saying it is not, as a ruling. I am saying I was asked about member id and MRN, those two columns do not match, and I do not say which id joins. The reference column is mapped as a copy of text onto the log that received the row. The copy does not reach into the other logs and demand a twin.

Region is a column on Harbor's sheet and on the bay's working sheet.
