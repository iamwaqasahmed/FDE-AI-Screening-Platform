NORTHLINE ACCESS COLLABORATIVE
Vancouver desk

Our file MIG-01-ASK
To: the bay drawer, and to Dana Okonkwo so the operations chair can see an ask it did not write
From: Morgan Ibarra, desk supervisor
Place: 4th floor, Vancouver
Date: 8 February 2021
Subject: I am asking for one workbook

I am Morgan Ibarra. I supervise the bay at the Vancouver desk of Northline Access Collaborative. I am writing on Monday, 8 February 2021. The office week is Monday through Friday, and this Monday is an office day. The wall clock in the bay is America/Vancouver. This page is the request itself. It is the thing I am asking. It is not a sketch of a later system, and it is not a rule for a glass. I am asking for one workbook.

I am putting a jacket line in the same ink, at the top, so a later hand cannot peel it off and grow a smoother author. Dana Okonkwo later adopted this request. She did not write it. I wrote it. No predecessor of hers appears on this page. I am not inventing a person who held the operations chair before her, and I am not borrowing a name from the hallway to carry the ask. Dana Okonkwo is the operations director. She holds that chair on this Monday. She does not run the bay. She does not stand over the sheets and tell Noel Park which cell to copy. She does not tell June Lau which workbook to open first when the tray is thick. Those are bay acts. I do them, on the days they are done, because the bay is the job I have. A habit in which one person directs the whole desk, every clerk's sentence and every figure a member hears, is not the habit of this room on this morning. I am not describing that larger habit as if it were already mine, and I am not describing it as if it were already hers. Dana later adopted the request. Adoption is a later act. It is not this morning, and it does not move the pen out of my hand into a chair I do not sit in.

The thing I want stopped is a pile of workbooks. Harbor Coverage sends a workbook. Cascade Seattle sends a workbook. The bay keeps a working sheet of its own, and that working sheet is a workbook too. A referral can sit in all three in a single week, typed three times, with time cells that do not match and a currency code left as the payer typed it. I have watched June open Harbor's file, then Cascade's file, then our sheet, and then a paper message Noel wrote by hand with a gap in the telephone number. I am not asking anyone to admire the care. I am asking for one workbook so the bay can stop holding the week as three typings plus a margin. One workbook. That is the whole of the request. I have said it in the hallway since January, and each time it has dissolved into a conversation about which time a person ought to believe. I am putting the request on paper so it stops dissolving into that conversation. The conversation may continue beside the paper. The paper does not award it.

What I mean by one workbook is a single book the bay opens when a member matter is in the tray. Harbor's columns and Cascade's columns would have a place in that book, or the senders would stop sending separate books and the bay would stop keeping a third. I am asking for the single book. I am not, in this letter, drawing the columns. I am not naming a field a clerk must read aloud. I am not saying which time cell the book would be allowed to trust if the cells still disagreed after they had been gathered. Staff already argue about those cells. June has asked me what she should say when a member asks how long a matter has been waiting and the sheets do not agree. I have not had a sentence that survives contact with the actual cells. This request does not invent that sentence in order to look finished. It asks for one workbook. People may keep arguing about stamps. This page does not hand the argument to a screen, and it does not paint a clock of its own.

I am leaving Cedar Ridge out of the workbook I am asking for. Sam Nguyen sends a nightly file from that rural clinic in Washington, and he sends it on Saturday and on Sunday as well as on the nights that follow our office days. Our office is closed Saturday and Sunday. The packet in the Monday tray is not Harbor's workbook and it is not Cascade's workbook. I am not asking Sam to become a tab in a Vancouver book so that the pile will look smaller. I am not asking him for a wire a clerk could call while a member is still on the telephone. Inlet Medical is still its own Washington clinic. I am not relabeling an Inlet sheet as Cedar Ridge, and I am not relabeling it as Cascade, in order to shrink the count of names by a trick. The name on a sheet is the name I want filed. Fraser Burnaby does not send these workbooks. Anita Desai's hospital is in British Columbia and keeps America/Vancouver. When Fraser needs the desk, what arrives is a telephone call or a short note. Priya Shah's paper stays with the clinician's jacket. I am not pulling a clinician's sentence into a workbook so the workbook will look as if it contained the care. Alex Kim's on-call notes stay with the jacket for the same reason. Evan Cho may keep asking where a sheet goes when it leaves the room. I am not answering him, from this request, with a picture of a screen.

Chris Pell takes a copy of the amount and the currency into finance. Harbor is a payer that uses both CAD and USD, and both codes appear in the cells. Chris has told the bay he does not want a currency code tidied before he sees it. I am not asking the one workbook to tidy it. A tidied code would be a code I had chosen. I have not chosen a code. Ruth Alvarez sends Harbor's paper, or the mailbox Lena Brooks uses when Ruth is not the one who attaches the file. Lena is the Harbor Coverage supervisor in Seattle. Jordan Hale keeps the records at Cascade Seattle, which is a hospital in Washington and keeps America/Los_Angeles. Omar Farouk has the night there, and a workbook from his shift can be on the wire after the bay has gone home. I receive what those rooms send. I am asking that the bay stop living in three books. I am not claiming that Harbor has agreed to give up Harbor's book, and I am not claiming that Cascade has agreed to give up Cascade's book. They have not been asked, inside this letter, to surrender their rooms. The request is for one workbook at this desk. If a later letter from either of them declines a shared surface, that letter will be theirs. It is not this one, and I am not writing their answer for them in February.

While the sheets are in front of me I will say what the telephone cells already are, because a request that pretended not to see them would be a thinner request than the tray. The telephone column arrives as digits. A Vancouver number in the sheet is ten digits in a row. A Washington number is ten digits in a row. I will give the shape and not a member's life. A cell that reads 6045550136 is a cell of digits. A cell that reads 2065550148 is a cell of digits. I do not insert a dash after the third digit, and I do not insert a dash after the sixth, and I do not leave a space where a person might pause in speech. Noel has a habit, on paper messages, of leaving a gap in a handwritten number. When the source is Harbor's file or Cascade's file, I do not want the one workbook to follow the gap. I want the digits as the cell has them. I am describing the sheets of this winter. I am not issuing a rule that every notebook in the building must obey for every year after this one, and I am not saying that a later change of shape would be a correction of the digits. The request is still one workbook. The digits are a description of what would be copied, if anyone copies, and not a second request hiding under the first.

Wes Calder's page is still in the platform binder. He left during last year. I have read the page because it sits behind the bay and because tired staff quote it when they are facing three time cells. The page speaks, in his sentences, about one instant. The workbooks in this week's tray do not arrive already reduced to one instant that I can point to. They arrive as workbooks, with the times their senders typed. I am not editing his page from the bay. I am not asking the one workbook to enact the page, and I am not asking the one workbook to retire the page. I do not have a display rule to attach to either object. Bobbi Tran is in information technology. She joined last year. She can open a workbook when a file will not open, and the bay asks her for that help. She does not own the clock page. I have not assigned it to her in this request, and I am not asking her to build the one workbook as a machine. Harper Singh is employed by Northline Access Collaborative. I am not writing another employer under his name, because I do not have one to write. I am not asking him, on this page, to construct the book. Asking for a book and building a book are different acts. I am performing the first. I am not performing the second.

Dana Okonkwo will see a copy because I am putting one on her shelf. Seeing a copy is not the same act as adoption. I have already written, for the jacket, that she later adopted this request. I am separating the two times on purpose, in sentences a reader can keep apart. The later adoption is not her signature on this Monday. It does not rewrite the request into her words. It does not mean that on 8 February 2021 she ran the bay, or ran the whole desk, or stood in the place I stand when June asks which sheet is open. Noel Park and June Lau work the sheets with me. They may telephone a member. They may read back a number the member has just spoken. They may not invent a visit out of a sentence a caller hoped was already true. I supervise that limit on this floor. I am not handing them a painted time in exchange for the workbook I do not yet have.

Copies of this request: the bay drawer, where the sheets land; Dana Okonkwo's shelf; my own book. I am not sending it to Wes Calder. He is not in the building. The office opens again on Tuesday under the same week it always keeps. If Sam sends a Saturday file, that file will wait in the tray with its own time on it. I am not telling the Monday clerk to replace that time with a workbook that is still only a request. I asked for one workbook. The pile of workbooks is what I want the one book to bring to a stop. Dana Okonkwo later adopted this request. She was not its author. She did not run this bay on the day it was asked.

Morgan Ibarra
