Subject: the first weekend file that was counted two ways, still unresolved

I am Sam Nguyen. I file at night for Cedar Ridge. Cedar Ridge is a rural clinic in Washington. The clock I work by is America/Los_Angeles. I am writing this note on Friday, 3 November 2023, in the afternoon by that clock, which is earlier than the hour I release a file. I am writing in the afternoon because the file I need to describe is not tonight's file. It is the Saturday file from 28 October 2023, file id cedar-2023-10-28, and the week since that Saturday has not told me which count to keep. This is an unresolved note. I am not resolving it by typing it. Typing is how the night book holds a thing that is still open.

It is the first weekend file I have had, since the June decision, that was counted two ways. I want "first" to be as narrow as I mean it. Harper Singh wrote on 20 June that Cedar Ridge stays on a nightly file, that there is no slot API, and that I agree. I did agree. I still agree. Weekend files are part of that agreement. I release a file on Saturday and a file on Sunday by the same rule as on a weeknight. The Vancouver desk keeps Monday through Friday and is closed when I am releasing a Saturday file. Their closed Saturday is not a reason for me to skip the release. Before 28 October, the weekend files I released after that June decision had one count I was willing to write once. Trailer and body matched, or I did not have a trailer pair to worry. I am not opening those earlier weekends in order to make this note longer. This note is about the first one that did not match. File cedar-2023-10-28. Saturday, 28 October 2023. Counted two ways. Unresolved.

The two ways are the two counts on the file itself. The trailer count I wrote was 6. The body count I wrote was 9. I am putting both numbers in the note because hiding one of them would be a resolution, and I do not have a resolution. Six is not nine. I saw six when I totaled the trailer line. I saw nine when I totaled the body lines that were actually in the file. I did not see a third number that split the difference, and I would not have written a third number if I had been tempted. A third number would have been mine. Six was a count. Nine was a count. They are the two ways. I do not know which one is the count of the file. I know which line I was looking at when I wrote each of them. That is not the same knowledge.

I did not release a reconciled file in order to make the weekend look finished. A bad pair is a pair that does not match. This pair does not match. I did not send the file and promise to explain the gap by telephone on Monday. I did not mint a second file id so that one id could wear the six and another id could wear the nine. There is one file id, cedar-2023-10-28, and it has two counts. I did not ask the Vancouver desk to ignore one of the numbers so their Monday would be quiet. Monday, 30 October, was an office day for them. I was not at their counter. I left both numbers in the night book. If someone at the desk counted the file a different way again, by rows they imported or by rows they refused, I have not been shown that third way, and I am not inventing it. The two ways I am willing to sign my name under are six and nine. Both stay. Neither is struck.

Bobbi Tran looked at what the hospital log had taken from the packet I did send up for reading, which was the packet with both counts still on it, not a cleaned packet. She is in information technology at Northline. She told me, on the telephone on Thursday, that she could see a row for the file and that she was not going to tell me which count the row believed. I am grateful she did not tell me, and I am not treating her silence as a hint. Harper has not picked. Jordan Hale archives a trailer page at Cascade when I release one. Jordan does not assign my file id, and he has not written to me this week to say that six is Cascade's number or that nine is. I am not asking him to pick. Picking would be a favor that would make this note false. The note is unresolved. A favor that ends it is not a favor I am taking.

I want the rest of the night described so the two counts do not become the only sentence in the book. Saturday, 28 October, was a file night. The building was quiet in the way it is quiet on a file night. Quiet is not a slot API. I do not have a slot API. The June decision did not give me one, and I did not grow one because a trailer and a body disagreed. I cannot take a sentence from a Vancouver telephone call and turn it into a detail line in order to make nine into six or six into nine. If a visit is not a line I can stand behind, I do not add it to force a match. I also do not delete a line to force a match. I did not delete. The body I totaled as nine is the body I still have. The trailer I totaled as six is the trailer I still have. Deleting three lines, or adding three lines, would be me choosing a winner. I am not choosing a winner.

Sunday, 29 October, had its own file. I am not rewriting Saturday into Sunday. Sunday's file is a different file id and a different night. I am not quoting Sunday's counts in this note as if they repaired Saturday. They did not. A later night is a later night. I have heard people talk as if a second send replaces a first send. I am not using that talk on cedar-2023-10-28. I did not send a second Saturday file that claimed to replace the first. I have one Saturday file with two counts. Whether a second arrival would replace a first arrival is a question I watched Bobbi leave open in the spring, on a test row, and I am not closing her question by borrowing it for my trailer. Her question was hers. My question is six and nine. Both questions can stay open in the same season without becoming one question.

The Vancouver office week does not govern this Saturday. I respect that their desk is closed on Saturday. Respect is not a transfer of cedar-2023-10-28 onto Monday, 30 October. Monday had Monday's work. The Saturday file kept Saturday's date. I am not asking Noel Park, if he opens a tray, to re-date the file so it will look like an office-day row. I am not asking June Lau to tell a member that the visit is the trailer or that the visit is the body. She has her own sentences for members. I have not given her a count to insert into them. Morgan Ibarra supervises that bay. I have not asked her to pick six or nine for a telephone script. Dana Okonkwo's office week is Monday through Friday. Her week does not choose my trailer. I am not sending this note to her as a request for a ruling. I am sending it so the ruling does not get invented in a hallway and then treated as mine.

Inlet Medical is still its own clinic. This file is a Cedar Ridge file. The site on the header I wrote is the Cedar Ridge site I write on Cedar Ridge files. I am not moving an Inlet sheet into cedar-2023-10-28 to explain a gap of three. A gap is not an invitation to relabel a neighbor. Fraser Burnaby is not this cabinet. Anita Desai's hospital is in British Columbia. Priya Shah's notes, if any were ever clipped to a jacket that is not mine, are not trailer lines. Alex Kim's on-call paper is not a count. Harper's page from June says a weekend file is still a Cedar Ridge file and that recording arrival is not a wire. I am holding him to that. If the hospital log has a line for this file, the line does not tell me whether six or nine is the file. Bobbi said as much, and I am repeating it because I do not want a log line to be promoted, by someone who likes machines, into the count I refused to pick. The log is their log. The night book is my book. This note is the night book. Two counts. No winner.

I looked again this afternoon before I typed. The trailer still says 6. The body still says 9. I did not find the three lines sitting in the wrong pile. I did not find three lines I had double-counted in a way I could show. "I did not find" is not "they are not there." I am not sure. I am writing not sure under both numbers. The note stays unresolved. If I become sure on a later night, that sureness will be a different page, with the later date on it, and it will have to say what I found. This page does not borrow a future finding. People may argue that a screen should show six, or nine, or neither, or the time on the file header. I am not in that argument as a judge. I do not award a clock. I do not say what a screen should show. I release files. This one has two counts, and I have not released a story that reduces them to one.
