From: June Lau, member services
Place: 4th floor, Vancouver
Date: 1 July 2023
Subject: the words I used when I said the system changed

I am June Lau. I work member services at the Vancouver desk of Northline Access Collaborative. I am writing on Saturday, 1 July 2023. Saturday is not an office day. The office week is Monday through Friday. The counter is shut. I am not opening the counter, and I am not pretending this page is a conversation I held across the desk while a queue was in the room. I came in to put on paper the words I already said to a member, because the words were spoken and the bay should have them in a file that is dated with the day I wrote them down. The member is a member of this collaborative's work, region on the sheet British Columbia. I am not printing the member's name in this copy. The bay slip has the name. This page has the words. I told the member the system changed. I told the member the visit is whatever the board says. I did not define the board.

I want the order of those two sentences preserved, because I said them in that order and a smoother order would be a different call. The member had telephoned on an office day and asked whether a visit was still the visit they thought they had. They had heard, from someone, that the sheets we used to open were not the thing we were opening anymore. They wanted to know if that meant the visit had moved, or vanished, or become a different day. I said the system changed. I said the visit is whatever the board says. Then I stopped. I did not add a third sentence that named a place, a clock, or a file. I am writing the stop down as part of what I told them. The stop is not a gap I intend to fill on Monday with a better definition. The stop is the call.

The system changed. I will say what I meant by that sentence, for the file, and I will keep it inside the limit of what I actually know from the bay. Harbor Coverage used to arrive as a workbook. Cascade Seattle used to arrive as a workbook. I used to keep a working sheet of my own, and that sheet was a workbook too. I was told, after 20 June, that those sheets are moving onto logs. Harper Singh is the integration engineer. The note I was given says the rest move to the logs, and that the logs are three, and that Cedar Ridge stays on a nightly file. I did not read that note aloud to the member. I did not say "three logs" to the member. I did not say "workbook" to the member. I said the system changed. That was the sentence they could use. The machinery under it is staff machinery. I am not ashamed of having been short. A member on a telephone does not become an integration trainee because they asked a fair question.

I am also not going to pretend, in this copy, that I invented the change. I did not. Morgan Ibarra put Harper's decision where the bay could see it. Dana Okonkwo's name is on the distribution of that decision as a reader. I do not know, from my chair, what Dana has adopted and what she has only received. I am not making her the voice of the call. The voice of the call was mine. Noel Park was not on the line. I was. If a later page says member services told people the system changed, this is the page that shows the words, and the words are the ones I have written twice now: the system changed, and the visit is whatever the board says.

The visit is whatever the board says. I am leaving that sentence untouched. The member asked me which board. I did not answer with a name. I did not say the board was a sheet in Seattle. I did not say the board was a sheet in Vancouver. I did not say the board was a night file. I did not say the board was a page on a screen at the bay. I did not say the board was a mart, and I did not say the board was a counter, and I did not say the board was a letter. I do not define the board in this file either. Defining it now, on a Saturday, when I refused to define it on the call, would make the file a second conversation the member did not have. The file matches the call. The visit is whatever the board says. The board is not given a surname here.

They asked me what time the visit was. I did not give them a time. I had time cells in my head from the old workbooks, more than one, and I had been told the logs keep the time that arrives with a row. I do not know which of those times I would have been right to speak. I did not pick one in order to be kind. Kindness that invents a hour is not the job I was doing. I told them the visit is whatever the board says. If the board has a time, I did not read it out, because I had not said which board I meant, and I was not going to smuggle a time in through a board I had refused to point at. They were unhappy. I am recording the unhappiness. Unhappiness is not a reason to add the sentence I withheld.

They asked whether the visit was at Cedar Ridge. The sheet in front of me, the one I had open on the office day of the call, mentioned Cedar Ridge as a place name in the member's own question, which they had repeated from an earlier conversation. Sam Nguyen files at night for that clinic, including Saturday and Sunday. Today is Saturday. I am not looking at tonight's file, because tonight's file is his release and I am not at his counter. There is no slot API I can call from this desk to turn their question into a booking while I type. I did not tell them the visit was booked. I did not tell them the visit was not booked. I told them the visit is whatever the board says. Cedar Ridge's file is a file. I did not call the file the board. I did not call it not the board. I left the word where I had put it.

They asked about coverage. Harbor's rows have carried a currency code, sometimes CAD and sometimes USD, and a region, and an answer I am not going to reconstruct from memory onto this page. Chris Pell does not want the bay to tidy a currency before he sees it. I did not tidy one on the call, and I did not offer one. Lena Brooks's office and Ruth Alvarez's spreadsheets are not a sentence I was willing to summarize as yes or no while the member waited. I said the system changed. I did not say the change was a coverage decision. A change in where a row is written is not, in my mouth, a change in what the payer answered. If they need the payer's answer, that answer is the payer's row, and I did not pretend to be the payer.

Phone numbers came up because they asked me to read back the number we have. The number on the working sheet I still had from before the move was digits, ten in a row, no dash. I read the digits. I did not insert pauses as if the pauses were part of the stored cell. Reading aloud requires breath. Breath is not a character I claimed was in the cell. I am not writing the member's number into this copy. The shape is enough for the file. Digits. I did not tell them a dashed number was the corrected number. I do not have a dashed number on that sheet.

I did not tell them which identifier we would use if a member id and an MRN did not match. I have seen those two columns fail to match. Harper's map says he does not say which id joins. I did not say it on the call in his place. The member did not ask in those words. They asked who they were in the system, which is a human question and not a header. I told them the system changed, and I told them the visit is whatever the board says. I did not answer the human question with a column name. Evan Cho has views about what leaves the room. I did not read him a jacket. There was no clinical sentence in my half of the call. Priya Shah's name did not come up. Alex Kim's name did not come up. Fraser Burnaby did not come up. The member was not standing at Fraser. I was not going to send them there by implication in order to have a building to name.

Morgan Ibarra supervises the bay. On Monday I will tell her the words I used, which are the words on this page, and I will tell her the member wanted a time and a place and a yes. I did not give the time. I did not give the yes. I gave the two sentences. If Morgan has a holding line she wants used instead, she can write it. I am not going to revise Saturday's copy into Monday's better manners. The copy is what was said. Dana Okonkwo directs operations in the sense that the operations chair is hers. She was not on the call. I am not asking this page to sound like her. Bobbi Tran keeps company with the logs in the machine room. I did not ask Bobbi, before the call, which row was the visit. I am not going to ask her after the fact to pick a board so that my sentence will turn out to have pointed at something. The sentence does not point. That is the difficulty of it, and it is also the truth of it.

I know how thin it sounds. A member who arranges a day away from work wants a day. "Whatever the board says" is not a day. I said it anyway, because the other sentences I could see were sentences I did not have the right to choose among. A night file, a payer row, a hospital sheet, a channel line, a page in a binder that talks about one instant: I can list them here for staff, and I did not list them for the member. Listing them would have been a definition parade. I refused the parade. Wes Calder's page is still in the binder. I did not quote it. Harper's September letter is still in the drawer. I did not quote it. The member heard two sentences. The system changed. The visit is whatever the board says.

This page does not award a clock to a screen. It does not say the board is allowed to use one stamp and not another. It does not say the visit occurred, and it does not say the visit did not occur. It says what I told one member on the call I am recording, written down on a Saturday when the desk is closed. If the member calls again on Monday, the words I have already used are these words. I may repeat them. I may not improve them by defining the board in the second call after I declined to define it in the first. Noel can read this copy before he picks up a related line. Reading it is not permission to add the definition I withheld.

Copies: the bay file, clipped to the slip that has the member's name; Morgan Ibarra's tray for Monday morning. I am not sending it to Cedar Ridge. I am not sending it to Harbor. I am not sending it to Cascade. The member has already heard it. The system changed. The visit is whatever the board says. I do not define the board.

June Lau
