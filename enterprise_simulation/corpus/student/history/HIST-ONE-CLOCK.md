NORTHLINE ACCESS COLLABORATIVE
Platform team, Vancouver desk
Internal memorandum

Our file HIST-ONE-CLOCK
To: the desk, the hospital record room, and the payer liaison
From: the platform team
Place: 4th floor, Vancouver
Date: 1 June 2019
Subject: a proposal to stamp every feed with one instant, and to store that instant on the mart

This leaf was typed on the platform machine. Two carbons were struck at the same time. The top copy is the one in the contract drawer. The second carbon went to the hospital record room by internal mail. The third sheet is the thin one, and it is the sheet that later took ink on the back. Nothing on these pages has been signed by the desk supervisor, by facility records, or by Harbor Coverage.

1. We are writing because the feeds that belong to one case do not carry one moment. A clerk at this desk takes a call and the desk book receives the time on the desk clock. A facility closes its day and the night file receives the time on the facility clock. A payer notice comes in the next post and receives the time at which our liaison opened it. Each of those times is honest about the room that wrote it. None of them is the same figure. When the three leaves are laid side by side, a reader cannot point to a single instant and say that every feed was stamped with it. We have spent the spring copying times from one sheet onto another by hand, and the copies do not agree with the sheets they were taken from. That labour is the reason for this proposal. It is not, by itself, a change in anyone's book.

2. The instant we mean is one recorded moment, written in the same characters on every feed that belongs to the case. We do not mean a fresh stamp invented by each room at the hour that room happens to touch the paper. We do not mean the civil time painted on a wall clock in Vancouver, and we do not mean the civil time painted on a wall clock in Seattle, except as those clocks may be consulted when the single moment is first chosen. The figure we want is one instant, kept as one instant, so that a feed from the desk, a feed from a facility, and a feed from the payer can be seen to have been marked together. The platform team proposes that every feed be stamped with that one instant.

3. We further propose that the same instant be stored on the mart. By the mart we mean the facility mart, the table the record room already keeps when it wants one row for a case rather than the whole night file. The instant would sit on that row, in a column of its own, written by the same stamp that marked the feeds. It would not live only in the margin of whichever feed arrived last, and it would not be reconstructed by a clerk with a pencil when someone asks what the feeds were marked with. If the night file is replayed, the instant on the mart would remain the instant that was stored, and the replay would not be an occasion for a second, different stamp. That is the whole of the storage request. The column is a place to keep the figure. This memorandum does not go on from there into any rule for a glass, a board, or a notice put in front of a member.

4. The mischief, as we have watched it on this floor, is ordinary and it repeats. A case is opened in the morning at the Vancouver desk. The handset time is written in the desk book. In the evening a facility file is produced, and the trailer of that file carries a later time, sometimes on the far side of the civil day as the coast counts days. The payer's notice, when it comes, carries a third time, often the time of the following office morning. The three times are then read as if they were three reports of one moment. They are not. They are three reports of three acts of writing. Staff have been circling the three times in red and asking the platform team which circle is to be copied onto the mart. We have refused those questions in the hallway, because the copy is exactly what this draft is trying to replace with a stamp made once. We are not, in this paper, choosing among the three circled times. We are asking that the feeds be stamped once, together, and that the stamp be the figure stored on the mart.

5. Local clocks make the disagreement look smaller than it is, and sometimes larger. The desk keeps America/Vancouver. Facilities on the Washington side of the work keep America/Los_Angeles. For much of the year the two zones show the same hour. They do not show the same hour on the mornings when one zone has stepped back and the other has not, and they do not make a night file and a morning desk entry into one instant merely because both can be written as an hour on a clock. A night file can be closed after the office has gone home. The office week at this desk is Monday through Friday. A facility can still put a file on the wire on a Saturday or a Sunday. Those are facts about when people write. They are not a method for turning three writing-times into one. Our proposal is that the stamp be applied as one instant in any case, including a file that arrives when the desk is dark, and that this instant, not a second rendering in local civil time, be what the mart stores.

6. We have been asked, in the drafting, who would hold the pen. The platform team is willing to be the room that applies the stamp, if the other rooms will let a stamp be applied to their feeds at all. We would take the feeds as they are produced, mark each of them with the one instant, and write that same instant onto the facility mart. We would not ask the night clerk to keep a second clock, and we would not ask the payer's clerk to telephone Vancouver before closing a notice. The practical picture is a batch. The feeds arrive. The stamp is put on. The mart is written. A late feed, arriving after the batch, would be the hard case, and we say so plainly: this draft does not yet say whether a late feed receives the instant already stored, or whether the stored instant is to be touched again. We leave that open because we do not yet have a writer who is permitted to touch every feed. Leaving it open is one reason this paper is not ready to be treated as practice.

7. A correction is the other open point. If the instant is wrong, someone will have to say so while the paper is still in the building. We do not propose that each room correct the mart in its own hand. A correction written three times would put us back among three instants. We also do not propose that a correction be silent. The sheet that changes an instant should be kept with the case, with the date of the change and the name of the person who made it. Who is allowed to be that person is not settled in these paragraphs. The desk will not want the hospital's pencil in the desk book. The hospital will not want the payer's pencil in the night file. Harbor Coverage will not want either of the others writing into a notice that has already gone to a member file on their side. We record the difficulty. We do not dissolve it.

8. An example, invented for this draft and not taken from a live jacket, may make the stamp plain. Suppose the desk writes that a request was taken at 16:10 universal time, a facility file closes at 05:00 universal time on the following calendar day, and a payer notice is opened at 15:40 universal time on the office day after that. Under the practice we have now, those three figures stay on their three feeds, and a clerk who builds a row on the mart must pick up a pencil. Under this proposal the three feeds would be stamped with one instant, and that instant would be stored on the mart, in the column described above. The example is only a picture of the stamp and of the place the stamp would be kept. It is not an instruction about any later use of the row. It names no case that is on our desks this month. The times were chosen so that they cannot be mistaken for a file we already hold.

9. We considered, and we are not advancing, a scheme in which each feed keeps the time of its own room and the mart stores all three side by side forever. That scheme needs no agreement, and it is in substance what we already do, with the three times scattered rather than lined up. It does not give us one instant. We also considered asking every room to type its own time in universal time and to call that good enough. Universal time would make the figures easier to compare. It would not make them one figure. Comparison is the work we are already doing by hand. The proposal is a stamp, not a prettier way to keep three stamps.

10. What we ask of the readers is modest on paper and large in practice. We ask the desk, the hospital record, and the payer's office to look at this wording before anyone builds the column. We ask them to say whether a feed of theirs may be stamped by a hand that is not their hand. We ask for a meeting in the third week of June, on this floor, with one person who can speak for each room. If they will not meet, the draft should stay in the drawer. The platform team will not cut a column into the mart on the strength of an unsigned carbon.

11. Distribution of this typing: platform drawer; desk supervisor's shelf; hospital record room; payer liaison folder. No copy is to be sent outside the collaborative. No copy is to be treated as the way the rooms work. The pages may be marked. They may be contradicted. They are not to be quoted as a rule that has been taken up.

12. The undersigned is the platform team as a desk, and not a single officer. No personal name is set under this typing on purpose. The person who carries the carbons is not, by carrying them, the owner of the other rooms' books.

Status: draft, not adopted.

Addendum. This was not typed. It is ink on the reverse of the last leaf, the thin carbon, kept in the integration drawer with the top copy. The hand is Harper Singh's. A corner of the leaf, written in the same ink, is dated 7 April 2022.

I took this out again in the spring. It has sat in the drawer since the platform typed it on 1 June 2019. I walked the carbon to the three organizations and asked them to let one writer put the instant on the feeds and on the mart. They would not have it.

I mean Northline Access Collaborative, the hospital, and Harbor Coverage. I had scratched a few words in the margin on a first pass. I had written that they might share a login, and then I put a line through those words, because a login was not the thing they refused. The refusal was the pen. The three organizations would not share a write path. The desk will write in the desk book. The hospital will write in the hospital book. Harbor will write in Harbor's book. There is no common place I am allowed to stand and mark all three.

I am not going to keep asking this year. I am putting the draft back as I found it, with this ink on the back so the next person in the drawer can see why the column was never cut. I have nothing further to add under these lines.

H. Singh
integration engineer
7 April 2022

Jacket footer, file room, left as filed with the ink still on the carbon.
