Subject: what the logs are, a year after the organizations refused a shared write

I am Harper Singh. I am the integration engineer at Northline Access Collaborative. I am writing on Friday, 15 December 2023. The office week is Monday through Friday. The wall clock is America/Vancouver. This year note is about the three logs, and about the note I already made when a shared write was refused. I am not writing it in order to finish a picture of a screen. I will say that at the start so the rest of the page cannot be skimmed into a display rule. I do not say what the screen should show.

The three logs exist. Their names are the hospital log, the channel log, and the payer log. They are not one table. They are not one book with three headings. A row that belongs to the hospital is appended to the hospital log. A row that belongs to the channel is appended to the channel log. A row that belongs to the payer is appended to the payer log. Each log keeps the time that arrived with the row. I do not convert those times onto each other when they are written. I do not keep a second column whose job is to line the three logs up. If a person sorts the three by the time strings and calls the sort the order of the day, the sort is a sheet that person built. It is not the store. I have said versions of this in the room all year. I am putting it in the year drawer because the spoken version gets smoothed by people who want a single sentence for a clerk.

The refusal is older than this December, and I am not rewriting it. In the spring of 2022 I took out the 2019 draft, the memorandum dated 1 June 2019 that proposes one instant on the feeds and on the mart, and I walked it to the organizations. They would not have it. I mean Northline's partners in that ask as I named them in the ink: the hospital, and Harbor Coverage, and this collaborative's own limit, which is that the desk writes the desk book. The sentence I wrote on the back of the thin carbon, dated 7 April 2022, is that the three organizations would not share a write path. The desk will write in the desk book. The hospital will write in the hospital book. Harbor will write in Harbor's book. There is no common place I am allowed to stand and mark all three. I put the draft back. I wrote that I had nothing further to add under those lines. I still have nothing further to add under those lines. This year note is not a second ask. It is a record that the refusal is still the refusal, and that the logs we actually keep are three logs because that shared pen was not given.

In September 2022 I asked a narrower question and I was refused again. I asked Harbor Coverage and Cascade Seattle to write one table, a table both of them would write. I left Fraser Burnaby out of that ask. I left Cedar Ridge out of that ask. I left the Vancouver desk's own book out of that ask. Lena Brooks declined for Harbor. Jordan Hale declined for Cascade. Harbor will write Harbor's book. Cascade will keep Cascade's charts in Cascade's book. A referral code copied from one header to another is text. Jordan said so, and he was right that a copied code is not a shared table. I filed those declines on 14 September 2022. I am not reopening them in December 2023. I am pointing at them so a reader of this year does not think the three logs are a clever way around the letters. They are not a way around the letters. The logs are separate because the organizations would not share the write. Northline did not receive permission to install one writer over all of them. I am not taking that permission now, quietly, in a year note.

The 2019 page is still on the index. Wes Calder wrote it. He left in 2020. The status printed on the page is the status he set. The page still speaks, in his sentences, as if the feeds shared one instant and the mart were that instant. I opened the page this morning. I did not strike it. I did not sunset it. I am not standing over it with a pen in this note. The logs I run do not share a write path, and they do not share a clock in the sense his page claims. Both of those things can be true of the building in the same December: the page is up, and the logs are three. I am not turning that pair into an instruction. I am not the person who will say which of those facts a screen should obey. I have been asked to say it. I am not saying it.

I want the ask recorded, because it will be repeated by anyone who finds a year note and wants it to end in a picture. People have asked me what the desk should put in front of a member. People have asked me which stamp a clerk should read aloud when the hospital time, the channel time, and the payer time do not match. People have asked me which field should be painted at the top of a panel. I did not answer those questions in 2022, and I am not answering them in 2023. I do not say what the screen should show. I do not name a field for it. I do not name a feed for it. I do not say that Wes's instant is the one a glass may use, and I do not say that one of the three log times is the one a glass may use. The screen question is a different subject from the logs. I am not taking it up. If a sentence under my name, after this page, claims that I did take it up, that sentence is not mine.

What the organizations did this year, with the logs as they are, was ordinary and separate. The channel log took the desk's appends. The hospital log took what the hospitals sent, including the Cedar Ridge nightly file. Cedar Ridge still has no slot wire a desk can call. Sam Nguyen still files at night, including Saturday and Sunday. The office week here is still Monday through Friday. A weekend file is a hospital append with the time that arrived with it. I do not move that time onto Monday so that it will sit inside the office week. The payer log took Harbor's appends. Harbor still uses CAD and USD as fields on its own rows. A currency code is not a clock, and I am not choosing a code. Inlet Medical is still its own clinic. Its site id, when a file carries it, is the id on the file. I have not merged Inlet into Cedar Ridge, and I have not been asked to pretend the merge has already occurred.

Bobbi Tran works in information technology. She can open the logs with me. She does not own Wes's page. I have not given her a script for a screen, because I do not have a script I am willing to give. Morgan Ibarra still supervises the bay. Noel Park and June Lau still speak to members. Evan Cho still treats a letter as a hospital document with its own audience. Dana Okonkwo still receives the arguments when they get loud. Chris Pell still does not want a platform sentence to pick a currency for him. I am not picking one for him in this note. Priya Shah, Anita Desai, Alex Kim, Ruth Alvarez, Lena Brooks, Jordan Hale, Omar Farouk: they remain in their rooms. Their rooms write their rooms. That is the residue of the refusal, and it is the shape of the year.

I considered, and I am not doing, a winter project in which Northline writes a fourth place and tells the others that the fourth place is the shared write they declined. A place only I can edit is my notebook. It is not the table they refused. I am not marking the 2022 letters provisional because a year has passed. Lena did not promise a revisit. Jordan did not promise a revisit. The logs will stay three. The ink will stay on the carbon. The page Wes wrote will stay on the index until someone who is not writing this sentence takes it down. I am not that someone. I do not say what the screen should show.

Copies: the year drawer; the integration drawer, in front of the April 2022 ink and the September 2022 letters; my shelf. Nothing under my name on this page chooses a stamp for a display.

Harper Singh
