NORTHLINE ACCESS COLLABORATIVE
Operations
Vancouver desk

Our file YR-2018
To: the year drawer for 2018, and to the shelf that holds the desk book
From: Dana Okonkwo, operations director
Place: 4th floor, Vancouver
Date: 14 December 2018
Subject: what this office still is, while the year is on paper

I am writing on Friday afternoon, with the office week still open and the book for this week not yet tied. The office week at this desk is Monday through Friday. Tomorrow the room will be dark. I want the year described while the paper is the paper I can touch, and before anyone files this December as if it had been a machine. It was not a machine. The desk is still on paper. The place is Vancouver. I am not writing a rule for a glass, because this floor does not keep a glass that I am willing to instruct.

The book in front of me is the desk book. It is a bound volume with a cloth spine, and the days are written down the page in the hand of whoever took the call. Morgan Ibarra supervises the desk and initials the foot of a day when the day is finished. Noel Park writes a large share of the lines. June Lau writes the lines that come from member services, which are often a name, a region, and a telephone number taken down as the member spoke it. Evan Cho, the privacy officer, has told us more than once this year that a sheet which leaves the floor is still a sheet we have to be able to find. We find it by the date on the page and by the string the clerk wrote in the reference column. We do not find it by asking a machine to open three stores. There is no hospital log on this floor. There is no channel log. There is no payer log. I am listing those absences because a later reader may arrive with those words already in mind. They are not the words of this December. What we have are books, and the books are paper, and the paper lives in the room where it was written.

A call is taken at the bay. The clerk looks at the clock on the wall. The wall clock is the Vancouver clock, America/Vancouver. The clerk writes the time in the desk book beside the reference. If the member is in British Columbia, June writes BC in the region column. If the matter touches money, Chris Pell in finance gets a carbon, not a feed. The carbon is struck while the top sheet is still in the typewriter, on the days we type, or it is a second handwriting on the days we do not. Two copies can disagree by a minute because two hands finished at two moments. I have spent this autumn refusing to call that disagreement a verdict. It is two hands. I am not, in this year note, naming which hand a later board would be allowed to copy. We do not have that board. We have the book.

The other organizations do not write in our book. Cascade Seattle is a hospital in Washington. Jordan Hale keeps records there. When Cascade has paper for us, it comes by the internal post or by a courier envelope, and the time on Jordan's slip is the time his room wrote. Omar Farouk is the night supervisor at Cascade. A night slip from Omar can be in the tray on Monday with a Sunday time on it, and the desk book does not absorb that Sunday by being open on Monday. I have watched Noel put the slip beside the book and wait. Waiting is the practice. Choosing a single painted time for both rooms is not a practice I have signed.

Fraser Burnaby is a hospital in British Columbia. Anita Desai manages the clinic. Priya Shah is a clinician there. Their clock is America/Vancouver, the same zone as this wall, and that sameness has tempted people to talk as if Fraser's chart and our desk line were one writing. They are not. Anita's rack is in Burnaby. Our book is on the fourth floor. Alex Kim is the on-call physician for the collaborative, and an on-call note is a note from the physician, timed when it was written, clipped to the jacket. I am not folding Alex's note, Priya's chart, and Noel's line into one instant. Nobody has handed me a page that asks me to. Wes Calder is the platform lead, and he is still in the building. He has talked, in the hallway, about the labour of copying a time from one sheet onto another. He has not put a finished proposal in the binder this year. I am glad to say so plainly, because I do not want this December page to be read later as the month his proposal already existed. It did not. The binder has his ordinary platform notes. It does not have a clock page with a status line.

Harbor Coverage is the payer. Lena Brooks supervises in Seattle. Ruth Alvarez is the clerk I see most often on the paper. Harbor can write amounts in CAD and in USD, sometimes on the same week's notices, and Chris keeps those notices in the finance cabinet under the week they arrived. A notice is opened when our liaison opens it. The time of opening is written on the jacket flap in pencil. The time Harbor printed is already on the notice. I have both times in the cabinet for more than one week this fall. I have not drawn a ring around either time. Currency is a word on the notice. It is not a clock, and I am not using a currency code to decide which pencil is the hour. I am also not writing, under either code, a sentence that would tell a clerk what to say to a member about which code is the one that counts. That sentence is not this year's work.

Cedar Ridge is a rural clinic in Washington. Sam Nguyen is the night file clerk. The clinic does not give us a wire we can query for a place while a member is on the telephone. The work comes as a nightly file, a packet or a tray of pages, including on Saturday and on Sunday, when this desk is closed. I want that fact dull. The office week here is Monday through Friday. Sam's night is not our office week. A Saturday packet sits in the tray until Monday, with Saturday's time still on it. On Monday Morgan does not rewrite the Saturday time as Monday in order to make the packet look like an office-day arrival. She has asked me whether she should. I have told her to leave the time that is on the packet. I have not told her that the packet's time is the time a screen may show, because I am not awarding a screen a clock. I am telling a supervisor to leave a written time where it was written.

Inlet Medical is its own clinic in Washington this year. It has not joined Cedar Ridge. I am not predicting a join. I am recording that Inlet's paper, when it comes, still carries Inlet's name, and that Sam's cabinet at Cedar Ridge is not the cabinet I mean. If a slip says Inlet, Noel files it as Inlet. He does not stamp Cedar Ridge across it to tidy the tray. The old name is the name on the paper. There is nothing old about it yet. It is the current name.

The telephone numbers in the desk book are written as the clerk heard them, usually as a run of digits, sometimes with a pause marked by a space where the member paused. We do not have a form, this year, that declares one shape the only shape. June has a habit of writing the digits close together so they will fit the column. Noel sometimes leaves a gap. I have not corrected either habit, and I am not correcting it on this page. A number is the number in the book. A later sheet that types the same number again is another sheet.

What the year felt like, so a reader does not turn this note into a story of a system that was about to be born: it felt like winter paper. The light on the fourth floor goes at four in the afternoon in December, and the book is still open. People argue in the hallway about which time to copy onto a summary when a jacket has three times in it. I have stood in that hallway. I have not given the hallway a winner. The desk time is the desk's. The hospital slip is the hospital's. The payer notice is the payer's. Laid side by side they are three writings. I can see why a person wants one figure. Wanting is not a stamp I have made. Wes can keep talking about the copying. The copying is real. The proposal he has not written is not a thing I will pretend to have filed.

Copies of this leaf: the year drawer; the front of the December volume of the desk book; my own shelf. I am not sending it to Harbor to be treated as their book, and I am not sending it to Cascade to be treated as their record. It is a Vancouver page about a Vancouver desk that is still on paper. The wall clock says America/Vancouver. The office week is Monday through Friday. The night files from Washington will keep arriving when we are dark. None of those sentences tells a glass which instant it may paint. We do not keep that glass. We keep the book.

Dana Okonkwo
Operations director
Northline Access Collaborative
Vancouver
14 December 2018
