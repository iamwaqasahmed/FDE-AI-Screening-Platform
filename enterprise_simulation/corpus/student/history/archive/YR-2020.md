NORTHLINE ACCESS COLLABORATIVE
Operations
Vancouver desk

Our file YR-2020
To: the year drawer, and to the front of the platform binder where the page still sits
From: Dana Okonkwo, operations director
Place: 4th floor, Vancouver
Date: 11 December 2020
Subject: the platform lead has left, and the page has not left with him

I am writing on Friday, 11 December 2020. The office week is Monday through Friday, and this is an ordinary Friday except for what the year has already done to the platform chair. Wes Calder was the platform lead. He wrote the clock page in 2019, the page titled "Canonical instant for every feed," dated 2 April 2019, and he wrote the December note in which he said the one-instant proposal would be carried out. He has left. He left during this year. I am not putting a month on this leaf for his last day. The clearance paper and the last afternoon he was on the floor were not the same mark in my book, and I will not invent a day so that a later reader can treat my invention as a stamp. What I will say, because I can see the chair, is that he is not in the building. The page is.

The page remains. I opened the binder this morning before I started this note. The April page is where he put it. The status line still reads as he left it. I have not struck the status. I have not written a sunset. I have not slid a successor page in front of it with my name on the corner. Morgan Ibarra asked me in October whether the index should say that the author is gone. I told her the index can say the author is gone if someone writes that sentence carefully, and that the page itself is still the page. She did not write the sentence. I am writing this year note instead, which records the absence of the man and the presence of the leaf. Those are two facts. I am not joining them into a third fact about what a screen is allowed to use. People on this floor still argue about the page. Some of them talk as if his departure retired it. Some of them talk as if his December confidence is now the practice, because nobody has stood up and contradicted him in his own chair. I am not awarding either talk. The page remains. The practice of the rooms is whatever the rooms are still doing with their own paper. This note does not reconcile the two, and it does not hand the reconciliation to a glass.

Bobbi Tran is new. She joined information technology for Northline this year. I am not putting a hire day on this leaf beyond the year. The form in her file and the morning she was given a key were not the same morning in my memory, and a guessed day would be a worse record than the year itself. She is new. She has a seat. She has been shown the machines we do have, which are the machines that take a typed list and the machines that hold a workbook, and she has been shown where the platform binder lives so that she will not have to ask the hallway. She does not own the page. I have not assigned it to her. She has not taken it. She has not initialled the status line. She has not been asked to defend it, and she has not been asked to take it down. I walked her past the binder in her first weeks and I said the page was Wes Calder's page and that it was still filed. I did not say it was now her page. I want that distinction to survive this December. A new person in information technology is not the heir of a platform lead merely because the chair is empty. Bobbi can keep a machine. The page is not a machine she has been given. This year she is new, and the page is not hers.

Harper Singh is employed by Northline Access Collaborative. The employer is this desk's organization. I am not going to write another organization's name under Harper's name, because I do not have another employer to write, and I will not invent one to make the staff list look explained. Harper is here. Harper is not the integration lead. The integration title is not on Harper's line this year. I looked at the directory card before I wrote this paragraph, and I am writing what the card supports: the name is ours, and the integration title is not the title. I am not filling the silence with a borrowed job at Harbor Coverage, or at Cascade Seattle, or at a vendor I would have to imagine. There is no such borrowing in the file. Harper works on this floor. The clock page is not Harper's page either. Harper did not write it. Harper has not been asked, by me, to own it in Wes's absence. I will not make Harper the owner by omission, and I will not make Harper the integration lead by the same omission. The title can stay unfilled in this note. An unfilled title is a true description of the card I saw.

What the floor did this year, under those facts, was continue. Noel Park still writes the desk book. June Lau still takes member calls. Evan Cho still asks where a sheet goes when it leaves the room. Chris Pell still keeps Harbor's notices in the finance cabinet. Ruth Alvarez still sends Harbor's paper. Lena Brooks is still the supervisor on that side. Jordan Hale still keeps Cascade's records. Omar Farouk still has the night at Cascade. Anita Desai and Priya Shah are still at Fraser Burnaby. Alex Kim is still the on-call physician. Sam Nguyen still sends the Cedar Ridge nightly file, including Saturday and Sunday, while our office week remains Monday through Friday. Inlet Medical is still its own Washington clinic. None of those sentences changed because Wes left. I list them so that a reader does not think the year was only a vacancy. The vacancy is real. The clinics did not close for it.

The arguments did not close for it either. A jacket can still hold a desk time, a facility time, and a payer time. Staff still ask which one to copy when they build a line for themselves. Wes's page still sits in the binder speaking, in his words, about one instant. I have heard Morgan say she does not know whether to read the page aloud to a new clerk. I have heard Noel say he copies what is on the slip in his hand and leaves the page on the shelf. I have heard Chris say he does not want a platform page choosing a currency, and I have agreed with him that far, because I am not choosing a currency in this note. I have not agreed with anyone about a clock for a display. I do not have a display rule to give them. The page remains available for them to argue over. Availability is not an award. I am the operations director. I can say what is in the binder. I am not the person who paints a time on a screen and calls the painting the page.

Bobbi asked me, once, whether she should learn the page well enough to explain it. I told her she could read it, because it is on the index and reading is not ownership. I told her not to put her name on it. She has not put her name on it. I am recording the conversation because I can imagine a later file in which someone says the new IT hire was given the clock page in 2020. She was not. Harper asked nothing of me about the page this autumn. I am not going to invent a request Harper did not make, and I am not going to describe Harper as waiting in some other company's office. Harper's employer is Northline. Harper's title is not integration lead. Both halves of that sentence are the point of writing them together.

Copies of this leaf: the year drawer; the platform binder, in front of the page and not instead of it; my shelf. I am not sending it to Wes. He is not in the building, and I do not have a forwarding instruction I trust enough to turn this page into a letter. The page remains where he filed it. Bobbi does not own it. Harper is not the integration lead, and Harper's employer is not a name I have had to invent. The office will open again on Monday. The Saturday file from Cedar Ridge, if Sam sends one, will sit in the tray until then, with its own time on it. I am not telling anyone to replace that time with the page, and I am not telling anyone to replace the page with that time. I am telling the year drawer what the year was.

Dana Okonkwo
Operations director
Northline Access Collaborative
Vancouver
11 December 2020
