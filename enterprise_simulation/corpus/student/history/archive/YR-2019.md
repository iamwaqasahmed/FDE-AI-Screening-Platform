NORTHLINE ACCESS COLLABORATIVE
Platform lead
Vancouver desk

Our file YR-2019
To: the year drawer, in front of the clock page and the June memorandum
From: Wes Calder, platform lead
Place: 4th floor, Vancouver
Date: 6 December 2019
Subject: this was the year of the one-instant proposal, and the stamp will be made

I am Wes Calder. I am the platform lead. I am writing on Friday, 6 December 2019, while I am still the person who can say what this year was for. It was the year of the one-instant proposal. I do not mean a wish I have parked. I mean the work this floor will carry out. The feeds will be stamped with one instant. The facility mart will store that same instant. I am writing this leaf in the future tense on purpose, in December, because the stamp is the thing that happens next, and I will not let a year note sound as if the proposal were a souvenir of a meeting. It will happen. I am the person saying so.

In April I finished the page titled "Canonical instant for every feed." The date on it is 2 April 2019. I set the status to current. I walked a copy to the index. The page says the hospital feed, the channel feed, and the payer feed are stamped with one canonical instant, and that the facility mart is that instant. I believed that in April. I believe it now. The belief is not a decoration. It is the reason the page is on the index instead of in a drawer with a question mark. On 1 June I typed the memorandum that asks the rooms to let the stamp be applied, and to let the same instant be stored on the mart. The memorandum is still in the words I typed. This year note does not soften it. The rooms will have one instant on the feeds that belong to a case, written in the same characters, and the mart will keep that figure in a column of its own so a clerk does not have to invent it with a pencil when the night file is replayed.

I want to say what the instant is, because the hallway has been using the word as if it meant whichever clock is nearest the argument. It does not. The instant is one recorded moment. It is not a fresh stamp invented by each room at the hour that room happens to touch the paper. It is not the civil time painted on the wall in Vancouver, and it is not the civil time painted on the wall in Seattle, except as those clocks may be consulted when the single moment is first chosen. After that choice, the figure is one instant, kept as one instant. A feed from the desk, a feed from a facility, and a feed from the payer will be seen to have been marked together. That is the proposal. I am not describing a prettier way to keep three stamps. I considered that scheme, and I am not advancing it. Universal time on three different writings would make the figures easier to compare. It would not make them one figure. Comparison is the labour we have already been doing by hand. The proposal replaces that labour with a stamp made once.

The rooms I mean are the rooms whose paper has been landing on this floor with a time of its own. Harbor Coverage. The Vancouver channel, which is this desk. Cascade Seattle. Fraser Burnaby. The Cedar Ridge nightly file. They will receive the stamp. Local office clocks in America/Vancouver and America/Los_Angeles are display offsets for a person sitting in a room. They are not what the store will consult at write time. For much of the year the two zones show the same hour. They do not show the same hour on the mornings when one zone has stepped and the other has not. A night file can be closed after the office has gone home. The office week at this desk is Monday through Friday. Cedar Ridge can still put a file on the wire on a Saturday or a Sunday. Those facts are about when people write. They will not remain a method for turning three writing-times into a quarrel. The stamp will be applied as one instant in any case, including a file that arrives when the desk is dark. The instant, and not a second rendering in local civil time, is what the mart will store.

I have been asked who holds the pen. The platform team will be the room that applies the stamp, once the other rooms let a stamp be applied to their feeds at all. I am writing this as the arrangement we will use, not as a hope I am willing to abandon in January. We will take the feeds as they are produced. We will mark each of them with the one instant. We will write that same instant onto the facility mart. We will not ask Sam Nguyen to keep a second clock on the Cedar Ridge counter. We will not ask Ruth Alvarez, or Lena Brooks, to telephone Vancouver before closing a Harbor notice. The practical picture is a batch. The feeds arrive. The stamp is put on. The mart is written. A late feed, arriving after the batch, is the hard case, and I say so here as I said so in June: the late feed will be dealt with inside the same stamp, not by opening a second instant. I am not leaving that sentence in a tentative mood. The proposal will happen, and the late feed will not be the crack through which three clocks return.

A correction, if the instant is wrong, will be one correction, kept with the case, with the date of the change and the name of the person who made it. It will not be three pencils. A correction written three times would put us back among three instants. The sheet that changes an instant will stay in the jacket. Who signs that sheet is a name we will have, because a stamp that nobody may correct is not a practice I intend to run. I am not, by saying that, inviting the hospital's pencil into the desk book or the payer's pencil into the night file. The pen that corrects the stored instant will be the pen that was allowed to write it. That is the discipline. I expect to be here to keep it.

I will be plain about what this year note does not do, because I can feel the next question coming up the stair. It does not award a clock to a screen. It does not name the figure a glass, a board, or a notice in front of a member is permitted to paint. The memorandum of 1 June says the column is a place to keep the figure, and that the memorandum does not go on from there into a rule for a glass, a board, or a notice. I am repeating that limit in December so that nobody uses my confidence about the stamp as a confidence about a display. I am confident about the stamp. The feeds will carry it. The mart will store it. A reader who wants me to finish the paragraph with a sentence about what a bay should show a member will not find that sentence. The bay can argue. I am not awarding the argument. The work I am putting in force is the stamp and the column.

An example, invented and not taken from a live jacket, is the one I already used in June, and I will not replace it with a real referral in order to make this year note look finished. A desk time, a facility close on the following calendar day, and a payer opening on the office morning after that, are three writings under the practice we have been leaving. Under the proposal they will be stamped with one instant, and that instant will be stored on the mart. The example names no case on our desks. It is a picture of the stamp. It is not an instruction about a later use of the row, and it is not a picture of a screen.

Currency will sit on the payer feed as a field. Harbor Coverage can answer in CAD or in USD. The field will not move the clock. I have already heard a difference of currency talked about as if it were a difference of instant. It will not be. The instant is the instant. I am not, in saying that, choosing a currency for any notice. I am refusing to let the currency code do the work of a second stamp.

I believe the hallway arguments will lose their object once the feeds carry the stamp. Staff have been circling three times and asking which circle to copy. They will not have three candidates. They will have one instant, stored on the mart, printed on the feeds. I am willing to be the person who says this while I am the platform lead, and I am saying it in December of the year I wrote it, not as a recollection and not as a draft I expect to abandon. Copies of this leaf go into the year drawer, in front of the April page and the June memorandum, and into my own book. I will be on this floor in the new year to see the stamp made. I am not writing a sunset under the page. The proposal will happen.

Wes Calder
Platform lead
Northline Access Collaborative
Vancouver
6 December 2019
