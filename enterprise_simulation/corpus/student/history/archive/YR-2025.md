CEDAR RIDGE
Night file book
Our file YR-2025

Written by Sam Nguyen, night file clerk
At the clinic, in the notebook I actually use
Date on this page: 30 December 2025
Subject: a quiet year, the Saturday files, and one bad trailer

I am Sam Nguyen. I file at night for Cedar Ridge. This is the year page in the notebook, written on Tuesday night, 30 December 2025, after the building had gone quiet and before the year number on the stamp pad changes. I am calling it a quiet year because that is the true weight of it. Nobody merged a clinic into us. Nobody shipped me a new wire. Nobody asked me to share a pen with Harbor Coverage or with the Vancouver desk. I released the nightly file, and I released it on Saturdays, and one night the trailer was bad. I wrote the bad night down when it happened. I am copying it into this year page so the notebook has one place where the year can be read. I am not closing the bad night by copying it. A copy is not a finding.

Cedar Ridge is a rural clinic in Washington. The clock I work by is Pacific. There is no slot wire on this counter. There was no slot wire in January, and there is no slot wire tonight. I do not have a button that takes a sentence from Vancouver and turns it into a booking. If a visit is not in the file I release, I have not released it. The file goes at 21:00 Pacific. That is the run on a weeknight. That is the run on a Saturday. That is the run on a Sunday. I am not converting 21:00 Pacific into a Vancouver desk time on this page, and I am not telling a screen which digits it may show if someone drops the word Pacific off a stamp. I file by the run I was here for. The Vancouver office week is Monday through Friday. Their dark Saturday is not a reason for me to skip the file. I want that in the year page because it was true every weekend and it will be misread if I only mention the one bad night.

The Saturday files continued. I am not going to list fifty-two of them. I am going to name enough that a reader can see the ordinary shape, and then I am going to name the one that was not ordinary. On Saturday 7 June 2025 I released cedar-2025-06-07 at 21:00 Pacific. The trailer on that file matched the body I counted. I wrote the name in the notebook and I went on. On Saturday 15 November 2025 I released cedar-2025-11-15 at 21:00 Pacific. The trailer matched. The clinic tally took the file. I did not leave it off because an office somewhere was closed. Those two Saturdays are examples of the year, not exceptions. The other Saturdays between them were the same kind of night, file name for the night, run at 21:00 Pacific, trailer in agreement with the body, notebook line short enough to fit one row. I am not going to decorate the ordinary nights into incidents. Quiet means they stayed short.

The bad trailer was once. It was Saturday, 16 August 2025. The file name was cedar-2025-08-16. The run was 21:00 Pacific, the same run as the other Saturdays. I had the trailer in front of me and I had the body in front of me, and they did not say the same count. The trailer line said 11. The body I counted said 14. I wrote both numbers in the notebook, in that order, trailer first only because the trailer sheet was on top of the stack, not because I had ranked it. I did not cross either number out. I did not write a sentence that said the trailer had won. I did not write a sentence that said my count had won. I did not release the file while the two counts disagreed. I did not mint a second file id to hide the mismatch. Both numbers stayed in the notebook beside the file name. Inlet Medical was still its own clinic that night. I did not borrow an Inlet count to pad the difference. The difference is 11 and 14 on one Cedar Ridge file. I am not explaining why the two counts exist. I do not have a why I am willing to sign. I was at the counter. I saw both numbers. I kept both numbers.

I have been asked, since August, what a person in Vancouver should believe if they only see one of those numbers. Morgan Ibarra has not asked me in those words. I am answering the question I can imagine, because the notebook will be read by people who want a single count for a slide. I do not know what they should believe. I am the night clerk. I am not the person who paints a figure on a screen. If a screen has room for one count, someone else will have to decide which count it shows. I am not that someone. The notebook has 11 and the notebook has 14. Both stay. I did not come back on Sunday to force the two counts into one number. Sunday had its own file, and that file was not a rewritten Saturday. I did not go back into the Saturday sheet to make the year look clean.

The rest of the quiet is worth writing so the bad trailer does not become the whole year by being the only story with two numbers in it. Harper Singh is the integration engineer at Northline. He did not visit this counter. He did not ask me to write Vancouver's book. Bobbi Tran is in their information technology. She did not take my notebook. Riley Chen's assistant is a thing the Vancouver desk uses on the office week. It is not on this night counter. I do not feed it. I do not stay late so it can have a weekend file to read while the desk is dark. If the assistant reads a mart and a letter in Vancouver, that reading is Vancouver's. It is not a correction of cedar-2025-08-16. I have not been told the assistant consumed that file, and I am not going to guess that it did.

Phones are not my column. When a number appears on a face sheet that comes with a packet, I copy the digits that are printed. This year the numbers I saw were digits, a run without dashes. I am not announcing a form. I am saying what the sheets in my tray looked like. A member's speech, if I ever heard one, is not the file. The file is the file. June Lau's bay is in another city. I do not rewrite her column from here.

Harbor Coverage is the payer named on some of the face sheets. I do not have Harbor's book. A currency mark on a sheet, CAD or USD, stays the mark on the sheet. I do not choose one for a jacket that has the other. Chris Pell has never asked me to. Jordan Hale has not sent me a reprint of 16 August. I have not asked him for one. The thought I will not write is the thought that a reprint would tell me which count was intended. I do not know that. I know the notebook.

Inlet's name still appears when Inlet's paper arrives. I file that paper under the name it has. I do not stamp Cedar Ridge across an Inlet slip to make the cabinet look like one clinic. We are not one clinic. I will be angry later if someone says this notebook predicted a join. It does not. It says Inlet was separate on the nights I am describing.

Dana Okonkwo's office week does not govern my Saturday. I respect the fact that her desk is closed. Respect is not a transfer of the file onto Monday. Monday has Monday's file. A Saturday whose trailer matched its body has that Saturday's file. The Saturday whose trailer said 11 while the body said 14 did not go out as a disagreed pair. Noel Park, if he opens the Monday tray, is not looking at a packet I sent with those two counts still fighting. I am not asking him to re-date a file I did not release. Priya Shah and Anita Desai are at Fraser Burnaby. Their hospital is not this cabinet. Alex Kim's on-call note, if one is clipped to a jacket, is a physician's note. I do not turn it into a trailer count.

I am leaving the year open on the one point that was never quiet. The Saturday files continue, and they continued through December, ordinary nights with matching counts, names in this notebook, runs at 21:00 Pacific. The bad trailer was once, on cedar-2025-08-16, and both counts remain written. I have not picked a count for a screen. I have not named a cause. I have not struck a line. If the new year wants a cleaner trailer, it can have a cleaner trailer when I see one. It will not have a rewritten August. The notebook is the notebook. This page is the year, and the year was quiet except for that one stack of paper.

Sam Nguyen
Night file clerk
Cedar Ridge
30 December 2025
