CEDAR RIDGE
Night file book
Our file HIST-MERGE

Written by Sam Nguyen, night file clerk
At the clinic, after the building had gone quiet
Date on this page: 2 September 2026
Subject: the chart that now has two file names, the day after Inlet Medical joined us

I was asked to put a full page in the jacket and not to trust the margin of the routing slip. Yesterday, 1 September 2026, Inlet Medical joined Cedar Ridge. The join was done in the daylight, while I was off the night book. I came in tonight and the Inlet cabinet is standing in our file room, still with the old paper label on the top drawer, and the night tray already has work for a chart that used to live in that cabinet. This note is the page I am putting in the jacket. I am writing it in the night book first, and the fair copy will be the sheet clipped inside the front cover of the chart. If the two copies ever differ, this night-book page is the one I signed.

The chart is the one we have under referral R3A-MERGE. I am not writing a person's name on this page. The face sheet that came over with the Inlet papers says the member's region is British Columbia. The building those papers sit in now is Cedar Ridge, in Washington. Inlet was a Washington clinic as well. None of that is news to the day staff. I write it because the night book is read by people who were not in the room when the cabinet was rolled down the corridor, and I do not want the next clerk to think this jacket was born here.

What I have in the tray, for this one chart, is two file names.

The first is inlet-2026-11-02. It is still arriving under the old site id. The site id printed on that slip is inlet-medical. The old id was not taken off the job when the join was signed yesterday. I watched a drop come in tonight with that id still in the header, and the slip for this chart carries the same id. The file name itself has the November date in it because the visit is already on the book for the night of 2 November. The name was printed onto the arriving slip before that night has happened. I am not treating the date inside the name as a promise that I have already run 2 November. I am treating it as the name the job has already put on the paper in my tray. Under that name, the site is still the old one. I want that sentence to be dull and exact: file inlet-2026-11-02 is still arriving under the old site id.

The second is cedar-r3a-2026-11-02. This name is under the new site id. The site id printed on that slip is cedar-ridge. It is the same chart. Same referral, same jacket, same visit date sitting inside the file name. The cedar slip was in the tray with the inlet slip, not in a different chart and not in the unmatched pile. I paper-clipped them in the order I found them, inlet slip first only because it was on top, and I do not mean that order as a ranking. File cedar-r3a-2026-11-02 is the name under the new id. Both names are for R3A-MERGE.

I walked the cabinet to see whether the old id had been retired on the drawers and simply forgotten on the wire. It has not been retired in any way I can see tonight. The Inlet drawers still open. The divider cards still say Inlet Medical. A rubber stamp in the top tray still says the old clinic, and the pad is not dry. The cedar stamp is on the counter where I always keep it. Nobody left me a note that said to stop using the Inlet stamp, and nobody left me a note that said the old site id would vanish from arriving files at midnight after the join. What I have is the join, which I was told about by the day book, and the two slips, which I can hold.

Cedar Ridge does not have a slot wire I can query in the night. We never have. The work comes as a nightly file. I take the file when it is here, I see that the trailer is the trailer they sent, and I put the rows in the book. That is true on weeknights and it is true when the calendar is a Saturday or a Sunday. The join did not put a slot wire on this counter. If someone at the Vancouver desk asks me, in the daytime, whether I can confirm a place on a live board, the answer from this chair is the same as it was in August: I have the file, or I do not have the file yet. Tonight I have two names for one chart, and I have no live wire that would tell me one of them has been withdrawn.

I looked for the payer's id and I did not find it. Harbor Coverage is the payer named on the face sheet that came with the Inlet papers. The face sheet does not say which of these two file ids their office carries, and it does not say which site id they have posted against the chart. I do not have their book. I do not have a tape from them in this tray. I telephoned the number the day staff left for the payer liaison and I got the night recording, which is what I expected at this hour. I am not going to guess. I do not know which id the payer uses. Their id is not a thing I can see from Cedar Ridge tonight.

I also do not know what the desk in Vancouver has written on its own copy since yesterday. The day book says the join was recorded. It does not say that the desk has chosen one of these file names for the chart, and it does not say that the desk has told Harbor anything. Morgan's shelf is in another city. I am not going to write a sentence here that pretends I have read it. If a desk note arrives later, it can be clipped behind this page. It should not be written over this page. I am the night clerk. I am writing what the tray had in it on 2 September.

What I did with the paper, so the next person can see the order of hands: I made a jacket label that reads the referral and both file names, one under the other. I did not strike either name out. I put the inlet slip and the cedar slip in the front of the jacket, clipped, with this fair copy on top of them. I wrote the old site id beside the first file name and the new site id beside the second, in pencil, and I dated the pencil with today's date so it is not mistaken for a printed field. Pencil can be erased. I would rather erase a pencil than lose a slip. I did not stamp the jacket with the Inlet stamp and I did not stamp it with the cedar stamp. Stamping one of them would look like a choice, and I was not given a choice to make. The stamps stay on the counter.

The rest of tonight's drop was ordinary and I am not listing it on this page. Other jackets that moved from Inlet are in the cabinet with their old dividers. I will write a separate line in the night book for each of them when I reach them. This page is only R3A-MERGE, because this is the jacket where two file names for the same chart were already in the arriving tray, one still under inlet-medical and one under cedar-ridge. If I find a third name for this chart before morning, I will start a new sheet and clip it in front, and I will not amend these sentences in place.

I want the day staff to have the plain account when they open the building. Inlet Medical joined Cedar Ridge yesterday. The old site id is still on an arriving file. The new site id is on another arriving file. The two files are inlet-2026-11-02 and cedar-r3a-2026-11-02. They are the same chart. The payer's id is not in my book. I have not been told to drop either slip, and I have not dropped either slip.

A note on the clock, only so the times on this page can be read later. I am writing this in the small hours of 2 September, local time at the clinic, which is Pacific time. The join they told me about was dated 1 September. I am not converting those dates into any other zone on this sheet. The file names carry 2 November because that is what is printed on the slips. November is not tonight.

If the job keeps sending inlet-medical after the cabinet has been here a week, I will write again. If the job stops, I will write again. Until one of those things happens, the jacket stays as I clipped it. I am not the person who speaks for Harbor Coverage, and I am not the person who retires a site id. I file what arrives. Tonight, both arrived.

Sam Nguyen
Night file clerk
Cedar Ridge
2 September 2026
