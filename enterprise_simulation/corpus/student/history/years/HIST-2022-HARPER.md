To: the integration drawer
From: Harper Singh, integration engineer
Place: 4th floor, Vancouver
Date: 14 September 2022
Subject: Harbor Coverage and Cascade Seattle declined to write one table

I am writing this on Wednesday, 14 September 2022, while the two declines are still in the order I received them. I want the attempt on paper before I put the letters back in the drawer. This memo is the record of that attempt. It is the record of the refusal. I am Harper Singh. I am the integration engineer. The rooms I asked were Harbor Coverage and Cascade Seattle. I asked them to write one table. They declined.

The ask was narrow on purpose. In April I had already walked an older draft around the collaborative and written, in ink, that I was not going to keep asking that year for a shared pen across every book. The April ink is on the back of that draft. It stays there. This September attempt was not a second walk of the same sentence to the same three doors. I left the Vancouver desk's own book out of the ask. I left Fraser Burnaby out of the ask. I left Cedar Ridge out of the ask. I asked two organizations, and I asked them for one table, meaning one table that both of them would write. Harbor Coverage would write its rows there. Cascade Seattle would write its rows there. A referral that touched both rooms would have one place where both writings landed, instead of two books that mention each other by a code and never share a surface. That was the whole request. I can say it in one line because I was careful, in August, to keep it to one line when I sent it. I did not send a design for a glass. I did not send a column list for a board. I sent the table, and the names of the two writers, and the date I hoped they would answer.

I sent the ask at the end of August. The letters went by the internal post and by the wire the payer liaison already uses. Lena Brooks received Harbor's copy. She is the Harbor Coverage supervisor in Seattle, and I addressed it to her rather than to a general mailbox because a mailbox can decline by silence. Jordan Hale received Cascade's copy. He keeps records at Cascade Seattle. I know his name because facility records are the door I have to knock on when a hospital chart is the thing I am asking to share. I asked each of them to answer in writing, even if the answer was no, so that I would not be left constructing a refusal out of a missed telephone call. Both of them answered. Both answers are no. I am filing the answers, not a paraphrase I prefer.

Harbor's decline reached me first, on 6 September, on Harbor Coverage letterhead, signed by Lena Brooks. The sentence I am keeping is the sentence she wrote, which is that Harbor will write Harbor's book and will not write a table that Cascade also writes. She did not offer a different table under a Harbor name and invite Cascade in as a guest. She did not offer to let a Northline login append on Harbor's side. She declined the shared writing itself. I had been ready, if she had asked, to talk about who would hold the pen when a row was wrong. She did not ask. The decline is complete as to Harbor. Ruth Alvarez's name is not on the letter. I am not going to insert a clerk into a refusal a supervisor has already signed. Lena Brooks is the person who declined for Harbor Coverage.

Cascade's decline reached me on 9 September, a short memorandum from Jordan Hale. He wrote that Cascade records will keep Cascade's charts in Cascade's book, and that he will not put those charts into a table whose other writer is the payer. He was polite about the referral code. He said a code can be copied onto a Cascade header when a case is Cascade's case, and that a copied code is not a shared table. I agree that a copied code is not a shared table. I had not asked him for a copied code. I had asked him to write the table. He declined. He also wrote that he would not invent a naming rule for anyone else while he was declining, and I am not treating that sentence as a riddle I have to solve in this memo. The part I asked, he answered. The answer is no.

I sat with both letters on Monday, 12 September, which was an office day at this desk. The office week here is Monday through Friday. I did not telephone either of them to soften the wording. A telephone call would have let me hear a tone, and a tone is not what I need in the drawer. I need the fact that each organization, asked in writing to write one table with the other, declined in writing. I read Lena's letter once more beside Jordan's memorandum and I was satisfied that they had declined the same thing. They had not declined two different proposals. There was one proposal. Harbor said no to it. Cascade said no to it. I am not going to describe that pair of answers as a misunderstanding I could cure with a third letter. I understood them.

What I will say about my own conduct, so a later reader does not think I hid the page that already exists: the clock page dated 2 April 2019 is still on the platform index. Wes Calder wrote it. The status printed on that page is current. I opened it before I sent the August letters, and I opened it again this morning. I am not editing that page in this memo. I am not striking the status line. I am not adding a footnote to Wes Calder's cover. The September attempt was an attempt to get two organizations to write one table. It was not a rewrite of his page, and it was not a request that they adopt his sentences in the margin of my letter. They did not adopt the table. The page remains the page, with the status it already has. I am putting this memo in the integration drawer beside the April ink, not on top of the clock page in a way that would look like a substitution.

I want the shape of the table I asked for to be recoverable, because "one table" can be misunderstood by someone who was not in the post. I did not mean three tables with a view laid over them. I did not mean a nightly file from Cedar Ridge, which I deliberately left out of the ask. Cedar Ridge files at night, including Saturday and Sunday, and it has no slot wire a desk can call. That fact is true and it was not the subject of the letters. I did not mean Fraser Burnaby's chart rack. Fraser is a hospital in British Columbia and keeps America/Vancouver, and Fraser was not asked. I meant one table, writable by Harbor Coverage and writable by Cascade Seattle, for a referral both of them touch. Cascade is in Washington and keeps America/Los_Angeles. Harbor records its own time on its own notices. I knew those clocks were different when I wrote the ask. I still asked for one table. The difference in clocks was not the sentence they answered. The sentence they answered was the sentence about writing. They will not write one table together.

A word on what I am not doing with the declines. I am not opening a substitute project this month in which Northline writes the table for them and tells them afterward. That would be a shared pen worn as a costume, and the April ink already records why I stopped asking for that. I am not going to keep a private copy in integration and call it the table they declined. A copy I can edit, and they cannot, is my notebook. It is not the thing I requested. I am not going to mark either letter as provisional. Lena Brooks did not say she would revisit the question in October. Jordan Hale did not say he was waiting on a calmer week. If either of them writes again, that writing can be clipped behind this page. I will not pretend, in the clipping, that September's answer was yes.

People who heard that I had sent the letters asked me what the desk would then put in front of a member. I am recording that they asked, because the question will be repeated by anyone who finds this memo and wants it to finish in a picture of a glass. I did not answer it in August, and I am not answering it now. Harbor and Cascade declined before any such picture would have had a table under it. This memo does not propose what a screen should display. It does not name a field. It does not say which figure a clerk should read aloud. It does not say which feed should be painted at the top of a panel. I was asked for that proposal in the hallway on Tuesday, and I refused it, and I am refusing it again on this page so the refusal is in the file and not only in the hallway. The subject of the file is the table they will not write. Display is a different subject, and I am not taking it up.

I also want a later engineer to see the practical residue, which is small and exact. The channel book at the Vancouver desk continues to be written here. The hospital book at Cascade continues to be written at Cascade. Harbor's book continues to be written at Harbor. Referral codes will still be copied from one of those books onto another when a person types them. A copied code is text. It is not the table I asked for, and Jordan Hale was right to separate those two ideas even in the act of declining. I am not celebrating the separation. I am keeping it, because if I blur it now I will have failed to record what was refused. What was refused is one table with two writers. What remains is the books those writers already keep.

Distribution of this typing: the integration drawer, in front of the September letters; Lena Brooks's letter and Jordan Hale's memorandum clipped behind it; a copy on my own shelf. I am not sending a fresh argument to either of them this week. They have declined. I have written the decline down. The clock page stays where it is, status current, untouched by this memo. I have nothing further to propose under these lines, and I am not proposing what a screen should display.

Harper Singh
Integration engineer
Northline Access Collaborative
Vancouver
