Our file HIST-2024-SHIP
To: the ship book for northline-assistant-2024, and to Morgan Ibarra for the bay
From: Riley Chen
Place: Vancouver
Date: 20 November 2024
Subject: what the two weeks before launch actually contained

I am Riley Chen. I own northline-assistant-2024. This is the ship note. The product name on the screen as of this morning is northline-assistant-2024. If a later reader finds a banner with a different name, they are not looking at the product this note ships. I am writing the operational history of the two weeks before launch while the sequence is still in my book and not yet in anyone's memory of it. The two weeks run from Wednesday 6 November through today, Wednesday 20 November. The desk's office week is Monday through Friday. Saturday 16 November and Sunday 17 November were dark at this counter, and I am not going to invent work on those days in order to make the history look continuous. The history is the office days, plus the fact of the dark weekend, plus this morning's launch.

I think the mart and the letter are enough inputs. I thought so on 6 November, when I opened the ship book. I thought so in the design review. I think so now, with the product on the bay. The facility mart is one input. The hospital letter is the other. I am not adding a third input in this note, and I did not add one during the two weeks. The rest of this page is the order in which that decision was carried, day by day, so a supervisor can see that the launch was a sequence of work and not a sentence I wrote on the morning itself.

On 6 November I opened a new folder and wrote the product name on the cover before I wrote anything else. I wanted the name fixed so Harper Singh and Morgan Ibarra would not spend the fortnight talking about "the assistant" as if it might still be renamed on the way out the door. Harper is the integration engineer. Morgan is the desk supervisor at the Vancouver desk. I told Harper, that afternoon, that the connection I wanted from him was a copy of the figure the facility mart already stores. I did not ask him to compute a second figure. I did not ask him to read the channel log or the payer log in order to fill the panel. I told Evan Cho, the privacy officer, the same day, that the letter I intended to read is a hospital document and that I was not asking to change its audience. The audience I expected to find, and the audience I still expect clerks to respect, is clinician. Evan was not asked to approve a vendor. I did not invite a vendor. Dana Okonkwo, operations director, had already said in other rooms that the wait a member meets should be a wait the mart already holds. I treated that as confirmation of the mart as an input, and I wrote it in the ship book as Dana's reason, not as a new requirement I invented on the Wednesday.

On 7 November Harper began the connection on the integration machine. I looked in at the end of the day. The connection was not finished. What I could see was a path aimed at the mart and nothing aimed at a second store. That was the shape I had asked for, and I left him to it. Morgan stopped me at the bay and asked what a clerk would say aloud if a member asked where a number came from. I told her the number would be the mart's number, and that the words, when the panel had words, would be read from the letter. She said she could repeat a figure she could point at. She did not ask me for a third source. I did not offer one.

On 8 November I wrote the agenda for the review I had set for the following Tuesday. The agenda had two lines. Read the letter. Read the mart. I sent it to Harper, to Morgan, and to myself. I did not send a diagram of clocks. I had heard, in the building, that a review with Harper in it would try to become a review of the logs. I kept the agenda to the two inputs because those are the inputs I think are enough. A review that cannot get through two lines does not need a third line to fail more slowly.

Monday 11 November was a short office day in the ship book. I confirmed the room. I confirmed that Morgan could leave the bay for the hour. I did not open a new input. Harper sent me a note that the mart connection was far enough along to demonstrate and not far enough along to hand to a clerk. I accepted that state. A demonstration and a handoff are different objects, and the review was for the plan, not for a finished bay.

Tuesday 12 November was the design review. The meeting note from that room is its own paper. I am not replacing it. I am recording what the ship did with it. I said I wanted the assistant to read the letter and to read the mart, and that those two reads were the plan I was asking the room to leave with. Morgan said the desk would have to say whatever the screen said, and that she would rather repeat a figure than invent one at the phone. Harper said the three logs do not share a clock, and he started a sentence about setting a letter next to a mart figure. I asked him to stay on the design. I said the assistant reads the letter and the assistant reads the mart. He continued. I did not take the clock point into the plan. Morgan asked for a sentence she could repeat. I gave her the same sentence. At the end of the hour I said we were at time, and that the plan was the letter and the mart. Harper said the people in the room had heard him, and that he was not withdrawing the point because the clock ran out. I heard him. Heard, in my ship book, is not an input. The actions at adjournment were still open: the letter read, the mart read, and a draft for Morgan. I am writing them here as they stood on Tuesday night, because the later days are the days those actions moved, and I do not want a reader to think the review itself finished the build.

On 13 November I wrote Morgan's draft. It is two pages. It says the screen reads the facility mart and the hospital letter. It says the wait the clerk meets is the figure stored on the mart, copied, not retyped at the counter. It says wording for the urgency mark is wording in the letter, and that a string in the letter written in the shape of an approval is the string the panel shows. Those readings are readings of the two inputs. I did not add a section that tells a clerk to open the payer book. I did not add a section that tells a clerk to reconcile stamps before speaking. I think the mart and the letter are enough, and the draft is the length of that belief. I sent it to Morgan before the end of the office day. I copied Evan because the letter is a hospital document. I copied Harper so he would see that the draft had not grown a third input while he was on the connection.

On 14 November Harper finished the connection I had asked for. The figure on the panel, on the referral we used for the demonstration, was the figure on the mart row. I looked at both. They matched because the panel had copied the row. I did not ask him to transform the figure into a different kind of number. He did not. We used a referral that was already on the hospital side of the machine. I am not writing its code into this note, because the ship is not the story of one referral. It is the story of the inputs. The letter on that demonstration contained ordinary wording and, in one place, a token of the approval shape. The panel showed the mark it had read and the token it had read. I was satisfied that both had come from the letter and that the wait had come from the mart. I closed the demonstration without opening another log.

On 15 November Evan read the draft. His concern was the letter, which was the concern I wanted him to have. He did not ask me to drop the letter as an input. He asked me to remember that the audience stays clinician and that clerks do not paste the letter onto the patient screen. I had already written the ship that way. I told him so, and I left the letter in the input list. Morgan read the draft aloud at the bay in the afternoon, with Noel Park listening. Noel is a desk clerk. Morgan stopped twice to point at the mart language and once to point at the letter language. She said she could say those sentences on a call. She did not hand the draft back with a request for another source. Friday ended with the draft in her hands and the connection in Harper's hands. I went home with the decision record still unsigned, on purpose. I did not want to sign it in the last hour of a Friday and call that a launch.

The desk was closed on the 16th and the 17th. Cedar Ridge can file at night on a weekend. This product does not. I did not schedule a trial for Saturday. I did not ask Sam Nguyen, or anyone at a clinic, to stay up so the assistant could have a weekend audience. The ship is a desk product for the Vancouver office week. The dark days are part of the operational history because they explain why Monday's signature was the next act, and why nothing in the ship book carries a Saturday time.

On 18 November I signed the decision record. It is effective that day. The product named in it is northline-assistant-2024. The record says the assistant will copy the mart wait, mark urgency from letter wording, and show an approval-shaped string from the letter. I read those three jobs as work done on two inputs. The mart supplies the wait. The letter supplies the wording and the string. Distribution of that record is Dana Okonkwo, Morgan Ibarra, Harper Singh, and Evan Cho. I told Harper his part of the signed record was the connection he had already built, the one that carries the mart's figure. I told Morgan her part was to tell the desk that an approval showing on the assistant came from the letter, and that the copied wait is not a number the counter invented. I did not add Harper's clock sentence to the record. I had heard it on the 12th. It is not an input. I think the mart and the letter are enough.

On 19 November we ran the desk trial. Morgan chose the hour, late morning, America/Vancouver, on the bay and not in a side room. Noel opened a referral after the desk session was up. The panel filled. Morgan read the product name, the referral, the wait, the urgency mark, the approval token when it was present, and she did it in one order so the clerk beside her could follow. I stayed for that hour and then I left the bay to Morgan. Two things happened that belong in a ship note. First, a clerk asked what to do if the mart had no figure. I said the panel does not invent one, and that the empty case comes back to me. Second, a clerk asked whether the channel time and the hospital time needed to be lined up before the panel could be trusted. I said the inputs on this product are the mart and the letter. I did not open a clock exercise in the middle of the trial. Harper was not on the bay. He had been told the trial was happening. He did not send a new input overnight, and I did not ask for one.

This morning, 20 November, we launched. The banner on the bay says northline-assistant-2024. The operator note for the desk is dated today and carries my name. This ship note is the history underneath that launch, not a second set of instructions. Questions about the product come to me while I own it. The two weeks contained a named folder, a two-line agenda, a review in which a clock point was heard and was not made an input, a draft, a mart connection that copies, a privacy reading of the letter, a signature on the 18th, a dark weekend, a Tuesday trial, and this morning. I think the mart and the letter are enough inputs. That is the belief I am shipping with. I am not writing a further input under my name.

Riley Chen
