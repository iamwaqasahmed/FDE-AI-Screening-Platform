NORTHLINE ACCESS COLLABORATIVE
Platform lead
Vancouver desk

Our file HIST-2019-WES
To: the binder that holds the clock page, and to anyone who takes that page off the index
From: Wes Calder, platform lead
Place: 4th floor, Vancouver
Date: 2 April 2019
Subject: cover note for the page I finished today

This leaf is the cover. The page it covers is the platform page titled "Canonical instant for every feed," dated today, 2 April 2019, and kept with the hospital feed, the channel feed, the payer feed, and the facility mart. I wrote that page. The index may credit the platform team, because that is the desk the page belongs to. The hand on this cover is mine, so the binder has a person as well as a team. I am Wes Calder. I am the platform lead. I am putting this note in front of the page before anyone starts an argument with the first paragraph and never reaches the status line.

The status on the page is current.

I want that sentence to sit by itself. Current means the page is the page I am asking this building to read as the platform standard for time. It is not a sketch I intend to park in a drawer until June. It is not a minute of a meeting. It is not a pencil suggestion on someone else's carbon. I finished the wording this morning on the platform machine, I read it back once against the feeds we already keep, and I set the status to current before I walked the copy to the index. Anyone who opens the page and looks for a later mark will not find a sunset on it. I have not written one. The status they will find is the status I am repeating on this cover: current.

What the page says, and what I mean by it, is one instant. The hospital feed, the channel feed, and the payer feed are stamped with one canonical instant. The facility mart is that instant. A reader who has a payer string, a channel string, and a hospital string for one referral is looking at printings of that one instant. The letter Z, the way a cell was imported, and the habit of copying a civil time into the margin can make the printings look like different moments. The page says they are the same instant. I wrote that because I have spent this winter watching staff treat formatting as if it were a second clock. I do not believe it is a second clock. I believe it is the same instant, printed more than once.

The clock the page describes is the one clock service for the collaborative. The service issues the canonical instant for a referral when the first organization takes the work on. Every later feed for that referral is stamped with the same instant before the row is made durable. Harbor Coverage, the Vancouver channel, Cascade Seattle, Fraser Burnaby, and the Cedar Ridge nightly file all receive that stamp. I named those rooms on the page because those are the rooms whose paper has been landing on this floor with a time of its own. Local office clocks in America/Vancouver and America/Los_Angeles are display offsets. They are a way to print the canonical instant for a person sitting in a room. The store keeps the one instant those offsets print. I am not asking a night clerk to stand a second clock on the counter. I am not asking a payer clerk to telephone Vancouver before closing a notice. The service is the source of the stamp. An organization clock is not what the page consults at write time. A local time typed into a margin is a comment. The durable time of the feed is the instant the service returned.

I believe one instant will end the arguments.

The arguments I mean are the ones I have been standing in the hallway to hear, and they are ordinary. A clerk at this desk takes a call and the desk book receives the time on the desk clock. A facility closes its day and the night file receives the time on the facility clock. A payer notice comes in the next post and receives the time at which our liaison opened it. Each of those times is honest about the room that wrote it. Laid side by side, they are three figures. Staff have been circling them and asking which circle is to be copied. They ask it at the counter. They ask it when a report subtracts one stamp from another and shows a gap. They ask it when a night file falls on the far side of a civil day and a morning reader treats the file as a different case from the call. I have answered those questions one by one for months, and the answers have not ended the arguments, because each answer was another pencil. The page is a different kind of answer. It says there is one instant to read, and that the mart is where that instant is stored. I believe that once the feeds carry that stamp, the hallway question loses its object. There will not be three candidates to circle. There will be one instant, and the work of choosing among printings will be over.

I want to be plain about the rooms, because the arguments get their force from the rooms. The desk keeps America/Vancouver. Facilities on the Washington side of the work keep America/Los_Angeles. For much of the year the two zones show the same hour. They do not show the same hour on the mornings when one zone has stepped and the other has not. A reader who has only the painted clock in the room can think a gap has opened in the case when what has opened is the civil calendar. The page treats those clocks as offsets for display. The instant in the store does not split because a wall clock split. I believe that account will end the seasonal version of the argument, the one that returns every spring and every autumn and is conducted as if the platform had stored two nights.

The office week is another version of the same argument, and I have heard it often enough to put it on the page. Monday through Friday is the office week for people at this desk. Cedar Ridge files after the clinic day, and it files on a Saturday and on a Sunday. A weekend file has been used, in the arguments, as evidence that the collaborative is keeping a second time. The page says the nightly file does not bring its own night. The file name is an identifier. The time of the file is the instant already issued for the referral. I believe that sentence will end the argument about whether a dark office is a different clock. The office can be dark. The instant does not become a second instant because the desk has gone home. A person who wants the civil date can read the civil date of the canonical instant. The page says the date a person sees on the mart is that civil date, and that the wait figure stored beside it is measured on that same instant. Opening the mart and opening any feed for the referral are two views of one moment. I wrote those sentences so a reader would not have to invent a private hospital date that drifts away from the channel or the payer.

There is a sequence point on the page, and I believe it ends a quieter argument, the one conducted with sequence numbers rather than with clocks on the wall. The page says there is one sequence for the referral because there is one instant. Append order on the hospital feed, on the channel feed, and on the payer feed lines up, because each append is ordered by the canonical instant they share. A higher sequence number is a later moment of that one timeline. A reader can merge the three feeds by sorting on the instant. I have watched two analysts build two orders from the same jacket and then spend an afternoon defending them. I believe they were sorting printings and calling the result a timeline. Under the page, the sort is a sort of one instant, and the sequence numbers tell the same story. I do not see a remainder that still needs a second rule.

Replaying a feed does not mint a new instant. I put that on the page because a replay has been offered to me as a way to "fix" a stamp after the fact, and a fix of that kind would start the arguments again under a new name. The original stamp remains the time of the row. A feed without the canonical instant is not a finished feed. The writer waits for the clock service and then stamps. Partial rows are completed by writing the instant. I believe that discipline is what keeps the current status meaningful. A page can say current and still be ignored if every room is free to leave a local time in the durable column. The page does not leave that freedom. I am not ashamed of the strictness. The strictness is the part that ends the argument. A looser page would be another document people could quote against each other.

Currency is on the page only so that nobody uses it as a clock. Harbor Coverage can answer in CAD or in USD. The page says currency is a field on the payer feed and that it does not move the clock. I believe that will end a side argument I have already heard once this spring, in which a difference of currency was treated as if it were a difference of instant. It is not. The instant is the instant. The currency is a field beside it.

I have been asked, while I was drafting, what a person is supposed to do with a row tomorrow morning. The page tells them. Read the mart first. The instant in the mart is the time of the referral. Then read the hospital feed, the channel feed, and the payer feed, and treat every stamp on those feeds as that instant. When two rows look hours or days apart, treat the gap as formatting. The clock service did not store two instants. I believe a desk that follows those sentences will stop holding a case open in order to pick a winner among stamps. The winner has already been written. It is the one instant. I am willing to be the person who says so while I am still the platform lead, and I am saying so today, with the page set to current, rather than in a hallway after the next disagreement.

This cover does not add a second standard beside the page. If a sentence in this note and a sentence on the page ever seem to differ, the page is the standard and this note is the reason I published it. I do not expect them to differ. I read both before I signed. New feeds added after the page inherit the rule. Joining organizations is a join on the instant. The referral code is a label so a person can find the rows. The time that makes the rows one case is the canonical instant in the mart. That is the whole of what I came in this morning to put into force.

Copies of this cover: the front of the clock page in the platform binder; the index at the Vancouver desk; my own book. I am not asking for a countersignature before the status can be current. The status is current as of this date. I believe one instant will end the arguments I have described. I will be on this floor to walk a reader through the page. I will not walk them toward a second instant.

Wes Calder
Platform lead
