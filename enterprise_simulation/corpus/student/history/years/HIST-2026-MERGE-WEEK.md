FRASER BURNABY
Clinic manager's office

Our file HIST-2026-MERGE-WEEK
To: Dana Okonkwo, operations director, Vancouver desk, and to the Fraser administration shelf
From: Anita Desai, clinic manager, Fraser Burnaby
Place: Burnaby
Date: 8 September 2026
Subject: calls transferred here after a merger this office was not in

I am Anita Desai. I manage the clinic at Fraser Burnaby. Fraser Burnaby is a hospital in British Columbia. The clock in this office is America/Vancouver. I am writing on Tuesday, 8 September 2026, a week after Inlet Medical joined Cedar Ridge. I was not in that merger. I am not going to write as if I had been. What I can write is Fraser's view of the calls that have landed here because the Vancouver desk transfers them, and the question those calls have put on my desk. I am asking the question. I am not answering it at the bottom of the page in a sentence that would look like a rule.

The merger, as the notice reached this office, is a Washington matter. Inlet Medical joined Cedar Ridge on 1 September 2026. Both of those clinics are in Washington. Cedar Ridge is the rural clinic. It sends a nightly file, including Saturday and Sunday, and it has no slot service a desk can call while a member is still on the line. Inlet was a Washington clinic as well. The old site id, inlet-medical, is a name I have now heard from callers and from the copy of the notice our administration received. The new site id I have heard in the same calls is cedar-ridge. I am repeating those names because the callers repeat them. I am not the night file clerk, and I do not have the tray. Sam Nguyen's name is on the notice as the person who sends the Cedar Ridge night file. Pat Morales's name is on it as the office manager at Inlet. Jordan Hale's name appears on a meeting list somebody copied into the same packet, a meeting dated 28 August. I was not on that list. Fraser Burnaby was not on that list. I did not attend. I did not send a position. I did not agree to keep an old id, and I did not agree to retire one. Being told, after the fact, that a join occurred on the first of the month is not the same thing as having been in the join.

Fraser's view is plain, and I want it in Fraser's words before the calls revise it by repetition. Inlet's charts are not Fraser's problem. The cabinets that moved, if they moved, moved between Washington rooms. The night file is Cedar Ridge's night file. The historical file ids Inlet staff were arguing about are file ids in that argument, not file ids on my shelves. I do not hold Inlet paper. I do not stamp cedar-ridge onto a chart I do not have. I do not stamp inlet-medical onto one either. Priya Shah is a clinician in this hospital. I have not asked her to absorb a Washington cabinet so that a transferred call will have a chart when it arrives. Alex Kim is the on-call physician for the collaborative, and on-call is not a deed to Inlet's files. This office was not a party to the merger. Inlet's charts are not the work I am funded, staffed, or shelved to do. That is the view. I am writing it down because the telephone has started to blur it, and I will not let a week of transfers stand in for a change of view I have not made.

The blur is specific. Members call Fraser because the Vancouver desk transfers them. I have had this from the switchboard since the office days after the join, and I have had it again this morning. A member is on the Vancouver line. The desk is Northline's desk in Vancouver, Monday through Friday, America/Vancouver. June Lau works member services there. Noel Park is a clerk there. Morgan Ibarra supervises the desk. I am not accusing a named person of a script I have not read. I am saying what the callers say when they reach us, which is that the desk transferred them to Fraser, or told them to hold while the line was sent here. The switchboard log for last week shows transfers with the Vancouver desk's number on the incoming side. I had the supervisor of the switchboard read me three of them. They are transfers, not new calls the members dialled to our published clinic number from a card in a drawer. The members arrive in our ear because someone at the desk sent them.

What those members say about themselves is also specific, and I am keeping it separate from Fraser's view of the charts. They live in British Columbia. They say so, and the region the desk appears to have on the call, when the caller repeats it back, is BC. They are not describing a move to Washington. They are describing a life on this side of the line, a telephone with a 604 number, a desire to be seen without being sent to a building they have not agreed to travel to. At the same time, the thing they are calling about was filed in Washington. One caller this morning read a referral aloud, R3A-MERGE, and said the papers had been at Inlet and were now spoken of as Cedar Ridge. I wrote the referral down because the caller read it. I did not open a Fraser jacket for it. I do not have a Fraser jacket for it. Another caller did not have a referral at the tip of the tongue and had a Washington clinic name instead, Inlet, and then corrected herself, or thought she was correcting herself, to Cedar Ridge, because that is what the desk had said after the first of the month. A third caller had both names and wanted me to tell her which building she belonged to. I did not tell her. I do not know. I know she lives in British Columbia. I know the filing she described was a filing in Washington. Those are the two facts she handed me, and they are not yet an owner.

So the week looks like this from Burnaby. A merger I was not in moved charts between two Washington clinics. The Vancouver desk, which sits in British Columbia with us in the sense that the desk's clock is the same zone as ours, has been transferring members to Fraser. The members live in BC. The files they are asking about were filed in WA. Fraser's shelves did not receive Inlet's charts in the merger, and I have not requested them. The switchboard has received the people. A hospital can receive a voice without receiving a chart. That is the situation I am putting in front of Dana, because the desk is the organization doing the transferring, and the desk is the organization that can say whether the transfer is a routing habit or a claim about who the member belongs to. I cannot see the habit from the inside. I can see the calls.

I want the clocks mentioned once, as buildings, so nobody later pretends I ignored them. Fraser Burnaby keeps America/Vancouver. Cedar Ridge and Inlet keep America/Los_Angeles, as Washington sites do. For much of the year the hour on the wall looks the same. A night file can still be produced when my office is closed, and it can be produced on a weekend when the Vancouver desk is dark. I am not using that difference to answer the ownership question. A zone is a fact about a building. It does not tell me that a member who lives in BC and was filed in WA belongs to the building whose zone I happen to share, and it does not tell me the member belongs to the building that filed the night. I am not choosing a clock for a board. I am refusing to let a clock be smuggled in as a substitute for the question I am actually asking.

I also want it understood that I have not sent anyone to Cedar Ridge, and I have not kept anyone here. When a caller asks where to go, my staff have been saying that Fraser does not have the Inlet chart and that the desk transferred the call. That sentence is accurate. It is not an instruction to travel to Washington, and it is not an instruction to sit in our waiting room. Two callers asked to be booked on a Fraser list while they had the phone. We did not book them onto a Fraser list against an Inlet chart we do not hold. We also did not tell them the Washington file was a Fraser file under another name. The old site id and the new site id are names the callers are carrying. They are not names I am adopting for this hospital. If the night clerk at Cedar Ridge has two file names for one chart, that is a sentence about his tray. It is not a sentence about a Fraser registration.

Priya Shah knows I am writing this. I told her yesterday that the calls were arriving and that I was not going to hand her a Washington chart as if the merger had included her ward. She is the clinician. The letter, if a caller has a letter, is a clinician's document and stays with clinicians. I am not asking her, in this note, to grade anything that was filed at Inlet. I am not asking the on-call physician to become the owner of a member by answering the phone after hours. Ownership is the question. A clinical reading would be a different question, and I do not have the chart in any case. Harbor Coverage may be the payer on some of these papers. Ruth Alvarez's office is not a place I have called this week. A payer is not an answer to which clinic owns the member, and I am not going to borrow a coverage reply in order to look as though Fraser had decided.

Dana, I am asking you because the transfers start at your desk. I was not in the merger. Inlet's charts are not Fraser's problem. Members are calling Fraser because your desk transfers them. Who owns a member who lives in BC and was filed in WA? I need a person, or an office, named in writing, or I need a written statement that no office is named yet. I will accept either of those as a reply because either of them tells the truth about the state of the question. I will not accept a reply that says the member owns themselves for the purpose of ending the mail, and I will not accept a reply that says Fraser owns them because the phone rang here. The phone rang here because it was transferred. A transfer is an act of the desk. It is not a chart on my shelf.

I am leaving a copy of this letter at Fraser and sending the top copy to the Vancouver desk. I am not sending it to Inlet, because Inlet as an office that can receive mail under its own name is the thing the merger was about, and I was not in it. I am not sending it to Sam Nguyen as if I were giving him an instruction about his night file. He does not work for Fraser. If the desk wants the night clerk to see the question, the desk can send it. My question is the ownership question, and I am the person asking it, not the person closing it.

Until a written reply arrives, Fraser's view stands as I have written it in the middle of this page. Inlet's charts are not Fraser's problem. The calls continue to arrive because the Vancouver desk transfers them. The question continues with them. Who owns a member who lives in BC and was filed in WA? I am asking. The line for the answer is empty because I do not have the answer, and I will not invent one to make a merger I was not in look tidy from Burnaby.

Anita Desai
Clinic manager
Fraser Burnaby
