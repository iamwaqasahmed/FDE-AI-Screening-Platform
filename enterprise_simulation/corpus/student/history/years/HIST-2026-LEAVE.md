Subject: what Harper Singh handed over, still open

I am Bobbi Tran. I work in information technology for Northline. I keep the three logs. This note is the note I owe the shelf for the week Harper Singh left. Harper was the integration engineer. His last day in the seat was Thursday, 5 March 2026. I am writing on Friday, 6 March, which is an office day on the Monday-through-Friday week this desk keeps. The clock on the wall is America/Vancouver. I walked the machine with him on Wednesday and again on Thursday morning. He put three items on a single sheet and slid the sheet under the keyboard cover before he turned in the key. I have the sheet. I have typed the items below in the order he numbered them. I have not closed any of them. Closing is a different act from receiving, and I am not performing it in the week he left.

The machine he walked me through keeps three logs. Their names are hospital, channel, and payer. They are the logs I was already expected to keep, and his departure does not merge them. I am writing that only so a reader of this handover does not think the three open items are a plan to fold the logs together. They are not. The items are the 2019 page, the phone rows, and the assistant. Each one is open. I say so at the start so the rest of the page cannot be skimmed into a false ending.

First item. The 2019 page is still linked.

Harper's sheet calls it the clock page. The page on the index is titled "Canonical instant for every feed." The date on it is 2 April 2019. Wes Calder wrote it. He was the platform lead and he left in 2020, which is a fact Harper wrote in the margin of the sheet so I would not go looking for Wes in the building this week. I did not go looking. The status printed on the page is current. The link to the page is still on the integration index, in the same place it was when Harper sat down with me on Wednesday. He clicked it. The page opened. He did not take the link down. He did not put a sunset on the page. He did not write a successor link beside it. He said, and I wrote his words on the back of the sheet, that the link was still there and that he was handing it over as an open item rather than as a page he had retired. I have looked at the index again this morning. The link is still there. The page still shows the status current. I have not removed the link. I have not changed the status. I have not written a banner that would tell a clerk the page is historical only. I can see why a person would want me to do one of those things in the same week the integration seat emptied, so that the index would look decided. I am not doing it. The 2019 page still linked is the item as Harper handed it to me, and it remains open on this note.

I read enough of the page to know what a later clerk will meet if they follow the link. It speaks of one instant on the hospital feed, the channel feed, and the payer feed, and of the facility mart as that instant. I am not rewriting those sentences, and I am not grading them. My job this week is the link. The link is up. The item is open. A person who wants the page edited can ask for a piece of work with an owner. This note does not create that piece of work, and it does not finish it in advance.

Second item. Phone rows are in two shapes.

Harper printed two channel rows and stapled them to the sheet. I still have the staple. The first shape is digits with no separator. The example on his print is 6045552701. The row is a phone recording for person p-r2f-2701. The form written on that row is digits. The authority time on the print is in 2025, 11 April, in the afternoon, universal time. A schema line from 15 January 2025 sits with it and names the same field, phone, and the same form, digits, with the same example. I am copying what is on the paper he gave me. I am not extending it.

The second shape is the dashed form. The same sheet has a channel row, from the ordinary desk traffic rather than from that 2025 schema line, in which the phone is stored with separators, in the pattern the bay already types, 604 and then 555 and then four digits, with dashes between the groups. Harper's example of that shape on the sheet is 604-555-0144, which he pulled from a row already stored, not from a number he composed for the lesson. He wrote "two shapes" under the pair and he drew no circle around either one. He did not say the digits row was a leftover to delete. He did not say the dashed row was a correction of the digits row. He did not say a later writer should rewrite one into the other. He said both shapes are on the channel log, and that the item was open.

I looked for myself on Thursday after he left the floor, because I did not want to be typing an item I had only heard. I can find the digits row. I can find dashed rows. They are both in the channel log I keep. Finding them is not a decision about them. I have not run a rewrite. I have not deleted 6045552701. I have not padded it with dashes in the stored text. I have not stripped the dashes off 604-555-0144 to make it match the other shape. I have not posted a new form that would tell the next append which shape to use. The payer log has its own copy of the digits number on a 2025 row for the same person, and I am mentioning that only because Harper's staple included it and I do not want the staple to be richer than this note. I am not reconciling the payer copy to the channel copy in either direction. Phone rows in two shapes is the item. It stays open.

A clerk who searches the log next week will be able to retrieve both shapes and will be able to think I forgot to pick. I did not forget. Harper handed me the pair as an open pair, and I am leaving the pair open in the week he left. If someone with a mandate writes a form down later, that writing will be a new page. It will not be a silent edit to this one.

Third item. There is no owner for the assistant.

The product name on Harper's sheet is northline-assistant-2024. He wrote "no owner" on the line under the name and he underlined it once. I asked him, on Thursday, whether the underline meant the 2024 ship paper was missing. He said the ship paper is in the product drawer and that Riley Chen's name is on it, dated November 2024, and that the handover item was still no owner. I went to the drawer after he had gone. The ship note and the decision record are there. Riley Chen's name is on them as the owner of that ship. The service card clipped to the running note, which is the card Harper used when he walked me past the process, has no name in the owner line. The line is blank. Harper did not fill it with his own name. He did not fill it with mine. He did not copy Riley Chen's name forward from 2024 onto the card. He handed the blank over as the item.

I am not filling the blank. I am not information technology's idea of a product owner, and I am not appointing myself because the seat next to me is empty. I am not writing Riley Chen back onto the card on the strength of a 2024 signature, because Harper, who knew where that signature was, still wrote "no owner" as the item he was handing me. I am also not striking Riley's name off the 2024 paper. Those pages stay in the drawer as the pages they are. The open item is the one Harper numbered: no owner for the assistant. A blank card and an old signature are both in the building. I am not choosing between them this week. Questions about the assistant that arrive at my desk will be written down and not answered by an appointment I have not made. Morgan Ibarra's bay can keep using whatever the screen already shows. Use is not ownership. I am not confusing the two in order to make the item look closed.

The three items, restated so the foot of the page matches the head: the 2019 page is still linked, and I have not unlinked it. Phone rows are in two shapes, digits and dashed, and I have not picked a shape. There is no owner for the assistant, and I have not named one. Harper Singh left this week. These are the open items he handed me. I do not close them.

Copies: the integration shelf, with his original sheet clipped behind this typing; Dana Okonkwo's shelf; my own book. I am not sending this to Wes Calder. He is not in the building. I am not sending it to Harper at a forwarding address. The week he left is this week, and the note belongs here. Nothing under my signature retires a link, rewrites a phone, or staffs the assistant.
