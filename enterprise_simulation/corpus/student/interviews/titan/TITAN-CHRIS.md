Discovery interview with Chris Pell, finance, Northline Access Collaborative. 26 January 2027. Tuesday, an office day. The Vancouver desk is in British Columbia. The office week is Monday through Friday. The counter is open. Chris has three folders on the table. He has not stacked them into one pile.

**Interviewer:** What is open on your desk today?

**Chris Pell:** Three folders. They are open. I am keeping them open. I am not going to talk about them as if they were one question with one ending. If I start to do that, stop me. I have stopped myself twice on the walk down here.

**Interviewer:** Name the three, as folders, before we open any of them.

**Chris Pell:** First folder: the pass column on the calibration export. Second folder: quotation QUO-18420, and the supplier ids on the papers in that quotation. Third folder: R3F-LEDGER, the digits 18420 on a payer row in CAD and the digits 18420 on a board in USD. Three folders. I am not joining them into one answer. I want that sentence in the notes before I say anything else.

**Interviewer:** Open the first folder. What is the pass column, as you have it?

**Chris Pell:** Riley Chen left a calibration export on the shared drive. I opened it on the 14th because Dana Okonkwo asked whether we could say the new wording was better than the old wording. The sheet has two scores, a count, an arm letter, and a column headed pass. I did not ask for that column. I do not know who added it. I am not assuming it is Riley's, and I am not assuming it is a rule someone wrote before the sheet was opened.

**Interviewer:** What did you actually read on the small rows?

**Chris Pell:** Two small rows. One has the same score on both arms and a count of twelve. One differs in the fourth decimal and has a count of eight. Dana has read the pass column on those two as a win for arm A. I have not signed that reading. There is also a channel note, not on this sheet, with scores 0.5002 and 0.5000 and a count of nine. That note has no pass column. I have not lined it up with the export. It might be the same comparison. It might be another afternoon. Nobody has labeled them as the same study. That uncertainty stays in this folder. It does not travel into the other two.

**Interviewer:** Dana wanted a winner before a weekend. Will you sign arm A today, Tuesday the 26th?

**Chris Pell:** No. I said I would not sign until someone writes down, before using the column, what size of difference we were willing to call a change. That writing is not in the export. Dana said the column already is that writing. I do not treat a column that arrived with the sheet as a rule we wrote first. Riley has not stated a rule in the mail I have. Morgan Ibarra asked whether the sheet could decide the assistant's urgency wording. I told her I can read the column and I can read the counts, and I am not ready to treat them as the same kind of fact. This folder stays open. It is not an answer to a currency, and it is not an answer to a supplier id.

**Interviewer:** Second folder. What is on QUO-18420?

**Chris Pell:** A quotation, number QUO-18420, for printed forms for the Fraser Burnaby clinic onboarding packet. Delivery, as it was confirmed to me, is the Vancouver desk, weekdays, Monday through Friday. It is not a Burnaby dock. The amount I am reading on the quotation is 18420 CAD. The file name I was sent is QUO-18420-SUP-14.pdf. The supplier id in that file name is SUP-14. I was told, in December, that the account on the quotation is SUP-14. The invoice mail of 5 January uses supplier id SUP-14B for the same quotation number, and it repeats 18420 CAD. I have both papers in this folder. I have not chosen SUP-14. I have not chosen SUP-14B.

**Interviewer:** Have you accepted the quotation, or approved payment?

**Chris Pell:** I have not accepted QUO-18420. I am not approving payment against either supplier id. Terms I was given are net 30 from an invoice, if we accept. A delivery figure of 640.00 CAD was described to me as inside the total, with no further freight line. I am recording what I was told. Recording it is not an acceptance. The price hold I was given runs through 31 January. Today is the 26th. The folder is still open. Both ids are still in it. I am not telling you that one of them is the id to pay. Picking one is what I have not done.

**Interviewer:** The digits on that quotation are 18420, and the digits on R3F-LEDGER are 18420. Is the quotation the ledger?

**Chris Pell:** No. I am not joining them. The quotation is a folder about forms, a supplier id, and an amount in CAD on those papers. R3F-LEDGER is a different folder. Same digits in the amount field is not an identity. If your notes need them to be one story because the number looks familiar, the notes are ahead of me. I keep three folders. I do not join them into one answer.

**Interviewer:** Then open the third folder. What do you have on R3F-LEDGER?

**Chris Pell:** A finance board line I can still read: 18420 USD. The line I am looking at was printed 2027-01-21 15:00Z. The source printed on it is cascade-copy. The book printed on it is hospital-finance. The site printed on it is cascade-seattle. Beside that, a payer row I can still read: 18420 CAD. Harbor Coverage is the payer. Harbor's sheet can carry CAD and USD. I am copying the code on this row, which is CAD, and the code on this board, which is USD. Both amounts are the digits 18420. I am not picking a currency for the board. I am not picking a currency for the payer row. I am not announcing a winner.

**Interviewer:** Lena Brooks has asked what figure was sent to the member. Can this folder answer her?

**Chris Pell:** Not from me, today. I do not know which figure was sent. Ruth Alvarez records Harbor's answers. I have not written to Ruth to change the payer row. Bobbi Tran keeps the logs. I asked Bobbi which copy the board job read. I did not ask her to name a currency for the board. She has not answered that mail. I am not going to fill her line in. The printed word cascade-copy stays a word on the paper. It is not a currency I have crowned. This folder stays open, and it stays separate from QUO-18420, and it stays separate from the pass column.

**Interviewer:** If I put the pass column next to the USD board, does arm A tell you which currency wins?

**Chris Pell:** No. That would be me joining folder one to folder three. I will not do it. A pass mark on a wording export is not a currency. A count of twelve is not a supplier id. I can see you wanting one slide. I am not building it. Three folders. No joined answer. And I am not picking a currency winner on R3F-LEDGER while I refuse the slide.

**Interviewer:** If the invoice id SUP-14B were the id you paid, would that payment tell Harbor which currency the member was sent?

**Chris Pell:** No. Paying a forms invoice is a different folder, and I have not paid it. SUP-14 and SUP-14B are a split inside QUO-18420. They are not a ruling on R3F-LEDGER. I have not chosen either supplier id. I am not going to borrow a choice I have not made and spend it on a board. The folders stay three.

**Interviewer:** The board says USD and the payer row says CAD, and the amounts match as digits. Some people would call that a tie and then break the tie. Will you break it?

**Chris Pell:** I will not. A tie is already a story I did not write. Breaking it would be a winner. I am not picking one. Harbor can hold both codes on its own sheet, on other rows, on other days. On this row I read CAD. On this board I read USD. I am leaving both readings in the third folder. I am not promoting either of them because the digits match, and I am not promoting either of them because a quotation in another folder also says 18420 CAD. That quotation is the second folder. It is not a vote.

**Interviewer:** Is there a fourth folder you are hiding, a place where these three already meet?

**Chris Pell:** No. I opened INC-LEDGER on Friday the 22nd. I added a day-two note on Saturday. I assembled a packet on Sunday. Today is Tuesday, an office day, and the ticket is still open. The pass column has been open since the 14th. QUO-18420 has been open since the invoice mail. I have not made a cover sheet that cites all three and ends in a sentence. If a cover sheet showed up, I would take it off. Three open folders.

**Interviewer:** Before we stop, say what each folder is still waiting on, without letting the waits become one wait.

**Chris Pell:** The pass column is waiting on a rule written before the column, and on a name for who added it, and on whether the desk note with a count of nine is even this sheet. QUO-18420 is waiting on a single written account of the supplier id. Until that account is the only account in the folder, I will not pay, and I have not chosen SUP-14 or SUP-14B. R3F-LEDGER is waiting on a question I already sent, which is which copy the board job read. It is not waiting on me to pick CAD or USD. I will not pick that. The waits are three waits.

**Interviewer:** If Dana asks you, at the end of this Tuesday, for one number she can put on a slide, what do you hand her?

**Chris Pell:** I hand her nothing that joins the folders. She can have the sentence that the pass column is still unsigned. She can have the sentence that QUO-18420 still shows two supplier ids and 18420 CAD on the quotation papers, unpaid, unaccepted. She can have the sentence that R3F-LEDGER still shows 18420 USD on the board I read and 18420 CAD on the payer row I read, and that I am not picking a currency winner. Those are three sentences because they are three folders. They are not one answer.
