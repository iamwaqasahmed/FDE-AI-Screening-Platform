Discovery interview with Wes Calder, platform lead, Northline Access Collaborative. 11 February 2020. Tuesday, an office day. The desk is in Vancouver, British Columbia. The office week is Monday through Friday. The counter is open. This recording is from before Wes Calder left. He is in the building. The page he wrote is in the binder.

**Interviewer:** You are still the platform lead. What are you trying to finish?

**Wes Calder:** The one-instant work. I wrote it down. The page is titled "Canonical instant for every feed." The date on it is 2 April 2019. I am still the person in this chair. I have not handed the page to anyone else. I still believe one timestamp will settle the arguments.

**Interviewer:** Which arguments?

**Wes Calder:** The arguments about copying a time from one room onto another and then fighting about which copy was typed last. Cascade Seattle writes a time. Harbor Coverage writes a time. Cedar Ridge sends a night file with a time. Fraser Burnaby writes a time. This desk writes a time. People get tired and they start saying the copies disagree. I still believe one timestamp will settle those arguments. I have believed it since I put the page in the binder. I believe it this Tuesday.

**Interviewer:** Say what the page claims, as a page, without turning the claim into a picture of a glass.

**Wes Calder:** The feeds carry one timestamp. The facility mart stores that same instant. Rows that belong to one referral carry that instant. I am describing the page I wrote. I am not, in this chair, assigning a clock to a screen. The arguments I am talking about are the arguments between the rooms. One timestamp is what I believe will settle them.

**Interviewer:** Have the other organizations told you they decline a shared write?

**Wes Calder:** No. I have not been told that. Nobody from Harbor has told me they decline to write in one place with the other rooms. Nobody from Cascade has told me that. Nobody from Fraser has told me that. Nobody from Cedar Ridge has told me that. I would remember a refusal. I do not have one. I have not been told the other organizations declined a shared write.

**Interviewer:** If a refusal were already in a drawer somewhere, would you still be talking this way?

**Wes Calder:** I am talking this way because I have not been told. I am not going to answer a hypothetical refusal as if it had been handed to me. It has not been handed to me. Lena Brooks has not put a decline on my desk. Jordan Hale has not put a decline on my desk. Anita Desai has not put a decline on my desk. Sam Nguyen has not put a decline on my desk. I still believe one timestamp will settle the arguments. That belief is the state of this interview.

**Interviewer:** Jordan keeps Cascade's records. What do you expect from his book?

**Wes Calder:** I expect the arguments to get smaller when the timestamp is one timestamp. I am not sitting in his records room. I am not rewriting his headers this afternoon. I am saying what I believe will settle the fight when a Cascade slip and a desk line are both in the tray. One timestamp. I have not been told he declined to write with anyone else. If he had declined, someone would have had to tell me. They have not.

**Interviewer:** Lena supervises Harbor Coverage. Harbor's notices carry their own time. Does that notice already settle the argument, in your view?

**Wes Calder:** A notice that carries Harbor's own time is one of the copies people fight about. I do not think a pile of local notices is the settlement. I think one timestamp is the settlement. Ruth Alvarez sends Harbor's paper. I am not taking her pen away in this interview. I am saying what I believe will end the argument about which pen's time wins the telling. I have not been told Harbor declined a shared write. I am not going to invent that telling so the notes look cautious.

**Interviewer:** Cedar Ridge files at night, including Saturday and Sunday, and the clinic has no slot API. The office week here is Monday through Friday. Does a weekend file need its own timestamp story?

**Wes Calder:** I do not think so. Sam files when he files. There is no slot line a desk can call. The office week on this floor is still Monday through Friday. A Saturday file can be in the tray on Monday. I still believe one timestamp will settle the argument about that tray, the same way I believe it will settle a weekday argument. I am not building a second theory because the clinic has no slot API. One timestamp. I have not been told Cedar Ridge declined to be in that settlement.

**Interviewer:** Anita's clinic is in British Columbia, on the same zone as this wall. Does the shared zone mean you are already done with Fraser?

**Wes Calder:** No. The same zone has tempted people to talk as if her chart and our desk line were one writing. They are not one writing today. Her rack is in Burnaby. Our book is on this floor. I still believe one timestamp will settle the argument that comes from that gap. Sharing a zone name is not the timestamp I wrote the page for. I have not been told she declined a shared write. I am not treating silence as a decline. Silence is what I have. The belief is what I am stating.

**Interviewer:** Omar Farouk runs Cascade's night. A night slip can sit in the tray until an office morning. Will one timestamp make his night and Jordan's morning the same act?

**Wes Calder:** I believe one timestamp will settle the argument about when the thing happened. I am not saying his night tray and Jordan's office morning are the same act. They are different work. The argument I hear is about the time people quote when they retell the night. One timestamp is what I believe ends that retelling fight. I have not been told Omar, or Jordan, declined a shared write. I am still in the chair. I am still saying the belief.

**Interviewer:** Evan Cho asks where a sheet goes when it leaves the room. Morgan Ibarra runs the bay. Noel Park and June Lau speak to members. Does the page tell them what to say out loud?

**Wes Calder:** The page is about the timestamp that settles the arguments between the feeds. I am not handing Noel a script in this interview, and I am not handing June one. Morgan can keep the bay. Evan can keep asking where a sheet goes. Dana Okonkwo hears the arguments when they get loud. I still believe one timestamp will settle those arguments. I have not been told to stop. I have not been told the other organizations declined.

**Interviewer:** A person on the floor will want this page to end every fight in the building, including fights you have not been in. Are you ending those today?

**Wes Calder:** I am talking about the arguments between the rooms that copy times onto one another. I still believe one timestamp will settle those arguments. I am not, in this recording, painting a panel or handing the bay a script. If the hour was booked for a glass, the hour is the wrong hour. The page is the page. The belief is the belief.

**Interviewer:** Chris Pell keeps Harbor's notices in the finance cabinet. If two notices carry two times, do you already know which one he should pay from?

**Wes Calder:** No. I am not in his cabinet, and I am not picking a figure for him. I believe the argument about the times gets settled by one timestamp. That is a belief about the argument. It is not a payment instruction, and it is not a currency. I have not been told the payer declined a shared write. I would not turn a payment into that news even if I had been. I have not been.

**Interviewer:** Priya Shah and Alex Kim work the clinical side. Is the timestamp a clinical sentence?

**Wes Calder:** No. I am the platform lead. I am not a clinician. The page is not a letter, and it is not a review of a letter. Alex can be on call. Priya can keep Fraser's letters. I am not folding their work into the timestamp in order to sound complete. The arguments I mean are the feed arguments. One timestamp. I have not been told a clinical room declined a shared write, and I am not here to collect that kind of sentence anyway.

**Interviewer:** You wrote, in December, that the proposal would be carried out. It is now February. Has anything in these eight weeks changed the belief?

**Wes Calder:** No. December was the future tense on purpose. February is still the work. I am still here. The page is still filed. The status on it is the status I set. I have not taken it down. I have not been told the other organizations declined a shared write. Until someone tells me that, I am not going to narrate a refusal I do not have. I still believe one timestamp will settle the arguments.

**Interviewer:** If you left the building next month, what should stay true of this recording?

**Wes Calder:** That it was made while I was still platform lead. That I had not been told of a decline. That I still believed one timestamp would settle the arguments. I am not writing a farewell. I am not naming a successor. I am not striking the page. Today is Tuesday, 11 February 2020, and the office is open. The belief is the same belief I walked in with.

**Interviewer:** Say it once more, as the last thing, in case a later reader only keeps the ending.

**Wes Calder:** I still believe one timestamp will settle the arguments. I have not been told the other organizations declined a shared write. Both of those sentences are true of me in this chair, on this date. I am not leaving either of them behind for someone else to soften.
