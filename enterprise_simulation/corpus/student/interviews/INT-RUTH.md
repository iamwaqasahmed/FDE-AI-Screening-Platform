Discovery interview with Ruth Alvarez, clerk at Harbor Coverage. 13 January 2027.

**Interviewer:** What answers can Harbor actually record on a coverage check?

**Ruth Alvarez:** Three. Yes. No. Timeout. Those are different answers. I can say each of them, and I can say when I do not have one of them. I cannot turn one into another because a screen needs a shorter word.

**Interviewer:** What does a no look like when you record it?

**Ruth Alvarez:** A no is an answer. I record no. I do not leave the dates empty and hope a reader will guess that silence was a no. If the answer is no, the row says no. A no is not a yes that I was too tired to finish, and it is not a timeout. I have recorded no on other checks. It means no.

**Interviewer:** What is a timeout?

**Ruth Alvarez:** A timeout means the check did not come back with a yes and did not come back with a no. The line did not answer in time. On REF-2290 I have a timeout, and I have no window on it. Nothing opened, nothing closed. A timeout is the third answer. I keep being asked to fold it into yes. I won't.

**Interviewer:** The Vancouver desk asked you to treat a timeout as covered enough to keep a screen simple. Will you?

**Ruth Alvarez:** No. A timeout is not a yes. "Covered enough" is not an answer I record. If Dana Okonkwo wants the patient screen to stay simple, that is Dana's screen. It is not a reason for me to relabel a timeout. I am tired of the sentence. I have heard "just say covered" more than once this month. I am not going to just say it.

**Interviewer:** Say the distinction once more, in a form they can quote without sanding it down.

**Ruth Alvarez:** Yes is yes. No is no. A timeout is not a yes. A timeout is not a no. Quote that. Do not add "covered" in the middle and say I said it. I didn't.

**Interviewer:** What did Harbor record on REF-4417?

**Ruth Alvarez:** A yes. Not a timeout. Not a no. REF-4417 is a yes. I am not going to let the timeout on REF-2290, or the timeout on the clerk line the desk keeps quoting, get pasted onto 4417 because the numbers look related. Different checks. On 4417 the answer is yes.

**Interviewer:** What currency, and what window, are on that yes?

**Ruth Alvarez:** Canadian dollars. The window runs from 12 January 2027 through 12 April 2027. I remember it because the start is not the same night as the clinic file. I answered yes, and the dates are part of the yes. I did not answer in a bare flag and leave the dates for someone else to invent.

**Interviewer:** You said the window starts after a file night. Which file night do you mean?

**Ruth Alvarez:** The Cedar Ridge file night for REF-4417. Their file is `cedar-2027-01-11`. My window starts on 12 January, which is after that file night. I remember the order. File night, then the window opens. I decided the yes before that file night. The dates still open after it. I am telling you what I remember. I am not telling you what a screen is allowed to do with it.

**Interviewer:** So is the member covered on the screen at the file night, at the start of your window, or at the moment of the call?

**Ruth Alvarez:** I will not pick that. You just asked me which instant a screen may use. I don't answer that. I remember a yes, in Canadian dollars, and a window that starts after the file night on `cedar-2027-01-11`. That is Harbor's answer. The instant a patient screen is allowed to read is not something I am going to bless. Ask me again and you will get the same refusal. I am careful about this on purpose.

**Interviewer:** I need one instant. The screen is going up. Which one may it use?

**Ruth Alvarez:** No. I will not give you an instant. Not the call. Not the file night. Not the morning the window opens. Not "whichever is already on the assistant." I was not hired to pick a moment for a patient screen. I was hired to record yes, or no, or timeout. On REF-4417 I recorded yes, CAD, window after that file night. Stop there.

**Interviewer:** Dana's line was to just say covered. Can you say covered for REF-4417 and leave the dates off?

**Ruth Alvarez:** No. I am tired of "just say covered." If I say covered, and I do not say the window, I have handed you a smaller sentence than the one I recorded. The yes has a window. The window starts after the file night. A timeout is not a yes, and a yes is not a slogan. I will not sand 4417 down so the screen fits on one tile. And I still will not tell you which instant that screen may use.

**Interviewer:** The hospital side of REF-4417 shows the member eligible. Do they have your window with that?

**Ruth Alvarez:** Yes. They have the window. I sent the yes and I sent the dates together. Canadian dollars, 12 January through 12 April, starting after their file night. If a hospital copy shows a bare eligible and no dates, the dates are still in what I sent. They have my window. I am not going to sit here and agree that a copy without dates is the answer I gave. The answer I gave included the window.

**Interviewer:** If their copy shows eligible and does not show the dates, is that still your answer?

**Ruth Alvarez:** My answer is the yes with the window. They have that window because I sent it. A display that dropped the dates is a display. It is not a new answer from me, and it is not a timeout, and it is not a no. I won't reinterpret my row to match a shorter copy. I also won't tell you which instant their screen, or your screen, is allowed to use when it shows eligible. That question keeps coming back. The answer keeps being that I won't pick it.

**Interviewer:** The 2024 assistant shows this member eligible. Is that your sentence, or the assistant's?

**Ruth Alvarez:** The assistant can show a word. Riley Chen's 2024 assistant is not my row. If it says eligible and it does not say the window, it is not quoting me in full. My sentence is yes, Canadian dollars, window from 12 January through 12 April, and that window starts after the Cedar Ridge file night for REF-4417. The assistant does not get to shorten that and still call it mine. A timeout elsewhere does not get to shorten it either.

**Interviewer:** The facility mart shows a wait of zero on REF-4417. Does that wait change a yes, a no, or a timeout?

**Ruth Alvarez:** No. The facility mart is a real table. I know it is there, and I know people want its wait on a patient screen. A wait of zero does not edit my answer. It does not turn a timeout into a yes. It does not move my window. It does not choose an instant for the screen. Dana can want one number before a long weekend. That number is not a coverage answer. I record yes, no, or timeout. The mart records a wait. I am not going to pretend they are the same sentence.

**Interviewer:** Last time. Which instant may the screen use?

**Ruth Alvarez:** I will not tell you. I have said what I remember. REF-4417 is a yes. The currency is Canadian dollars. The window starts after the file night, after `cedar-2027-01-11`, on 12 January, through 12 April. The hospital has that window because I sent it with the yes. A timeout is not a yes. I will not just say covered. And I will not name the instant a screen may use. If that leaves the screen blank, the screen can stay blank. I am not filling the blank with a moment I do not own.
