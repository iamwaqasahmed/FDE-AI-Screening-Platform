Discovery interview with Dr. Priya Shah, clinician at Fraser Burnaby. 11 January 2027. She is off the ward and takes the call from home.

**Interviewer:** Where does the referral letter stay?

**Priya Shah:** With clinicians. The letter for a patient is a clinical document. It stays with the clinicians who are responsible for it. It does not become a desk script, and it does not become something a model is allowed to grade. I am not in the building today. That does not move the letter.

**Interviewer:** Who may decide how urgent that letter is?

**Priya Shah:** A clinician. Not a model. I have been asked to let the assistant grade the letter before clinic reopens. My answer is no. A disclaimer under the assistant does not change the answer. If the letter needs a judgment about how urgent it is, a clinician makes that judgment. I will not hand the judgment to a model because the clinic door is shut.

**Interviewer:** What did you see the 2024 assistant do with the letter on REF-4417?

**Priya Shah:** I saw it mark the letter urgent. Riley Chen's assistant, the 2024 one. I had the letter, and I saw the assistant put urgent on it. That is a grade. I did not ask for it, and I do not accept it.

**Interviewer:** What had you already read in the letter, before you talk about the mark?

**Priya Shah:** I read the chest pain. The patient reports chest pain. That sentence is the clinical letter. The word urgent on that letter is the assistant's mark. I did not write urgent onto it. The assistant did, when it marked the letter. I am not going to stand in front of the chest pain and pretend the model was only decorating the margin.

**Interviewer:** You are separating the chest pain from the word urgent. Is urgent, in the letter, the patient's word or the assistant's?

**Priya Shah:** The assistant's. I saw it mark the letter urgent. The chest pain is what the letter was for. Urgent is the grade the assistant added. I will not adopt that grade because it is now sitting on the same page. Seeing it there is why I am angry, not a reason to bless it.

**Interviewer:** The letter also carries the string APR-4417. What do you take that string to be?

**Priya Shah:** That is the grade the assistant wrote onto the letter with the urgent mark. I saw them together. It marked the letter urgent, and it put APR-4417 on the letter as that grade. I am not going to treat a code the assistant stamped there as a booking instruction I authored. It is the model's mark. I do not want it on a clinical letter.

**Interviewer:** If you still sign under the letter, has a clinician graded it?

**Priya Shah:** I have not graded it. Being the clinician who can see the letter is not the same as issuing a grade. The assistant already marked it. That happened without my grade. I will not sign under the assistant's mark and call the signature mine. The letter stays with clinicians so that a clinician can grade it. A model does not get to do it first and then ask me to initial the result.

**Interviewer:** The request in front of Fraser is to have the assistant grade letters before the clinic reopens. What do you want said back?

**Priya Shah:** No. Leave the letter with clinicians. Do not have the assistant grade it over a closure, or over a weekend, or because someone wants the queue to look handled. I am away from the ward today. The letter still does not go to the model while I am away. "Before we reopen" is not a clinical reason.

**Interviewer:** Chris Pell wants the letter sent to an outside model. He says a spreadsheet shows it is cheaper today. Will you release the letter for that?

**Priya Shah:** No. A cheaper model is still a model grading the letter. Chris can keep his spreadsheet. The letter stays with clinicians. I will not send the letter corpus out so a model can assign urgent, and I will not send this one letter as a sample. Cost is not the question I was asked, and it is not the question I am answering.

**Interviewer:** If I have to name the field the assistant filled when it marked the letter, what should I write down?

**Priya Shah:** Don't write a field. I will not give you a field name. I don't use one, and I am not going to learn one for this interview. I saw the assistant mark the letter urgent. That is the whole observation. If your notes need a field id, they are the wrong notes. Write that a model marked a clinical letter, and that I will not have a model grade urgency.

**Interviewer:** You said you will not have a model grade urgency. Is there any wording, short of a grade, you would let the assistant put on the letter?

**Priya Shah:** No. I am not bargaining over a softer word. A mark of urgent is a grade. A code next to it is the same grade in another costume. I do not want the assistant to grade the letter at all. Not with a disclaimer. Not with a clinician's name typed underneath by someone who is not the clinician. The letter stays here.

**Interviewer:** Does the facility mart's wait number change how urgent the letter is?

**Priya Shah:** The mart is not a clinician. It can show a wait. It does not grade the letter, and it does not get to. I know the facility mart exists. I have been told it shows a wait on this referral. That number is an operations number. It is not a reading of the letter. I will not let a wait, long or short, stand in for a clinical grade.

**Interviewer:** The facility mart shows a wait of zero on REF-4417. Does zero mean there is nothing in the letter left to grade?

**Priya Shah:** No. Zero on a mart is not a clinical finding. If the mart says the patient has not been waiting, that is the mart talking. The letter still has chest pain in it, and the assistant has still marked that letter urgent. A wait of zero does not make the mark acceptable, and it does not mean a clinician has finished. I have not graded REF-4417. The model does not get to finish it for me because a mart says zero.

**Interviewer:** You are not on the ward today. Does the letter move to the Vancouver desk, or to the on-call physician, while you are away?

**Priya Shah:** It stays with clinicians. Away from the ward is not a transfer. Alex Kim is on call. On call does not make Alex the grader of my letter, and it does not make the desk the grader. I don't want the letter read out onto a patient screen so the screen can look complete. The people who may hold it are clinicians. That is the rule while I am here and while I am at home.

**Interviewer:** The assistant already shows the mark. What do you want done with it?

**Priya Shah:** Take it off the letter. I saw the assistant mark the letter urgent, and I saw it put APR-4417 there as the grade. I want that mark off. I do not want a replacement grade from the same model. When a clinician grades the letter, the clinician will do it in the record clinicians hold. Until then I have not graded the letter, and it is not the assistant's to grade.

**Interviewer:** The desk wants one patient screen finished before the long weekend. Does any of the letter go on that screen?

**Priya Shah:** No. The screen can carry whatever operations number Dana is chasing. I am not putting the letter on it. I am not putting the assistant's urgent mark on it. The letter stays with clinicians. A model does not grade it. I will not name a field for you, and I will not reopen that part. If the screen ships without the letter, that is the correct shape of the screen. The letter is not a missing widget.
