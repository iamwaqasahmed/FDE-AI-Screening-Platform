Discovery interview with Evan Cho, privacy officer, Vancouver desk. 16 January 2027. Saturday, the day after his note. The office week is Monday through Friday. He is not at the counter.

**Interviewer:** Where does the letter stay?

**Evan Cho:** On the hospital side. The letter stays on the hospital side. I wrote yesterday, Friday, and I am saying the same thing today. It does not come over to the Vancouver desk because someone wants a screen to look finished, and it does not become a copy's destination because a job returned a status.

**Interviewer:** Priya Shah says the letter stays with clinicians. Are you saying her sentence, or a different one?

**Evan Cho:** Priya can speak for the clinical reading. I am not grading the letter, and I am not going to borrow her sentence and call it mine. I am telling you which side holds the file. The letter stays on the hospital side. A desk in Vancouver is not the hospital side. I have yesterday's note. I do not have a new home for the letter this morning.

**Interviewer:** What did the copy job do?

**Evan Cho:** A copy to ca-central-1 returned HTTP 200. No reviewer. That is what came back. I have those three things in front of me: the destination ca-central-1, the status HTTP 200, and no reviewer. I am not decorating them. The job returned that. I wrote it down on Friday. Today is Saturday, and the return has not grown a name overnight.

**Interviewer:** HTTP 200 is a success status. Does success mean someone reviewed the copy?

**Evan Cho:** No. A status is a status. HTTP 200 is what the job returned. No reviewer is named on that return. I am not going to look at 200 and invent a person. A destination string is not a person either. ca-central-1 is where the return says the copy went. It is not a signature.

**Interviewer:** If the return left the reviewer blank, are you willing to fill a name from the desk so the paperwork looks complete?

**Evan Cho:** I will not fill a name the return does not have. If the request record has a name, the record will show it, when I have the record. I do not have it. I am not remembering a name into the blank. No reviewer means no reviewer, on the return I actually saw.

**Interviewer:** You have a desk rule that wants a named reviewer. The job came back with no reviewer and HTTP 200. Did the job follow your rule?

**Evan Cho:** I am not folding those together. The desk rule is the rule I wrote for this desk. What the job did is the return: HTTP 200, destination ca-central-1, no reviewer. Those are different sentences. I can put them on the same page so Dana and Morgan can see both. Putting them on the same page does not make the return into the rule, and it does not make the rule into a description of the job.

**Interviewer:** Say the difference once more, as you would say it in the hallway, where people try to sand it down.

**Evan Cho:** The desk rule is ours. What the job did is the job's. HTTP 200 does not become the rule. The rule does not become the story of the job. A copy to ca-central-1 returned HTTP 200 with no reviewer. That is the job. The letter stays on the hospital side. That is not the job talking. I distinguish them because people on the desk want one sentence that settles the pair. I am not giving them that sentence.

**Interviewer:** Then which sentence should the desk obey on Monday, the May rule or the return?

**Evan Cho:** I am not awarding that. I asked for the request record. The record of what was asked before the job returned. I do not have it. Until it is on my desk I have the return, and I have the desk rule, and I am keeping them apart. Monday is the next office day. The office week is Monday through Friday. Today is Saturday. A weekend does not close the ask, and it does not pick a sentence for me.

**Interviewer:** Can you name the statute that settles whether that copy was allowed?

**Evan Cho:** I am not citing a statute. I don't have one on this note, and I am not going to reach for one so the HTTP 200 looks finished or unfinished. Desk words for the desk rule. Job words for what the job did. That is as far as I go. If you write a statute into your notes and attribute it to me, you will have written it yourself.

**Interviewer:** Dana or Morgan may bring you the letter on Monday and call that the record you asked for. Is the letter enough?

**Evan Cho:** No. If they bring me the letter, I will send it back and ask again for the request record. The letter is the letter. The request record is the record of what was asked. I am not turning the letter into the request because it is the paper they could find. The letter stays on the hospital side while I wait.

**Interviewer:** A screen line already mentions the referral. Morgan called that referral the call Noel took. Can you read the letter off the screen line and be done?

**Evan Cho:** A screen line is screen text. I am not treating a screen line as the letter, and I am not treating it as the request record. Noel is not being asked to open the letter file so this interview can have a prop. The letter stays on the hospital side. I still want the request record. I still do not have it.

**Interviewer:** If the copy returned HTTP 200 to ca-central-1, has the letter already left the hospital side?

**Evan Cho:** The letter stays on the hospital side. The job returned a status and a destination and no reviewer. I am not promoting that return into a move of the letter's home. ca-central-1 is the destination on the return. It is not a sentence that the hospital letter now lives wherever a status pointed. I distinguish the desk rule from what the job did. I am not citing a statute to glue them. I will read the request record on an office day, if it arrives. I do not have it now.
