Exit interview with Harper Singh, integration engineer. 2 March 2026. Monday of Harper's last week. The office week is Monday through Friday. Harper is still in the integration seat for this week.

**Interviewer:** This is your last week. What do you want left on the record before you go?

**Harper Singh:** The logs. There are three of them. I am not going to spend my last week pretending they are one machine with one story. People keep asking me for a sentence they can paint on a screen. I don't have that sentence. I have the logs.

**Interviewer:** Do the three logs share a clock?

**Harper Singh:** No. The three logs do not share a clock. Hospital, channel, and payer. Each one keeps the time that arrived with it. I do not convert them onto each other when they are written. I have been saying that in the room for a long time. I am saying it once more because I am leaving, and I don't want it smoothed out after Friday.

**Interviewer:** People still talk as if a referral has one instant underneath those three. What are they reading?

**Harper Singh:** A 2019 page. It is still up. Wes Calder wrote that clock page. It still claims the feeds share a clock. One canonical instant, the same stamp, the mart as that instant. The page is still there. I am looking at the logs I actually run. The three logs do not share a clock. Both of those things are true of the building this week. The page claims they do. The logs do not.

**Interviewer:** If the page claims they share a clock, and you say they do not, are you saying the 2019 page is wrong?

**Harper Singh:** I am saying the three logs do not share a clock. I am saying a 2019 page still claims they do. I am not taking the next step for you. I am not handing you a corrected page on my way out, and I am not standing over Wes's page with a pen. The page is still up. It still claims they share a clock. The logs do not share one. That is the disagreement. I am leaving it as a disagreement.

**Interviewer:** Then what should a screen use?

**Harper Singh:** I am not going to say what a screen should use. Ask me about the logs and I will talk about the logs. Ask me about the 2019 page and I will tell you it is still up and it still claims they share a clock. The screen question is the one I will not answer. I have been asked it in hallways for years. I am not answering it in my last week either.

**Interviewer:** A clerk has a member on the phone. The hospital stamp, the channel stamp, and the payer stamp do not match. What does the clerk say?

**Harper Singh:** I don't know what the clerk says. That is a desk sentence. I am the integration seat, and I am almost not that. The three logs do not share a clock. If the clerk's screen needs a single time, somebody else will have to decide what the screen uses. I am not that somebody. I will not pick one of the three stamps and call it the one a screen may show.

**Interviewer:** Bobbi Tran keeps the three logs after you leave. What should Bobbi tell a clerk?

**Harper Singh:** Bobbi can keep the logs. I am not leaving Bobbi a script for a screen. Bobbi will have the same three logs. They do not share a clock. The 2019 page will still be up, and it will still claim they do, unless someone who is not me takes it down. I am not telling Bobbi to take it down. I am not telling Bobbi to preach it. I am leaving the disagreement where it is.

**Interviewer:** In 2022 you wrote on the back of the old draft. You said the organizations would not share a pen. Did that settle the clock?

**Harper Singh:** It settled nothing about a screen. I took the 2019 draft out in the spring of 2022 and I wrote on the back. The organizations would not share a write path. I put the draft back. That ink is why a column never got cut the way the draft asked. It is not a rule for what a screen shows. I am not expanding it this week. I have nothing further under those lines. I said that then. I still mean it.

**Interviewer:** So the screen should use each log's own time, since they do not share a clock.

**Harper Singh:** I did not say that. You just said it. The three logs do not share a clock. That is a fact about the logs. A 2019 page still claims they do. That is a fact about a page that is still up. Neither of those sentences is me telling you what a screen should use. I heard the conclusion you want. I am not signing it.

**Interviewer:** Your last Friday is inside this office week. Will you take the 2019 page down before you go?

**Harper Singh:** No. The office week is Monday through Friday, and this is my last week, and I am not spending the rest of it editing Wes's page. It stays up. It still claims the logs share a clock. The logs I am leaving do not share a clock. I can hold both of those without becoming the person who tells a screen which one it may use. I am tired of being recruited into that.

**Interviewer:** Then say the part you will stand on, and stop before the part you will not say.

**Harper Singh:** The three logs do not share a clock. A 2019 page still claims they do. I will not say what a screen should use. That is the exit. If someone writes a fourth sentence under my name after I am gone, it will not be mine.
