Discovery interview with Dana Okonkwo, operations director, Vancouver desk. 20 January 2027. Wednesday, an office day. The desk is in Vancouver, British Columbia. The office week is Monday through Friday.

**Interviewer:** This is the second time we have sat down. What do you still want on the patient screen?

**Dana Okonkwo:** One number. I have not changed that. I wanted one number before the long weekend, and the weekend came and went, and I still want one number. I am not opening a second project because Morgan and Jordan have been talking. The screen gets one number.

**Interviewer:** Where do you want that number to come from, now that you have heard them?

**Dana Okonkwo:** The facility mart. I am still leaning on the mart. The mart has the figure. I am not asking the desk to invent a different one because two other people have sentences. The patient screen gets the mart's figure. That is still the ask.

**Interviewer:** What have you actually heard Morgan Ibarra and Jordan Hale say?

**Dana Okonkwo:** I have heard them disagree. Morgan starts the count when the desk takes the call. Jordan— wait. I had this wrong in my mouth a second ago. I was about to say Jordan starts when the night file lands. That is not what I heard. I was mixing him up with the clinic. What I heard from Jordan is that his board starts when the referral is accepted into the hospital record. Morgan's start is the call. Jordan's start is that acceptance. Those are not the same sentence. I heard the disagreement. I am not pretending I didn't.

**Interviewer:** You almost gave Jordan the night file. Are you sure you have his sentence now?

**Dana Okonkwo:** I am sure of what I heard, and I am not sure I could recite it if you woke me up. What I heard is the call, from Morgan, and hospital acceptance, from Jordan. I do not keep Cascade's board. I do not supervise Jordan. I heard them disagree, and I am not going to sand that into one sentence so I look tidy. I still want one number. I still want it from the mart.

**Interviewer:** If they disagree about the start, why is the mart still the figure you would ship?

**Dana Okonkwo:** Because the mart already has a figure, and the screen is what I was asked to fill. Morgan can keep the floor. Jordan can keep a board in Seattle. I have not line-checked either of them against the mart, and I am not going to do that reconciliation as the price of one number. They disagree. I heard it. I am still leaning on the mart. The patient sees one number. I want that number to be the mart's.

**Interviewer:** The mart's figure on REF-4417 has been described to you as zero, on a booking date of 11 January. Is that still the number you mean?

**Dana Okonkwo:** Yes. Zero. I am not building a special wait for one caller. The caller spoke. The mart has the figure. Use the figure. I am impatient about this because it is still one number, and it is still not a meeting.

**Interviewer:** Have you read Harbor Coverage's window on REF-4417?

**Dana Okonkwo:** No. I have not read it. I have not opened their dates. I said that on the 10th, and it is still true today. People keep handing me the window as if reading it were the same job as the number. It isn't. I have not read the payer window for REF-4417.

**Interviewer:** You hesitated. For a second you looked as if you had a date. What were you about to say?

**Dana Okonkwo:** I was about to say the window opens on the 11th, because that is the booking date I keep repeating, and my mouth tried to make them the same day. That was me guessing. I should not have started the sentence. I have not read the window. I do not know where Harbor's dates start, and I do not know where they end. Do not write down the 11th as a window I read. I did not read it. The guess does not count.

**Interviewer:** You also said, last time, that you had heard a timeout and told people to treat the member as covered enough to keep the screen simple. Was that timeout REF-4417?

**Dana Okonkwo:** I— I think I tied them together too fast. I heard timeout. I have not read a window on REF-4417. If the timeout belongs to another referral, I have not sat down and separated them. I am not going to stand here and repair that by inventing which check timed out. I heard the word. I have not read the dates. The screen I am chasing is still the mart's number, not a coverage sentence I have not opened.

**Interviewer:** If the window you have not read turned out to start after the booking date, would you still put the mart's zero up?

**Dana Okonkwo:** Yes. I have not read the dates, so I am not going to pretend a hypothetical window moves the number. Coverage can be read by the people who own coverage. I am not those people. Harbor uses Canadian dollars and US dollars. I have not opened this row, so I am not going to tell you which of those is sitting on it. The wait number does not wait on a window I have not read.

**Interviewer:** The handset on the REF-4417 call was not delivered. Does that change the number?

**Dana Okonkwo:** I don't run the handset. I said that before, and I still have not sat down with the handset log. A message that did not arrive is a delivery problem. It is not a second wait. I am not withholding the mart's figure because the handset failed, and I am not inventing a different figure to punish the failure. One number.

**Interviewer:** The 2024 assistant also shows a wait of zero on that referral. Is that the same figure as the mart, or a second figure that happens to match?

**Dana Okonkwo:** I treat them as the same ask. Zero and zero. Riley Chen can keep the assistant. I am not asking for a new one, and I am not going to delay the screen while someone explains how two zeros could be different objects. If they both say zero, I am not staffing that argument. I have already heard Morgan and Jordan disagree about the start. I am not adding a third disagreement about two zeros that look alike.

**Interviewer:** The assistant also shows a close and the string APR-4417. Do those go on the patient screen with the wait?

**Dana Okonkwo:** No. I asked for one wait number. Close of day is not the wait. An approval string is not the wait. Leave those on the assistant. The screen gets the number. I do not want the rest of the panel pasted underneath it so the screen looks finished.

**Interviewer:** Cedar Ridge has no slot line the desk can call, and that clinic files at night, including Saturday and Sunday. Your office week is Monday through Friday. Does a weekend file become the number?

**Dana Okonkwo:** No. I am not taking the number from a night file, and I am not opening the Vancouver counter because a Washington clinic filed on a weekend. Sam Nguyen files at night. That is his work. The office week here is Monday through Friday. Today is Wednesday, so the desk is open, and the ask is the same ask I had on Sunday the 10th. One number, from the mart. A weekend file is not a reason to invent a second one.

**Interviewer:** If Jordan's board and the mart differ after this, what does the patient see?

**Dana Okonkwo:** The mart's number. I am not publishing two waits. Jordan can keep his board. I have heard him. I have heard Morgan. They disagree, and I am not crowning either of them in order to feel fair. I am also not going to tell you I have reconciled them. I haven't. I still lean on the mart. The patient screen gets one number.

**Interviewer:** Who is allowed to change it once it is up? You, Morgan, the mart, the assistant?

**Dana Okonkwo:** The mart is the figure. I am not assigning a committee to edit it by hand this afternoon. If the mart says zero, that is the number I want on the screen. I have heard the disagreement. I have not read the payer window for REF-4417. Neither of those facts has given me a second number. One number. That is the whole of the second interview.
