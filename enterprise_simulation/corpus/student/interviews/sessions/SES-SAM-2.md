Discovery interview with Sam Nguyen, night file clerk at Cedar Ridge. 18 January 2027. Monday. Cedar Ridge is a rural clinic in Washington. There is no slot API. Sam files at night, including weekends. He takes this call in the morning after a Sunday night file. The Vancouver office week is Monday through Friday. That office week is not his filing week.

**Interviewer:** Does Cedar Ridge have a way to book a slot while a Vancouver caller is still on the phone?

**Sam Nguyen:** No. There is no slot API. I don't have a button that takes a sentence from the desk and turns it into a booking. I build a file at night and I send it. If it is not in the file, I have not booked it. People still talk as if the phone call already created the visit. It did not.

**Interviewer:** You file on Saturday. Say that in the form you want quoted.

**Sam Nguyen:** I file at night, and that includes Saturday and Sunday. The clinic is not on the Vancouver office week. Saturday night is a file night. Sunday night is a file night. I released a Saturday file this month, `cedar-2027-01-09`, the same way I release a Monday file. I counted that Saturday file on the clinic tally. I am not leaving Saturday off because an office in Vancouver is closed.

**Interviewer:** Last night was Sunday, and the night before that was Saturday. Did you file?

**Sam Nguyen:** I filed. Saturday night and Sunday night both went out. I am not going to invent file ids for those two nights in this chair if I do not have the sheets under my hand. I am telling you the practice, and I am telling you I did the practice. Weekend nights are file nights. That part I will sign.

**Interviewer:** There is an older file id that showed up twice. What is the id?

**Sam Nguyen:** `cedar-2025-05-25-45`. One Cedar file id. Two nights. The referral on both lines is HX-0045. The site on both lines is cedar-ridge. I wrote it into the notebook in November because I could not see a finished account of what the second night did to the first. I am still in that notebook. I have not closed it.

**Interviewer:** Read the two nights.

**Sam Nguyen:** The first line I copied is night 2025-05-25, stamped 21:00Z, file id `cedar-2025-05-25-45`. The second line is night 2025-05-26, stamped 21:00Z, the same file id. The calendar dates are a day apart. The id is not a day apart. The digits inside the id say 2025-05-25, and the second line is the 26th. I noticed that. I did not edit either line to make the dates match. I copied the stamps as they were printed. I am not finishing what the trailing Z means, and I am not turning those stamps into a Vancouver desk time.

**Interviewer:** Is the first row gone?

**Sam Nguyen:** I thought— wait. I was about to say yes, it's gone, the second night replaced it. That is the sentence I hear other people say, and it came out of me too fast. I am not sure the first row is gone. I copied the 25 May line. I did not strike it. Seeing it in my notebook is not the same thing as seeing it still stored, and striking it would look as if I had proved the stored row was gone. I have not proved that.

**Interviewer:** You sounded sure a second ago. What did you look for?

**Sam Nguyen:** A deletion note. A slip that said the 25 May line had been pulled. A mark I was willing to trust. I did not find one. The night folder I was reviewing still showed me the id twice, which is how both lines got onto the page. Whether the hospital log still has the first append is a question I have not finished. I am not sure the first row is gone. I need that sentence to stay soft. I said "gone" and I should not have.

**Interviewer:** Bobbi Tran keeps the three logs. Did she tell you the first row had been removed?

**Sam Nguyen:** No. I— I think I implied she had. She hasn't. I did not ask Bobbi whether the first row is still in the hospital log. I did not ask her to drop it. I am not going to write her answer for her. If I said she confirmed it, that was me getting ahead of a conversation I have not had. I am not sure the first row is gone. That is the whole of what I can honestly say about the stored row.

**Interviewer:** What do you think the second night is, if you will not say the first row left?

**Sam Nguyen:** I think the second night is a correction. Same file id, same referral, same site, later stamp. That is a thought. I have not signed it as a finding. I was not on that desk in May 2025. I have not found a note from the clerk who was. Thinking the second night is a correction does not tell me the first row has left. I am leaving both lines in the notebook. I did not tear out the 25 May line, and I did not drop the 26 May line to make the id look as if it happened once.

**Interviewer:** Separate question. After the merger, a file named inlet-2026-11-02 showed up. What is that?

**Sam Nguyen:** A leftover. Inlet Medical merged into Cedar Ridge on 1 September 2026. The old site id still appears on some files. `inlet-2026-11-02` arrived after that date still carrying the old site. I saw inlet-medical on the header. November is after September. I am not disputing that. I also clipped another name beside it, `cedar-r3a-2026-11-02`, and I did not strike either name. The referral on that jacket is R3A-MERGE. I am not going to pretend the merger wrote a rule I can recite.

**Interviewer:** Did you just treat that inlet file as the same object as the two-night id?

**Sam Nguyen:** I did, for a second, and that was wrong. `inlet-2026-11-02` is not `cedar-2025-05-25-45`. Different id, different year, different jacket. The two-night id already begins with cedar-. The November file is the merger leftover, the old site still on it. I file on Saturdays. That practice is not a reason to call the November leftover a Saturday file. I should not have let the two stories share a sentence. I am separating them now. I am sorry I blurred them.

**Interviewer:** Will Cedar Ridge also file inlet-2026-11-02, or is the old-site arrival the only filing?

**Sam Nguyen:** I am uncertain. I do not have a yes I can sign, and I do not have a no I can sign. There is no slot API I can query to settle it. A nightly file being the method is not an answer to whether this particular inlet file is also a Cedar Ridge file. I have not been handed a rule that turns an old-site arrival into a second filing, and I have not been handed a rule that forbids one. I am not going to invent either rule this morning. Both names are still in the jacket. I have not struck them.

**Interviewer:** The clinic tally has Saturday files. The Vancouver office tally has been described to you as an office week, Monday through Friday. Which tally is right?

**Sam Nguyen:** I am not declaring that. I counted `cedar-2027-01-09` on the clinic tally. I file Saturday nights. Their office week is Monday through Friday. I don't decide which list wins, and I don't want my count rewritten so the two lists agree. Quoting me as the person who picked a tally would be you putting a verdict in my mouth. I won't name one.

**Interviewer:** On REF-4417 the caller said the visit was booked at Cedar Ridge tonight. Your file for that referral is `cedar-2027-01-11`. Does the caller's sentence retire any of this?

**Sam Nguyen:** No. A caller's sentence is not the file. `cedar-2027-01-11` is the Monday night file for that referral. It is not the Saturday file, it is not the two-night id, and it is not `inlet-2026-11-02`. I am not sure the first row of `cedar-2025-05-25-45` is gone. I am not sure what the cedar name on the November leftover amounts to. I can tell you what I filed and what I refused to strike. I cannot tell you a rule I was never given.
