Discovery interview with Dr. Priya Shah, clinician at Fraser Burnaby. 22 January 2027. Friday, an office day. She has stepped off the ward to take the call. Fraser Burnaby is in British Columbia. This is a follow-up. The letter and the assistant are the subject. She has the chart with her.

**Interviewer:** On the 11th you said the letter stays with clinicians, and that you will not have the assistant grade it. Has that moved?

**Priya Shah:** It has not moved. I have had more days to get angry, and the answer is the same answer. The letter stays with clinicians. A model does not grade it. I am on the ward's side of the door today. That does not make the letter available to the assistant. It makes me easier to find, which is not the same thing.

**Interviewer:** Riley Chen still wants the 2024 assistant to read the letter. What do you want said back to him?

**Priya Shah:** No. Riley owns the assistant. He is not a clinician, and a read by his assistant is not a clinician finishing the page. I said no on the 11th. I am saying no again on a Friday, with the chart in my hand, so nobody can tell me I was only refusing because I was at home. The letter stays here. The assistant does not grade it.

**Interviewer:** You said you would bring one chart. Bring it. Which one, and what did you hold?

**Priya Shah:** The Fraser chart for the letter on REF-4417. I had the page in my hand, not a summary. I am not going to call it a Cascade chart. I almost did. Cascade is Jordan Hale's hospital, in Seattle. This page was here, at Fraser Burnaby. Anita Desai manages the clinic. The letter is not hers to hand to a model, and it is not Cascade's because a referral number can be spoken in more than one building. It is the letter. I held it.

**Interviewer:** What was on the page, in the order you saw it?

**Priya Shah:** The body of the letter says the patient reports chest pain. That sentence is the patient's report. It is why the letter exists. Above that, not inside the patient's sentence, the word urgent is written, and the string APR-4417 is on the same page. I did not write urgent. I did not write that string. The 2024 assistant put them there. I can still see them sitting where a clinician's note would sit. I am describing the page. I am not giving you a name for a box on a form.

**Interviewer:** If I have to write down the place on the form where urgent sits, what do I call it?

**Priya Shah:** You don't. I will not give you a name for it. I don't use one, and I am not going to learn one so your notes look complete. I saw the word urgent on the letter, and I saw APR-4417 on that letter with it. That is the observation. If your notes need a label for a column, they are the wrong notes. Write that a model marked a clinical letter. Do not ask me to baptize the mark.

**Interviewer:** You blurred the string. Say it again, slowly.

**Priya Shah:** I had the digits turned for a second. I almost said APR-4471. That was me rushing. The string on the page is APR-4417. I looked at it again before I came off the ward, because I did not want to carry a scrambled code into this call and then have someone treat the scramble as the chart. Chest pain is in the body. Urgent is written above it. APR-4417 is on the page. The assistant wrote the mark. I am not adopting it because I can read it.

**Interviewer:** Is urgent, on that page, the patient's word or the assistant's?

**Priya Shah:** The assistant's. The patient reports chest pain. I read that. Urgent is the grade the assistant added. I will not stand in front of the chest pain and pretend the model was only decorating the margin. Seeing the word on the same page as the pain is why I am angry. It is not a reason to bless the word.

**Interviewer:** Will you let a model grade urgency?

**Priya Shah:** No. I will not let a model grade urgency. Not this model. Not a later one. Not a cheaper one. A clinician grades the letter, in the record clinicians hold, when a clinician grades it. I have not graded this one. The assistant already marked it. That happened without my grade. I will not sign under the mark and call the signature mine, and I will not let the model finish the grade because I have a clinic to get back to.

**Interviewer:** Riley's ask was to let the assistant read the letter before clinic reopened. Clinic is open today. Does that retire the refusal?

**Priya Shah:** No. "Before we reopen" was never a clinical reason, and "we have reopened" is not one either. The letter does not become fair game because the door is unlocked. I am in the building. The letter still does not go to the model. Riley can keep the assistant. He does not get this page.

**Interviewer:** A disclaimer under the assistant. Does that change what you will allow?

**Priya Shah:** No. A disclaimer is a sentence under a grade. It does not stop the grade being a grade. I am not bargaining over a softer word than urgent. I do not want the assistant to grade the letter at all. Not with a disclaimer. Not with my name typed underneath by someone who is not me.

**Interviewer:** Chris Pell says a spreadsheet shows an outside model is cheaper. Will you release the letter for that?

**Priya Shah:** No. A cheaper model is still a model grading the letter. Chris can keep his spreadsheet. I will not send the letter out so a model can assign urgent, and I will not send this one page as a sample. Cost is not the question I was asked. The chart I just described is the question. Chest pain in the body. A mark above it that I did not write. I will not hand that page over.

**Interviewer:** Alex Kim is on call. If you are off the ward, does Alex grade this letter?

**Priya Shah:** No. On call does not make Alex the grader of my letter. I don't know whether Alex has even seen this page. I am not going to invent that he has, and I am not going to treat on-call as a signature. The letter stays with clinicians. Away from the chart for an hour is not a transfer. Alex is not the assistant, and the assistant is not Alex.

**Interviewer:** The facility mart shows a wait of zero on REF-4417. Does zero change how urgent the letter is?

**Priya Shah:** No. Zero on a mart is not a reading of the letter. I know the mart exists. I have been told it shows a wait. That number is an operations number. It does not grade chest pain, and it does not make the assistant's mark acceptable. I have not graded REF-4417. A wait of zero does not mean a clinician has finished. I also thought, earlier this month, that the 21:00 on the assistant was our clinic's close. I don't actually know that, and I should not have treated a panel time as Fraser's day. It is not a grade. I will not let a model grade urgency because a panel has a clock on it.

**Interviewer:** You said "a clock on it." Are you assigning that time to anything?

**Priya Shah:** No. I am taking back a confusion. I do not know what the assistant's time is measuring, and I am not going to learn it in this call. I am a clinician. The letter is the chart I described. I will not let the model grade it. A time on a panel is not a clinical finding, and I am not here to explain the panel.

**Interviewer:** Does any of this letter go on the patient screen the desk is trying to finish?

**Priya Shah:** No. The screen can carry whatever operations number Dana Okonkwo is chasing. I am not putting the letter on it. I am not putting the word urgent on it. I am not putting APR-4417 on it. The letter stays with clinicians. If the screen ships without the letter, that is the shape I want. The letter is not a missing piece of the screen.

**Interviewer:** Do you know whether anyone at the desk already read the word urgent to the member?

**Priya Shah:** I don't know. I have not asked Noel Park, and I have not asked June Lau, and I should not guess. If they read it, they read the assistant's mark, not my grade. I still have not graded the letter. I will not let a model grade urgency, and I will not repair a conversation I did not hear by pretending I know what was said. The chart is the page I described. That is the follow-up. I am going back to the ward.
