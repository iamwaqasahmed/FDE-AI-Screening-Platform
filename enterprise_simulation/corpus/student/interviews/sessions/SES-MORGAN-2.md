Discovery interview with Morgan Ibarra, desk supervisor, Vancouver desk. 21 January 2027. Thursday, an office day. The desk is in Vancouver, British Columbia. The office week is Monday through Friday. The counter is open.

**Interviewer:** When does the count you will say out loud at this desk actually start?

**Morgan Ibarra:** When the desk takes the call. That is the start. The member is on the phone, or at the counter, and the desk has taken it. I start there. I am not starting it from a figure I have not agreed to read, and I am not starting it from a board I do not keep.

**Interviewer:** Dana Okonkwo still wants one number off the facility mart. Is the mart the start of your count?

**Morgan Ibarra:** No. Dana can keep wanting one number. I have heard her. I have not signed the mart onto the phone. The count I start is the count that starts when the desk takes the call. If the mart is counting something else, I have not agreed to call it mine. I am not going to turn her sentence and mine into one sentence because she is impatient.

**Interviewer:** Tell me what Noel Park almost said to a member.

**Morgan Ibarra:** Noel almost told a member the visit was booked. I had the wrong clerk in my mouth just now— I almost said June. It was not June Lau. June was on the counter side of the bay. It was Noel. Noel Park, my clerk. He almost told the member the visit was booked. He did not finish the sentence. He told me he heard it coming and shut his mouth. I believe that. I was not on the handset for the whole of it.

**Interviewer:** Follow that. Did any part of the booking sentence actually reach the member?

**Morgan Ibarra:** I don't know how much of it got out. Noel says he stopped. I was not the ear on the phone. "Almost" is the word I will use, because "almost" is the word he used to me. I am not going to upgrade almost into a finished sentence so the story is cleaner, and I am not going to downgrade it into nothing. He almost told the member the visit was booked. He stopped. I am uneasy about how close that was, and I am not going to decorate it.

**Interviewer:** Why was that sentence in his mouth?

**Morgan Ibarra:** Because the caller had said the visit was booked. What Noel heard, and what he repeated to me, was "booked at Cedar Ridge tonight." He almost gave that sentence back to the member as if it were ours. Cedar Ridge is in Washington. We do not have a slot line we can query while the caller is still talking. A sentence on our phone is not a booking I have seen. Noel had their words. He almost handed the words back. He stopped.

**Interviewer:** So was the visit booked?

**Morgan Ibarra:** I don't know. I have not seen a night file that I am willing to call the visit. Sam Nguyen files at night, including Saturdays and Sundays, and that file is his release, not a sentence Noel almost said. I supervise the desk. I do not release Cedar Ridge's file. I will not tell you the visit was booked, and I will not tell you it was not. Noel almost said it. Stopping is not the same as knowing.

**Interviewer:** There is a separate thing you have seen on the handoff queue. Describe it.

**Morgan Ibarra:** The queue kept the previous caller. I have seen it. A call ends. A new caller is on the line. The new slip still has the previous caller's person id on it. The next clerk has the new person in front of them and the previous person on the paper. I saw that pair. I am not guessing at a story from Seattle. It happened on this queue, at this desk.

**Interviewer:** Follow that. The new call was supposed to start blank. What was still sitting there?

**Morgan Ibarra:** The previous caller's person id. Not a fresh identification. The slip had kept the person who had just hung up, or just left the counter. The rule I wrote says that must not happen. Seeing it does not make it allowed. The new call is a new call. It does not inherit the previous caller because the bay is busy.

**Interviewer:** Which two calls were they? I want the marks.

**Morgan Ibarra:** I— I don't have the marks in my head. I thought one of them might have been the REF-4417 call, and now I am not sure I am allowed to say that. I recorded the office date and the two call marks in the desk book. I did not bring the book to this chair. If I name the marks from memory I will name them wrong. I know what I saw. I do not know, sitting here, which two marks they were. I am not going to invent them.

**Interviewer:** What did you do when you saw the previous caller still on the new slip?

**Morgan Ibarra:** I had the slip pulled back. The previous person id gets struck. The person id of the caller who is actually waiting gets written, from that caller, not from the slip that just ended. If someone has already been spoken to under the wrong person id, the clerk stops, tells me, and I tell Evan Cho. That is the desk. It is paper and a voice. I am not describing a program.

**Interviewer:** Should Bobbi Tran change the software so the queue cannot keep the previous caller?

**Morgan Ibarra:** No. I am not asking Bobbi to change a program, and I am not going to design one in this interview. Bobbi keeps the three logs. This queue is slips. If you want a screen, a table, a fix in a system, I am the wrong person, and I am not becoming that person because the slip failed. The correction I authorized is the pull-back and the fresh identification. That is the whole of it. I will not add a design on top.

**Interviewer:** If the same failure shows up on the next call, what do you publish as the repair?

**Morgan Ibarra:** The same check. Pull the slip. Strike the previous caller. Obtain the person id from the person who is actually there. Write it again in the desk book so Monday knows the check is not theoretical. Repetition is not a specification. I am not leaving you a design. I am leaving you the thing I already saw, and the thing the clerks already do with paper.

**Interviewer:** Noel almost told a member a visit was booked, and the queue kept a previous caller. Do those two events make you move the start of the count?

**Morgan Ibarra:** No. The count starts when the desk takes the call. An almost-sentence does not move the start onto a night file. A slip that kept the previous caller does not move the start onto the mart. Both of those are failures I have to live with. Neither of them is a new start. I start when the desk takes the call. I am shaky about the call marks. I am not shaky about the start.

**Interviewer:** Jordan Hale says Cascade's board starts when the referral is accepted into the hospital record. Dana still wants the mart. You have heard them. Who wins?

**Morgan Ibarra:** I will not pick a winner. I start the count when the desk takes the call. That is the desk. I have heard Jordan. I have heard Dana. I am not crowning the mart, and I am not crowning hospital acceptance, and I am not going to pretend the almost-booking was a visit. If you need a person who will point at one and cross the others out, I am the wrong person.

**Interviewer:** Today is Thursday. The counter is open. What does Noel say if that member calls back this afternoon?

**Morgan Ibarra:** The holding line. The desk has the call. We do not yet have a number everyone on Dana's thread will stand behind. He does not tell them the visit was booked. He does not finish the sentence he already almost said. He does not read them a figure I have not agreed to feed the phone. I hate the holding line. It is the sentence I have. I start the count when the desk takes the call. I am not designing anything else under that.
