Discovery interview with Ruth Alvarez, clerk at Harbor Coverage. 19 January 2027. Tuesday. Harbor Coverage records in Canadian dollars and in US dollars. Ruth is at her own desk, not at the Vancouver counter. The Northline office week is Monday through Friday. Today is an office day there, and the desk has been on her line again.

**Interviewer:** What answers can you actually record when a coverage check comes back?

**Ruth Alvarez:** Three. Yes. No. Timeout. They are different answers. I can say each of them. I cannot turn one into another because a screen needs a shorter word. I am going to be dull about this on purpose. Dull is the point.

**Interviewer:** Stay on REF-2290. What did Harbor record?

**Ruth Alvarez:** A timeout. No window. Nothing opened, nothing closed. The line did not come back with a yes and it did not come back with a no. I keep being asked to fold that into a yes. I won't. REF-2290 is a timeout, and the window on it is absent. I am not filling the absence.

**Interviewer:** Is a timeout a yes?

**Ruth Alvarez:** No. A timeout is not a yes. A timeout is not a no. I want that quoted without a softer word slipped into the middle. I have said it until I am tired of my own voice, and I am saying it again because the desk is still asking.

**Interviewer:** The Vancouver desk wants you to say covered. Will you, for REF-2290?

**Ruth Alvarez:** No. I am tired of the desk asking me to say covered. "Covered enough" is not an answer I record. If they want a patient screen to stay simple, that is their screen. It is not a reason for me to relabel a timeout. I have heard "just say covered" more than once. I heard it again after the 13th. I am not going to just say it.

**Interviewer:** Who asked you? Name the person.

**Ruth Alvarez:** I was about to say Jordan Hale, and that is wrong. Jordan keeps records at Cascade Seattle. He is not the voice on my line. It is the Vancouver desk. Dana Okonkwo is the operations director who wants the screen simple. I am not sure, this morning, whether the latest ask was Dana herself or a clerk repeating her. I think I heard June in it, and I may be mixing the voices. I know it was the desk. I know they wanted me to say covered. I will not. I am not going to pin the latest sentence on the wrong mouth just to sound precise.

**Interviewer:** Give me a window for REF-2290. Even a start date.

**Ruth Alvarez:** I— no. I started to. I had "12 January" in my throat because that is a date I actually have on a different referral, and my mouth tried to reuse it. I am not doing that. I will not invent a window. REF-2290 has no window. If I give you a start, I have lied. Stop asking me for a date I do not have.

**Interviewer:** A rough window, then. Not the official one. Something the desk can put under the timeout so the tile is not empty.

**Ruth Alvarez:** No. A rough window is an invented window. I will not invent one to keep a tile from looking empty. Empty is the honest shape. Timeout, no window. I am tired, and I am not tired enough to get sloppy about this. If the desk needs a word, the word is timeout. It is not yes. It is not covered. It is not a pair of dates I made up in this chair.

**Interviewer:** REF-4417 is a different check. What did you record there?

**Ruth Alvarez:** A yes. Not a timeout. Not a no. Do not paste REF-2290 onto 4417 because the desk is in a hurry. On 4417 the answer is yes. The currency is Canadian dollars. The window runs from 12 January 2027 through 12 April 2027. I remember it because I recorded it. I am not remembering 2290 when I say those dates. If I blur them, stop me.

**Interviewer:** You record Canadian dollars and US dollars. Which is on the yes?

**Ruth Alvarez:** Canadian dollars, on REF-4417. I almost said US dollars just now, because Harbor uses both and the codes sit next to each other in my head. That would have been wrong for this row. This row is CAD. I am correcting it while it is still in the air. Do not write USD against 4417 from this interview. I misspoke. The yes I recorded is in Canadian dollars, with the window I just gave you.

**Interviewer:** The hospital copy of REF-4417 has been described as eligible, with the window not sitting on that copy. Is that still your answer?

**Ruth Alvarez:** My answer is the yes with the window. A display that dropped the dates is a display. It is not a new answer from me, and it is not a timeout, and it is not a no. I won't reinterpret my row to match a shorter copy. I also will not choose, for them or for the desk, the moment at which that copy is allowed to speak. That question keeps coming back. I keep not answering it.

**Interviewer:** The desk will ask again this afternoon. If I need one instant for the screen, which one do you give me?

**Ruth Alvarez:** I don't give you one. Not the call. Not the file night. Not the morning the window opens. Not whatever is already on the assistant. I was not hired to pick a moment for a patient screen. I was hired to record yes, or no, or timeout. On REF-4417 I recorded yes, CAD, and the window. On REF-2290 I recorded timeout, and I will not invent a window to sit beside it.

**Interviewer:** The facility mart shows a wait of zero on REF-4417. Does that wait change a yes, a no, or a timeout?

**Ruth Alvarez:** No. A wait of zero does not edit my answer. It does not turn the timeout on REF-2290 into a yes. It does not move the window on 4417. It does not fill a window that was never there. Dana can want one number. That number is not a coverage answer. I record yes, no, or timeout. The mart records a wait. They are not the same sentence, and I am tired of being asked to pretend they are.

**Interviewer:** If June Lau calls you after lunch and says the member is waiting, and all she needs is the word covered, what do you say?

**Ruth Alvarez:** I say no. I am tired of the desk asking me to say covered. If June is the voice this time, the answer is the same answer I would give Dana. Timeout is not yes. REF-2290 does not grow a window because a member is waiting. I will not invent one on the phone, and I will not invent one in a mail after the phone. She can tell the member I would not say covered. That is an accurate sentence. "Covered" is not.

**Interviewer:** Last time through. REF-2290, in one breath, and then stop.

**Ruth Alvarez:** REF-2290 is a timeout. A timeout is not a yes. There is no window, and I will not invent one. I am tired of the desk asking me to say covered. I will not say it. REF-4417 is a different check, and I am not going to let the desk collapse them so the screen fits on one tile.
