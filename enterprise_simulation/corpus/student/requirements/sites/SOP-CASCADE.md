# How Cascade records accepts a referral

Northline Access Collaborative
Cascade Seattle, hospital, Washington
Time zone of this records office: America/Los_Angeles
Issue date: 12 April 2026
Author: Jordan Hale, records
Procedure owner: Jordan Hale

## What this issue is

This issue is the procedure Jordan Hale uses at Cascade Seattle when a referral is accepted into the hospital record, and the procedure for the board that records office keeps. Cascade Seattle is a hospital in Washington. The records office that keeps the board works Monday through Friday. That is the office week of this issue. Saturday and Sunday are not days on which this office accepts a referral or moves the board.

Omar Farouk is the night supervisor at Cascade Seattle. Omar watches the night tray. A packet that arrives after the Friday close, or on Saturday, or on Sunday, stays in that tray until the next office morning. Omar does not accept the referral. Omar does not add to the board and does not take away from the board. Jordan accepts on the office morning, from the tray and from the packets that arrive that day, under the steps below.

The Vancouver desk of Northline Access Collaborative has its own office week, Monday through Friday, in America/Vancouver. This issue does not move Cascade records onto the Vancouver counter, and it does not move the Vancouver counter onto the Cascade board. Dana Okonkwo directs operations at that desk. Morgan Ibarra supervises it. They may ask what this office has accepted. The answer is given by the steps below, and the answer names the copy it was read from.

## The packet

A referral reaches this office as a packet. The packet names the referral. Jordan reads the referral id on the face of the packet before any other step. A packet that does not name a referral is not accepted. It goes back to the tray with a note that the id was missing, and it does not touch the board.

Jordan confirms that the packet is for Cascade Seattle. A nightly file from Cedar Ridge is a file from a rural Washington clinic. It is not, by itself, an accept into this hospital's record. Cedar Ridge has no slot API, and this office does not invent one in order to treat a Cedar Ridge file as a Cascade accept. A letter that stays with a clinician at Fraser Burnaby is not an accept at Cascade. A sentence spoken on a phone at the Vancouver desk is not an accept. A row someone describes as already on a facility mart is not an accept. Jordan has heard each of those offered as if they were the same act. Under this issue they are not the act.

If the packet is for another site, Jordan does not accept it into the Cascade Seattle record. He notes the site named on the packet and leaves the packet out of the board. Inlet Medical, on the date of this issue, is not Cascade Seattle. Fraser Burnaby is not Cascade Seattle.

## How an accept is made

Acceptance is a records act on an office day. Jordan writes the referral into the hospital record at Cascade Seattle and marks that record accepted. He writes the referral id, the site name Cascade Seattle, and the office date of the accept in the book he is writing in that day. The book is the working book. The mark is the accept. Until that mark is in the working book, the referral is not accepted, whatever a caller, a file, or another office has already said.

The order is fixed. First the packet is identified. Then the site is confirmed. Then the mark is written in the working book. Then, and only then, the board in the next section may change. Jordan does not change the board first and write the mark later. He does not accept by telephone and leave the book for the afternoon. If he is interrupted after the mark and before the board, the accept stands and the board is brought into line before he leaves the desk. If he is interrupted before the mark, there has been no accept, and the board is left as it was.

A correction later the same office day is a correction of the record, not a second accept. Jordan strikes the wrong line in the working book, writes the corrected line, and records that the referral was already accepted. He does not add a second accept for the same referral because a name, a clinic wording, or a date on the packet was amended. If the accept itself was made in error and Jordan reverses it the same office day, he marks the reversal in the working book and the board step below reduces the count by one. A reversal is a reversal. It is not a quiet omission.

Omar Farouk does not perform this section on a night shift. The night tray holds the packet. The accept waits for the office day.

## What the board counts

The board in this records office counts referrals this office has accepted and has not reversed. The unit of the count is the accept. One referral accepted once adds one. A second conversation about the same referral does not add one. A Cedar Ridge file that mentions the referral does not add one. A call answered by Noel Park or June Lau does not add one. A figure Morgan Ibarra might read at the Vancouver counter does not add one. A packet sitting in Omar Farouk's night tray does not add one.

When Jordan has marked the accept in the working book, he adds one to the board for that referral if the referral was not already on the count. When he has marked a same-day reversal, he subtracts one if the referral was on the count. He writes the new count in the board book with the office date. He does not copy a count from the Vancouver desk into this board. He does not copy a count from Harbor Coverage into this board. Chris Pell may ask, from finance, what number should sit beside a later display. This issue answers only for the board Jordan keeps: the count of accepts not reversed.

The board count is a count kept in this office. Staff at other offices have described other counts, begun on other events, and Jordan has heard those descriptions. This issue states what this board counts. It does not name a clock for a screen. It does not say that the accept is the instant any other display is allowed to use. It does not choose among the desk, the mart, the file night, and this board. A person who wants that choice will not find it in these pages. Jordan will not add it in the margin.

## The copy the desk saw yesterday

This office keeps the working book, and it keeps a copy that is one version behind the working book. Staff here call that copy the copy the desk saw yesterday. The name is the name used on the records floor. It means the version the desk was shown on the previous office day, still sitting one version behind the book Jordan is writing in today.

An accept marked this morning is in the working book. It is not yet on the copy the desk saw yesterday. A correction marked this morning is in the working book. The copy the desk saw yesterday still shows the earlier version, including a clinic name or a line that the working book has since changed. A clerk who reads only that copy is reading yesterday's version. The clerk is not reading the working book, even when the two sit on the same table.

When the Vancouver desk asks what Cascade has accepted, the clerk says which copy the answer came from. If the answer came from the copy the desk saw yesterday, the clerk says that those words, and says that the copy is one version behind the working book. The clerk does not describe that copy as if it were the book Jordan has open. The clerk does not read the working book aloud and call it the copy the desk saw yesterday. The note that goes back to Morgan Ibarra or Dana Okonkwo names the copy in the first sentence.

If the two copies disagree, the clerk writes both readings on the note and writes which reading came from which copy. A clinic name on the copy the desk saw yesterday may differ from the clinic name in the working book. The clerk records each name with the copy it was read from. This issue does not say which name prevails. This issue does not tell the desk to show one of them on a screen. The disagreement stays visible because the copy is one version behind, and the procedure is to name the copy.

The copy is refreshed when Jordan sets the day's working book aside at the close of the office day and the next office morning's side copy is taken from it. Until that refresh, the side copy remains the copy the desk saw yesterday. Omar Farouk does not refresh it at night. A Friday close leaves Friday's version as the copy the desk will still be looking at until Monday's office work moves the working book. Weekend tray packets are not accepts and do not appear on either copy until Jordan accepts them on an office day.

## What the clerk says at the counter of this office

A member does not receive the board count as a promise about another office's display. If a person at Cascade asks whether a referral has been accepted, Jordan or the records clerk looks at the working book, not at the copy the desk saw yesterday, and answers yes or no from the mark. The answer is about the hospital record at Cascade Seattle. It is not a reading of a Cedar Ridge file, not a reading of a Harbor Coverage reply, and not a reading of a letter held by Priya Shah at Fraser Burnaby.

If the question is what the desk was told, the clerk uses the copy the desk saw yesterday and says so. The two answers are allowed to differ for so long as the copy is one version behind. The clerk does not smooth them into one sentence. The clerk does not pick a time of day and declare it the time a screen should carry. The working book has an office date on the accept. The copy has the office date of the version it still shows. Both dates are written when both are asked for. Neither date is awarded to a screen by this issue.
