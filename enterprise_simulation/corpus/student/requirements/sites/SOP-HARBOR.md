# Harbor Coverage answers

Northline Access Collaborative
Harbor Coverage, payer
Supervisor: Lena Brooks, Seattle
Issue date: 15 September 2026
Author: Lena Brooks, Harbor Coverage supervisor
Procedure owner: Lena Brooks

## What this issue is

This issue is the procedure for a coverage answer at Harbor Coverage. Lena Brooks wrote it on 15 September 2026. Harbor Coverage is the payer. The correspondence unit that issues answers under this issue works Monday through Friday. That is the office week. Saturday and Sunday are not days on which this unit issues a new answer. A check that is still open at the Friday close stays open through the weekend as whatever answer, if any, was already recorded. It is not completed on Saturday by a shorter word. Ruth Alvarez is a clerk in this unit and follows these steps. The other clerks on Lena Brooks's staff follow the same steps.

The Vancouver desk may ask for an answer before its own Monday open. Morgan Ibarra supervises that desk. Dana Okonkwo directs it. Their office week is also Monday through Friday, in America/Vancouver. A question that arrives from that desk on a weekend is read on the next office morning of this unit. Lena does not authorize a weekend answer so that a desk screen can be filled. This issue does not name a clock for a screen. It says what this unit records, in which currency, and what the partner message is still doing.

## The three answers

Harbor records one of three answers on a coverage check. The words are yes, no, and timeout. They are different answers. A clerk does not turn one into another because a desk would like a shorter sentence. A clerk does not add a fourth word and call it an answer. Covered is not an answer on this issue. Eligible is not an answer on the Harbor row. The three words above are the list.

The clerk opens a check with the referral id, the member region, and the request that came in. The clerk does not open a check by copying the previous referral's answer onto the new referral. Each check is its own row. Ruth Alvarez writes the row. Lena reviews a row when a clerk asks, and when a partner message is about to leave the unit.

A yes is the word yes written on the row. With the word yes the clerk writes the window and the currency. The window is a start and an end, both written on the same row as the yes. A yes without its dates is not the answer this unit recorded. A clerk who has a yes and has not yet written the dates has not finished the row. The row stays unfinished until the dates are on it. The clerk does not send the yes ahead of the dates and promise to add the dates later.

A no is the word no written on the row. The clerk writes no when the check came back no. The clerk does not leave the answer blank and hope a reader will decide that silence was a no. A no is not a yes that the clerk was too tired to finish. A no is not a timeout. If the dates of a refusal are part of what the check returned, the clerk writes those dates beside the no. If the clerk writes no, the word on the row is no.

A timeout is the third answer. The check did not come back with a yes and did not come back with a no. The line did not answer. The clerk writes timeout on the row. A timeout has no window. The clerk does not write a start. The clerk does not write an end. The clerk does not write a count of days. The absence of dates is not a window the desk may pencil in, and it is not a window this unit forgot. There is no window on a timeout. This issue does not state a number of seconds, a number of minutes, or a span of dates that would turn the timeout into something else. The timeout is the answer.

A timeout is not a yes. Lena Brooks has said so to the staff, and this issue says so again. A timeout is not a no. A clerk who is asked to treat the member as covered, or to keep a screen simple, or to let the member move on a slogan, does not rewrite the timeout. Morgan Ibarra may ask. Dana Okonkwo may ask. The row stays timeout. The clerk does not put a window on it in order to be kind. The clerk does not tell the desk that the member is covered underneath the timeout. There is no underneath. The answer is timeout, and the timeout has no window.

## Currency

Harbor Coverage keeps CAD and USD. The currency written on a row follows the member region on that row.

When the member region is BC, the clerk writes CAD. A British Columbia member is a CAD row. The clerk writes the code CAD on the same row as the answer. On a yes, the CAD code sits with the word yes and with the window. On a no, the CAD code sits with the word no. On a timeout, the clerk may record that the member region is BC, and the clerk still does not invent a window. The currency code does not turn a timeout into a yes.

When the member region is WA, the clerk writes USD. A Washington member is a USD row. The clerk writes the code USD on the same row as the answer. On a yes, the USD code sits with the word yes and with the window. On a no, the USD code sits with the word no. A timeout for a WA member is still a timeout with no window.

The clerk does not swap the codes to match a board printed in the other currency. Chris Pell may write from finance about an amount at Cascade Seattle and an amount on a payer row. This issue does not edit a currency to make those amounts look alike. A BC member stays CAD. A WA member stays USD. Cedar Ridge is a clinic in Washington. A member's region is the region on the coverage row, not the region of the clinic someone hopes to visit. A BC member who asks about a Washington clinic is still a BC member on the Harbor row, and the currency on that row is still CAD. A WA member is still USD. The clerk does not split one row across both codes.

The clerk reads the region from the check in front of them. The clerk does not inherit the region from the previous row on the desk. If the region is missing, the clerk does not guess BC or WA from the telephone accent or from the clinic named in a sentence. The row waits until the region is known. Lena is asked. No currency is written until the region is written.

## What Lena has told the staff

Lena Brooks has told the clerks, including Ruth Alvarez, not to paste the word eligible into a partner message. She has told them not to drop the dates. The instruction is standing. It applies to a yes, which has a window that must travel with the answer. It applies to a no, whose word must remain no. It applies to a timeout, which has no window and must not be dressed as eligible.

The partner message, when the clerks follow the instruction, carries the answer word that is on the row. If the answer is yes, the message carries yes and it carries the start and the end. If the answer is no, the message carries no. If the answer is timeout, the message carries timeout and it carries no window. The message does not carry the word eligible. The message does not leave the dates off a yes. The clerks have been told this in the unit, and this issue writes it down.

A clerk who is composing the message reads the row and then reads the message before it is released. The words on the message have to match the row. Dates that are on the row have to be on the message. The clerk does not paste a single comforting word over the row in order to help the Vancouver desk. June Lau and Noel Park may be waiting. They wait. The row is the answer. The message is supposed to repeat the row.

## What the partner message still does

The partner message still pastes the word eligible. The partner message still drops the dates. Lena has told the staff not to do either thing. The instruction stands, and the message that leaves this unit still does both. A reader who looks at the message the partner actually receives will see the word eligible, and will not see the window that was on a yes. That is the message as it still goes out on the date of this issue.

This issue does not describe a change to a program that would stop the message. It records the instruction Lena gave, and it records that the partner message still pastes eligible and still drops the dates. Clerks remain under the instruction. The message has not come into line with the instruction. Both of those sentences are part of the procedure, because a clerk who pretends the message already matches the row will mis-brief the desk.

When a clerk sees that the partner message says eligible, the clerk does not copy eligible back onto the Harbor row. The row keeps yes, or no, or timeout, whichever was recorded. Eligible on the message is not a new answer and is not a yes. When a clerk sees that the dates are absent from the message, the clerk does not decide that the row never had dates. If the row is a yes, the dates are still the dates on the row. If the row is a timeout, there were no dates to drop, and the message must not be treated as having created a window by saying eligible. The clerk does not fill a timeout with dates remembered from a different referral. The clerk does not tell Morgan Ibarra that the member is covered because the partner message says eligible.

If the desk has already been shown the partner message, Ruth Alvarez or the clerk who owns the row sends the desk back to the row: the answer word, the currency, the member region, and the window if the answer is a yes. The clerk says that the partner message still pastes eligible and still drops the dates, and that Lena has told the staff not to do that. The clerk does not defend the message as a second, shorter answer. The message is what the partner received. The row is what Harbor recorded. This issue does not collapse them.

A timeout that the message has rendered as eligible is still a timeout on the row. It still has no window. It is still not a yes. A clerk who is pressured to "just say covered" because the message already says eligible refuses. The pressure does not edit the row. Lena supports that refusal. She has already told the staff not to paste the word. She is not withdrawing the instruction because the message still ignores it.

## Office week and the desk

During Monday through Friday the unit issues answers and replies to questions about answers already issued. A reply quotes the row. It quotes the currency required by the member region, CAD for BC and USD for WA. It quotes the window when the answer is yes. It states that a timeout has no window and is not a yes. It does not quote the partner message as if the word eligible were the row.

After Friday close, the unit does not issue a fresh yes to quiet a weekend. A timeout recorded on Friday is still a timeout on Saturday. It does not grow a window overnight. Monday's first work is the row as it stood, not the partner message. If the partner message over the weekend still says eligible and still lacks dates, Monday's clerk says so, and then reads the row.

Chris Pell is not given a currency other than the currency this section requires. A finance board that shows a different code is a finance board. Harbor's row is written as this issue says. Jordan Hale's board at Cascade Seattle counts accepts at that hospital. It is not a coverage answer. Sam Nguyen's nightly file at Cedar Ridge is not a coverage answer. Anita Desai's clinic day at Fraser Burnaby is not a coverage answer. None of those offices is asked, by this issue, to repair the partner message.
