# Fraser Burnaby clinic day

Northline Access Collaborative
Fraser Burnaby, hospital, British Columbia
Time zone: America/Vancouver
Issue date: 3 August 2026
Author: Anita Desai, clinic manager
Procedure owner: Anita Desai

## What this issue is

This issue is the clinic-day procedure for Fraser Burnaby. Anita Desai wrote it on 3 August 2026. Fraser Burnaby is a hospital in British Columbia. The clinic day in this issue is an office day, Monday through Friday, in America/Vancouver. Saturday and Sunday are not clinic days under this issue. A member who reaches the clinic by telephone on Saturday or Sunday is not given a clinic-day answer. The name and the number are taken for a callback on the next office morning. The Vancouver desk of Northline Access Collaborative keeps the same office week, Monday through Friday. This issue does not open a Fraser clinic day to match a night file at Cedar Ridge, and it does not open one because Cascade Seattle has a night tray.

Priya Shah, MD, is the clinician named in this issue for the letters of the clinic day. Alex Kim, MD, is the on-call physician. Alex may be called when Priya is off the floor. The letter rules below apply to Alex as they apply to Priya. Evan Cho is the privacy officer at the Vancouver desk. Anita calls Evan when someone asks to move a letter off the clinician's record. Riley Chen owns the 2024 assistant. The assistant is not a clinician and is not given the letter. Noel Park, June Lau, and Morgan Ibarra work the Vancouver desk. They may telephone the clinic. They receive the same limits as a person standing at the Fraser counter.

## Opening the clinic day

Anita opens the clinic book at the start of the office day. She writes the date, the site Fraser Burnaby, and the names of the clinician on duty. On a day Priya Shah is on duty, Priya's name is the clinician name. On a day Priya is away and Alex Kim is covering, Alex's name is written as on-call cover, and the letter cabinet stays under the clinician rule. Anita does not open the clinic book on Saturday or Sunday.

The reception counter is staffed for members who have a clinic-day appointment and for members who arrive asking whether they are in the right hospital. The counter confirms the member is at Fraser Burnaby. The counter does not confirm a booking at Cedar Ridge. Cedar Ridge releases a nightly file and has no slot API. A sentence about Cedar Ridge is referred to the Vancouver desk, not answered from the Fraser book. The counter does not confirm an accept on the Cascade Seattle board. That accept is Jordan Hale's act. The counter says, when asked, that Fraser Burnaby is a different hospital.

The first hour is for arrival. Charts that belong to the day are already with the clinician, or they are fetched to the clinician. They are not stacked on the reception counter for a member to read. A chart that cannot be found is reported to Anita before the member is told that the visit will proceed without it. Anita decides whether the visit waits. The clerk does not invent a chart from a telephone note.

## Letters stay with clinicians

A letter about a member is a clinical document. It stays with the clinicians who are responsible for it. On this clinic day the clinician is Priya Shah, or Alex Kim when Alex is the on-call cover named in the clinic book. The letter is kept in the clinician's record. It is not placed in the reception folder. It is not placed in a pouch for the Vancouver desk. It is not read aloud at the counter. It is not summarized into a sentence the member can carry away. It is not dictated to Noel Park or June Lau over the telephone. It is not handed to Morgan Ibarra. It is not shown to Dana Okonkwo as a substitute for a desk script. It is not given to Chris Pell. It is not given to Riley Chen, and it is not given to the 2024 assistant.

The audience of the letter remains the clinician. A clerk who can see that a letter exists still does not read it to a member. Seeing the folder is not permission to speak the folder. If a member asks what the letter says, the clerk says that the letter stays with the clinician and offers to ask Priya Shah, or Alex Kim if Alex is the cover that day, whether the clinician will speak with the member. The clerk does not preview the answer. The clerk does not quote a phrase in order to be helpful while the clinician is in another room.

If Riley Chen, or anyone else, asks that the assistant read the letter during the clinic day, during the closure of the clinic, or before the next office morning, the answer on this issue is that the letter stays with clinicians. Anita does not approve the read. She tells the asker to speak to Evan Cho. She does not put the letter text into a message while she waits for Evan. A request that arrives after Friday close waits, with the letter unmoved, until Monday. The weekend does not loosen the rule because the clinician is away from the ward.

At the end of each clinic day the clinician returns letters to the clinician cabinet. Anita confirms the cabinet. A letter that was on a desk in a clinic room goes back before the room is left. A letter does not go home in a coat pocket. A letter does not go to the night file at any other site. The Friday close uses the same cabinet check. Monday begins with the letters still in the cabinet, not with a stack that spent the weekend at the Vancouver desk.

## The draft hours page

A draft hours page for Fraser Burnaby exists. The page is marked draft. It may sit in the document room or on a screen a clerk can open. Its presence is a fact of this clinic. It is not a script.

The draft hours page must not be read to a member. The clerk does not read its opening time, its closing time, or any sentence of its body. The clerk does not read it because the member is in a hurry. The clerk does not read it because the member asks for "whatever is on the screen." The clerk does not hand the member a print of the draft. The clerk does not leave the draft face up on the counter. The clerk does not turn the screen so the member can read the draft while the clerk looks for a different page. If the draft is the page already open when the member asks, the clerk closes it before speaking. The clerk then says that a draft page is not being read.

The same prohibition applies on the telephone. June Lau or Noel Park may call from the Vancouver desk and ask what to tell a member about Fraser hours. The clerk at Fraser does not read the draft into that call. Morgan Ibarra may ask for a sentence the desk can repeat. The draft is not that sentence. Dana Okonkwo may ask for one number before a weekend. The draft is not that number. Anita does not authorize an exception for a long weekend, for a night when Cedar Ridge is filing, or for a morning when the published pages are slow to open.

Staff may open the draft only to confirm that the page in front of them is the draft, so that they do not read it by mistake. That check is silent. The member is not told the words that were on it. Bobbi Tran keeps logs for Northline and is not asked, by this issue, to read the draft to a member or to place it on a spoken script.

## Published pages

Fraser Burnaby may have more than one published hours page. Published pages are the pages that are not the draft. This issue does not list them, and it does not rank them.

Anita Desai does not say which published page is current. She does not name a page in this issue. She does not point a clerk at a binder tab, a version, a season, or a close time and call that the page to use. A clerk who wants this issue to settle the question will not find the settlement. The omission is deliberate. Anita has left the sentence out.

When a member asks when the clinic closes, or when a service at Fraser Burnaby ends, the clerk does not answer from the draft, and the clerk does not choose a published page on Anita's behalf. The clerk tells the member that the closing time is not being read from a draft, and that this clinic-day issue does not name the published page. The clerk offers to take the member's name and a telephone number for a callback. The callback is made on an office day, Monday through Friday, when Anita can be asked. If Anita is in the building and free, the clerk asks Anita in person and waits. If Anita answers with a page, the clerk reads only what Anita just said on that occasion, and the clerk does not treat that spoken answer as a standing choice written into this issue. This issue remains silent about which published page is current.

A member who brings a printed page from home is not told that the brought page is the current one, and is not told that it is retired. The clerk takes a copy of the brought page for Anita, does not read it aloud as the clinic's answer, and does not compare it aloud with the draft. The comparison, if Anita later makes one, is Anita's later act. It is not a sentence this issue already contains.

If the Vancouver desk has been telling members a close time from a card of its own, that card is the desk's card. This issue does not adopt the card, and it does not correct the card. Noel Park's question about what Fraser will stand behind is answered with the draft rule and with Anita's silence on the published page. The desk is not given a Fraser close time by these pages.

## Through the middle of the day

After arrival, the member is seen by the clinician on the book. The clerk walks the member to the clinic room and does not carry the letter in view. The clinician conducts the visit. Anything the clinician chooses to say to the member about care is the clinician's speech. It is not a reading of the draft hours page, and it is not a release of the letter into the reception record. When the visit ends, the member returns to the counter for the ordinary close of the visit: the next office-day appointment if the clinician has written one in the clinic book, or a statement that no further appointment was written. The clerk reads the appointment from the clinic book. The clerk does not read it from the draft hours page. The clerk does not invent an appointment to fill a silence.

A member who asks about Harbor Coverage is not given a coverage answer from this clinic. Harbor Coverage is the payer. Ruth Alvarez and Lena Brooks work that unit. The Fraser counter does not say yes, no, or any other coverage word. The counter takes the referral id, if the member has one, and tells the member the question belongs with the payer and with the Vancouver desk.

A member who asks about a person id, a handoff, or a previous caller is not answered from this issue. The handoff queue is a Vancouver desk matter. The Fraser clerk does not look up a person id in order to be useful. If the member's chart and the member at the counter do not match, the clerk stops and calls Anita. The clerk does not continue the visit on a chart that belongs to someone who was here earlier.

## Close of the clinic day

At the end of the office day Anita confirms three things. Letters that were in use are back in the clinician cabinet. The draft hours page is not on the reception screen and is not in the night pouch. No published page has been marked, by this issue or by a clerk's note, as the current page. The Friday close uses the same three checks. The clinic day then stays shut until Monday. A question about hours that was left for callback is called on the next office morning. It is not answered from the draft on Saturday in order to clear the list.
