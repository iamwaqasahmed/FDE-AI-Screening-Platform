# Vancouver desk handoff queue

Northline Access Collaborative
Vancouver desk, British Columbia
Time zone: America/Vancouver
Issue date: 6 January 2027
Author: Morgan Ibarra, desk supervisor
Procedure owner: Morgan Ibarra

## What this issue is

This issue is the procedure for the handoff queue at the Vancouver desk. Morgan Ibarra wrote it on 6 January 2027. The desk is the Northline desk in Vancouver. The time zone is America/Vancouver. The office week is Monday through Friday. A handoff is worked on those days only. Saturday and Sunday are not office days. A call that arrives after the Friday close waits for Monday. It is not queued on the weekend as if the counter were open. Cedar Ridge may release a nightly file on a weekend, and that file is not a reason to open this queue. Cascade Seattle keeps its own records office. Harbor Coverage keeps its own answers. This queue is the desk's queue.

Noel Park and June Lau follow this issue on every office day they take a call that must be handed on. Any other clerk assigned to the Vancouver desk follows the same steps. Dana Okonkwo receives the issue as operations director and does not change a step during a live call. Evan Cho is called when a person id has been used for the wrong person. Bobbi Tran keeps the three logs. This issue does not ask Bobbi to change a program.

The queue carries a call from the clerk who has it to the clerk who will take it, or from the phone bay to the counter. The thing that moves is a slip. The slip names one call. The slip carries one person id, and that person id is the person id of the caller on that call. The rest of this issue is how the slip is opened, how the person id is obtained, and what the clerk does when the previous caller's person id is still sitting on the new call.

## The slip

Each call that enters the queue has its own slip. The slip has a call mark, a person id, the office date, and the time of day the slip was written at the desk. The call mark identifies the call. It is not a person id. Two calls may sit one after another and still be two slips. The clerk does not reuse the previous slip for the next caller. A worn slip is copied onto a fresh slip only when the caller is the same call still in progress, and the copy is checked under the section below before it is released.

The person id is written by the clerk from the identification made on this call. The clerk asks for the identification this caller is giving now. The clerk writes that person id on the fresh slip. The clerk does not look at the previous slip and continue its person id onto the new one. The previous slip may still be on the board. It stays the record of the previous call. It is not a template.

If the caller does not give a person id, the slip is not released. The clerk does not borrow an id from the call that just ended so that the queue will have something in the field. The clerk tells the caller that the handoff cannot go forward without the identification, and the clerk tells Morgan Ibarra. The call may be ended or it may be held at the same clerk. It is not handed on with an empty person id, and it is not handed on with someone else's person id.

A referral id, a clinic name, or a coverage word may be noted on the slip if the caller stated them. Those notes do not fill the person id. A member region of BC is not a person id. A file id from Cedar Ridge is not a person id. An accept at Cascade Seattle is not a person id. A Harbor Coverage answer is not a person id. The person id field is only the person id obtained on this call.

## A new call

When one call ends and another begins, the clerk finishes the slip of the call that is ending. That slip keeps the person id of that caller. The clerk then takes a fresh slip for the caller now on the line. The person id field on the fresh slip starts blank. The clerk obtains the person id from the caller now on the line and writes it in the blank. The clerk does this even when the new caller begins speaking before the previous slip has been filed. Speed is not a reason to leave the old person id in place.

Before the fresh slip is released into the queue, the clerk reads the person id on the fresh slip and the person id on the previous slip. The clerk reads them as two strings. The check is the clerk's, at the desk, on the paper.

If the two strings differ, and the person id on the fresh slip is the one just obtained from the caller now waiting, the clerk may release the slip. The previous caller's person id stays on the previous slip only.

If the two strings are the same, the clerk stops. A match is not, by itself, proof that the id belongs to the new call. The clerk asks whether this caller is the same person as the caller who just finished. If it is a different person, the fresh slip has kept the previous caller's person id, and that is the failure this issue forbids. The clerk strikes that person id on the fresh slip, writes the person id obtained from the caller now waiting, and reads the two slips again. The slip is not released while it still carries the previous caller's person id.

If it is the same person calling again, the person id may match because it is that person's id. The match is allowed only after the clerk has obtained the id again on this call, from this caller, and has written a short note on the fresh slip: the person id was obtained again on this call. A match that was produced by leaving the old id untouched is not that note. The clerk does not write the note in order to bless a copy. The clerk writes the note only after a fresh identification.

The rule is the same when the handoff is from Noel Park to June Lau, from June Lau to Noel Park, from either of them to Morgan Ibarra, or from the phone bay to the counter. The clerk who releases the slip is the clerk who does the check. The clerk who receives the slip reads the person id aloud against the caller now present before speaking about that caller's matter. If the receiver cannot do that reading, the receiver does not use the slip. The receiver sends it back. A queue that has already moved the slip is not a reason to continue with the wrong person.

## What Morgan has seen

Morgan Ibarra has seen a new call keep the previous caller's person id. She has seen it on this handoff queue. The new slip left the clerk with the person id of the caller who had just ended. The next clerk had the new caller in front of them and the previous person id on the slip. Morgan saw the pair. She is not guessing at a story from another site.

The procedure says that must not happen. Seeing it does not make it allowed. A slip that has already gone out in that state is pulled back. The clerk who released it, or Morgan if that clerk has left the chair, strikes the previous caller's person id and writes the person id of the caller now waiting, obtained again from that caller. The pulled slip is not returned to the queue until the check in the previous section passes. If the caller now waiting has already been spoken to under the wrong person id, the clerk stops the conversation, tells Morgan, and Morgan tells Evan Cho. The clerk does not finish the call "since it has already started."

Morgan records, in the desk book, that she has seen the previous person id survive onto a new call. The record names the office date and the two call marks. It does not name a repair to a machine. The record is so the next office morning knows the check is not theoretical. Noel Park and June Lau read that record when they open the queue on Monday.

## What this issue does not describe

The clerk's work is the slip, the fresh identification, the comparison, the strike, and the pull-back. That is the whole of the correction this issue authorizes.

This procedure does not describe a fix in software. Bobbi Tran keeps the three logs. She is not asked to change them. A line in a log is not permission to release a slip that still carries the previous caller's person id. Harper Singh left in March 2026. This issue does not assign anyone a change to a program.

If the same failure appears again on a later call, the clerk repeats the pull-back and the fresh identification. The repetition does not become a specification for a program. Morgan may write again in the desk book that she has seen it. The procedure remains the check before release. The procedure still says the new call must not keep the previous caller's person id.

## Office week

The queue opens when Morgan opens the counter on an office morning and closes when Morgan closes it. Monday's first slip is a fresh slip. It does not inherit the person id from Friday's last slip. The clerk who opens Monday puts a blank person id field on the first call and obtains the id from the caller who is actually there. Friday's last slip is filed with Friday's calls. It is not left on top of Monday's pad as a convenience.

A call that arrives after Friday close is not given a slip until Monday. The person who calls on Saturday is not written onto Friday's last person id. The weekend message, if one was taken by a recording, is opened on Monday as its own slip, with its own identification. Cedar Ridge may have filed at 21:00 America/Los_Angeles on that weekend. That file does not identify the caller on the Vancouver slip. Sam Nguyen's file id is not written into the person id field.

A handoff that is unfinished at the Friday close is not left "almost released" so that Monday can skip the check. Monday begins the check again. The caller, if they return, is identified again. The person id is obtained again. The previous slip is only a previous slip.

## What the slip is not

The time written on the slip is the time the clerk wrote the slip at this desk. This issue leaves that time on the slip. It does not tell a screen, a board, a mart, a nightly file, or a payer row to use it. Jordan Hale's board at Cascade Seattle counts accepts in that office. This queue does not add to that board. Lena Brooks's unit at Harbor Coverage records yes, no, or timeout. This queue does not change those answers, and it does not paste a coverage word onto a slip in place of a person id. Anita Desai's clinic day at Fraser Burnaby keeps letters with clinicians. This queue does not carry a letter. Priya Shah is not asked to confirm a person id from a letter. Alex Kim is not asked. The slip is identification for the handoff, obtained on the call, checked against the previous slip, and withheld when the previous caller's person id is still on it.
