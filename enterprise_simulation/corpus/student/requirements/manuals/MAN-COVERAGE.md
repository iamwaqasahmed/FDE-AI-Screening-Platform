# Harbor Coverage answers and the copy a clerk makes

Northline Access Collaborative
Harbor Coverage, payer
Supervisor: Lena Brooks, Seattle
Issue date: 12 November 2026
Author: Lena Brooks, Harbor Coverage supervisor
Procedure owner: Lena Brooks

## What this manual is

This manual is the operating procedure for a coverage answer at Harbor Coverage, and for the copy a clerk makes when a message leaves the unit. Lena Brooks wrote it on 12 November 2026. Harbor Coverage is the payer. The correspondence unit that issues answers under this manual works Monday through Friday. That is the office week. Saturday and Sunday are not days on which this unit issues a new answer. A check that is still open at the Friday close stays open through the weekend as whatever answer, if any, was already recorded. It is not completed on Saturday by a shorter word. Ruth Alvarez is a clerk in this unit and follows these steps. The other clerks on Lena's staff follow the same steps.

The Vancouver desk may ask for an answer before its own Monday open. Morgan Ibarra supervises that desk. Dana Okonkwo directs it. Their office week is also Monday through Friday, in America/Vancouver. A question that arrives from that desk on a weekend is read on the next office morning of this unit. Lena does not authorize a weekend answer so that a desk screen can be filled. This manual does not name a clock for a screen. It says what this unit records, which currency follows the member region, and what staff must not drop when they copy a message.

Cedar Ridge files at night, including weekends, and has no slot API. A nightly file is not a coverage answer. Jordan Hale's board at Cascade Seattle counts accepts at that hospital. It is not a coverage answer. Anita Desai's clinic day at Fraser Burnaby is not a coverage answer. None of those offices repairs a Harbor row.

## The three answers

Harbor records one of three answers on a coverage check. The words are yes, no, and timeout. They are different answers. A clerk does not turn one into another because a desk would like a shorter sentence. A clerk does not add a fourth word and call it an answer. Covered is not an answer on this manual. Eligible is not an answer on the Harbor row. The three words above are the list.

The clerk opens a check with the referral id, the member region, and the request that came in. The clerk does not open a check by copying the previous referral's answer onto the new referral. Each check is its own row. Ruth Alvarez writes the row. Lena reviews a row when a clerk asks, and when a message is about to leave the unit.

A yes is the word yes written on the row. With the word yes the clerk writes the window and the currency. The window is a start and an end, both written on the same row as the yes. A yes without its dates is not the answer this unit recorded. A clerk who has a yes and has not yet written the dates has not finished the row. The row stays unfinished until the dates are on it. The clerk does not send the yes ahead of the dates and promise to add the dates later.

A no is the word no written on the row. The clerk writes no when the check came back no. The clerk does not leave the answer blank and hope a reader will decide that silence was a no. A no is not a yes that the clerk was too tired to finish. A no is not a timeout. If the dates of a refusal are part of what the check returned, the clerk writes those dates beside the no. If the clerk writes no, the word on the row is no.

A timeout is the third answer. The check did not come back with a yes and did not come back with a no. The line did not answer. The clerk writes timeout on the row. A timeout has no window. The clerk does not write a start. The clerk does not write an end. The clerk does not write a count of days. The absence of dates is not a window the desk may pencil in, and it is not a window this unit forgot. There is no window on a timeout. This manual does not state a number of seconds, a number of minutes, or a span of dates that would turn the timeout into something else. The timeout is the answer.

A timeout is not a yes. Lena Brooks has said so to the staff, and this manual says so again. A timeout is not a no. A clerk who is asked to treat the member as covered, or to keep a screen simple, or to let the member move on a slogan, does not rewrite the timeout. Morgan Ibarra may ask. Dana Okonkwo may ask. The row stays timeout. The clerk does not put a window on it in order to be kind. The clerk does not tell the desk that the member is covered underneath the timeout. There is no underneath. The answer is timeout, and the timeout has no window.

## Currency by member region

Harbor Coverage keeps CAD and USD. The currency written on a row follows the member region on that row.

When the member region is BC, the clerk writes CAD. A British Columbia member is a CAD row. The clerk writes the code CAD on the same row as the answer. On a yes, the CAD code sits with the word yes and with the window. On a no, the CAD code sits with the word no. On a timeout, the clerk records that the member region is BC and writes CAD with that region. The currency code does not turn a timeout into a yes. The timeout still has no window.

When the member region is WA, the clerk writes USD. A Washington member is a USD row. The clerk writes the code USD on the same row as the answer. On a yes, the USD code sits with the word yes and with the window. On a no, the USD code sits with the word no. A timeout for a WA member is still a timeout with no window, and the currency code on the row is still USD.

The clerk does not swap the codes to match a board printed in the other currency. Chris Pell may write from finance about an amount at Cascade Seattle and an amount on a payer row. This manual does not edit a currency to make those amounts look alike. A BC member stays CAD. A WA member stays USD. Cedar Ridge is a clinic in Washington. A member's region is the region on the coverage row, not the region of the clinic someone hopes to visit. A BC member who asks about a Washington clinic is still a BC member on the Harbor row, and the currency on that row is still CAD. A WA member is still USD. The clerk does not split one row across both codes.

The clerk reads the region from the check in front of them. The clerk does not inherit the region from the previous row on the desk. If the region is missing, the clerk does not guess BC or WA from the telephone accent or from the clinic named in a sentence. The row waits until the region is known. Lena is asked. No currency is written until the region is written.

Example. A check names member region BC. The answer is yes. The clerk writes yes, writes CAD, and writes a start date and an end date on that same row. A colleague points at Cedar Ridge and says the clinic is in Washington, so the row should say USD. The colleague is pointing at the clinic. The rule points at the member region. The row stays CAD.

Example. A check names member region WA. The answer is no. The clerk writes no and writes USD. The clerk does not borrow a window from a yes that was on the pad an hour earlier. The word on the row is no. The code on the row is USD.

Example. A check names member region BC. The line does not come back. The clerk writes timeout, writes the region BC, writes CAD, and writes no start and no end. A later reader who sees the currency code and invents a window has left this manual. The currency code is not a window.

## The rule staff break

This section is written as the rule staff break. The rule is in force. Staff break it when they copy a message. Both of those facts are the procedure, because a clerk who pretends the copy already matches the row will mis-brief the desk.

When a clerk copies a message from the row, the clerk must not drop the answer word. Yes stays yes. No stays no. Timeout stays timeout. The clerk must not drop the currency code that follows the member region. CAD stays on a BC row. USD stays on a WA row. The clerk must not drop the dates. On a yes, the start and the end travel with the word yes. On a no, any dates that were written beside the no travel with the word no. On a timeout, there are no dates to carry, and the clerk must not drop the fact that the window is absent by writing dates the row does not have.

The clerk must not drop those parts and paste the word eligible over the gap. Eligible is not a substitute for yes. Eligible is not a substitute for no. Eligible is not a substitute for timeout. A message that says eligible and has lost its dates is a copy that broke the rule. It is not a new answer.

Lena has told the staff, including Ruth Alvarez, not to paste eligible and not to drop the dates. She has told them not to drop the currency code. The instruction is standing. It applies when the message goes to a partner. It applies when the message is copied to the Vancouver desk. It applies when a clerk copies the row onto a pad for a phone call. The words on the copy have to match the row. Dates that are on the row have to be on the copy. The currency code that is on the row has to be on the copy. The clerks have been told this in the unit, and this manual writes it down.

Staff break that rule. The copy that leaves this unit still pastes the word eligible. The copy still drops the dates. The copy still leaves the currency code off when a clerk is hurrying to make the message short. Lena has watched a yes leave the unit as the single word eligible, with the start gone, the end gone, and the CAD or USD gone. She has watched a timeout leave as eligible, which is a worse break, because a timeout had no dates and the copy invented the look of a finished yes. The rule stands. The break is what the copy still does. This manual does not describe a change to a program that would stop the copy. It records the rule, and it records that staff break it.

A clerk who is about to release a message reads the row and then reads the message. If the message has dropped the dates, the clerk does not release it. If the message has dropped the currency code, the clerk does not release it. If the message says eligible, the clerk does not release it. The clerk rebuilds the message from the row: the answer word, the currency, the member region, and the window if the answer is a yes. Then the clerk reads the message again. The rule is the rebuild. The break is what happens when the clerk releases the short copy anyway. Lena does not treat the short copy as compliance. She treats it as the break this section names.

## What the clerk does after the break has already left

When a clerk sees that a message already gone says eligible, the clerk does not copy eligible back onto the Harbor row. The row keeps yes, or no, or timeout, whichever was recorded. Eligible on the message is not a new answer and is not a yes. When a clerk sees that the dates are absent from the message, the clerk does not decide that the row never had dates. If the row is a yes, the dates are still the dates on the row. If the row is a timeout, there were no dates to drop, and the message must not be treated as having created a window by saying eligible. The clerk does not fill a timeout with dates remembered from a different referral. The clerk does not tell Morgan Ibarra that the member is covered because the message says eligible.

If the currency code was dropped on the way out, the clerk does not invent a code to match whatever board is on the caller's desk. The clerk returns to the row and reads the code that follows the member region. A BC row is still CAD. A WA row is still USD. The dropped code on the message is part of the break. It is not a correction of the row.

If the desk has already been shown the broken message, Ruth Alvarez or the clerk who owns the row sends the desk back to the row. The clerk says the answer word, the currency, the member region, and the window if the answer is a yes. The clerk says that staff break the rule when they copy, that the copy pastes eligible and drops the dates, and that Lena has told the staff not to do that. The clerk does not defend the message as a second, shorter answer. The message is what the partner received. The row is what Harbor recorded. This manual does not collapse them.

A timeout that the message has rendered as eligible is still a timeout on the row. It still has no window. It is still not a yes. A clerk who is pressured to "just say covered" because the message already says eligible refuses. The pressure does not edit the row. Lena supports that refusal. She has already told the staff not to paste the word. She is not withdrawing the instruction because the copy still ignores it.

## Worked rows

A yes for a BC member is written yes, CAD, start, end. The clerk copies a message. The faithful copy contains those four parts and the region BC. The broken copy, the one staff still send, says eligible and stops. The row is not edited to match the broken copy. The desk, if it has seen eligible, is walked back to yes, CAD, and both dates.

A no for a WA member is written no, USD. The faithful copy says no and USD. A broken copy that says eligible has dropped the answer word and dropped the currency code. The row remains no. The clerk does not add a window to a no in order to make the broken copy look finished.

A timeout for a BC member is written timeout, region BC, CAD, and no dates. The faithful copy says timeout and says there is no window. It may carry CAD because the region is BC. It does not carry a start. It does not carry an end. A broken copy that says eligible is still a timeout underneath, and there is no underneath that means yes. The clerk does not pencil dates. The clerk does not tell the Vancouver desk to treat the member as covered for the weekend.

## Office week

During Monday through Friday the unit issues answers and replies to questions about answers already issued. A reply quotes the row. It quotes the currency required by the member region, CAD for BC and USD for WA. It quotes the window when the answer is yes. It states that a timeout has no window and is not a yes. It does not quote the broken message as if the word eligible were the row.

After Friday close, the unit does not issue a fresh yes to quiet a weekend. A timeout recorded on Friday is still a timeout on Saturday. It does not grow a window overnight. Monday's first work is the row as it stood, not the message staff broke on the way out. If that message still says eligible and still lacks dates, Monday's clerk says so, and then reads the row.

## Exceptions

If the member region is missing, no currency is written and no message is copied. The check waits for Lena. A clerk who copies a message from a row with no region, and who picks CAD or USD to be helpful, has broken the rule before the dates even come into question.

If a yes is unfinished because the dates are not on the row yet, the clerk does not copy a message that says yes alone. An unfinished yes is not released as a short yes. The break this manual describes is a drop from a finished row. A row that was never finished is not copied in order to look finished.

If two referrals are open on the desk and the clerk's copy takes the dates from the wrong row, the clerk does not send the message. The dates are part of the row they were written on. A window from the neighboring check is a different check. The clerk strikes the copy and builds it again from the row in front of them.

If finance asks the unit to change CAD to USD, or USD to CAD, so two amounts will look like one amount, the clerk refuses. Lena supports the refusal. The currency follows the member region. A board in another office is that office's board. This manual does not edit a code to produce a match, and it does not name an instant a screen may use. People argue about what a display should show. This manual records yes, no, or timeout, with the currency the region requires, and with the dates a yes actually has. It does not award the argument.

A Cedar Ridge file that arrives at night, including a weekend file, does not close a timeout and does not open a window. Sam Nguyen's file is not a Harbor answer. The Monday clerk reads the Harbor row as it stood.
