# Opening and closing Fraser Burnaby

Northline Access Collaborative
Fraser Burnaby, hospital, British Columbia
Time zone: America/Vancouver
Issue date: 2 January 2027
Author: Anita Desai, clinic manager
Procedure owner: Anita Desai

## What this manual is

This manual is the operating procedure for opening Fraser Burnaby and for closing Fraser Burnaby. Anita Desai wrote it on 2 January 2027. Fraser Burnaby is a hospital in British Columbia. The clinic day is an office day, Monday through Friday, in America/Vancouver. Saturday and Sunday are not clinic days under this manual. A member who reaches the clinic by telephone on Saturday or Sunday is not given a clinic-day answer. The name and the number are taken for a callback on the next office morning.

The Vancouver desk of Northline Access Collaborative keeps the same office week, Monday through Friday. This manual does not open a Fraser clinic day to match a night file at Cedar Ridge. Cedar Ridge releases a nightly file, including on weekends, and has no slot API. A file leaving that clinic at night is not a key to this building. Cascade Seattle keeps its own records. Harbor Coverage keeps its own answers. This manual is the Fraser open and the Fraser close.

Priya Shah, MD, is the clinician named for the letters of the clinic day. Alex Kim, MD, is the on-call physician. Evan Cho is the privacy officer at the Vancouver desk. Anita calls Evan when someone asks to move a letter off the clinician's record. Riley Chen owns the 2024 assistant. The assistant is not a clinician and is not given the letter. Noel Park, June Lau, and Morgan Ibarra work the Vancouver desk. They may telephone the clinic. They receive the same limits as a person standing at the Fraser counter. Dana Okonkwo directs that desk. She does not, from this manual, receive a close time to publish.

The date on this issue is a Saturday. The clinic is not open because the issue is dated. Monday is the next open. The steps below are the steps for that Monday and for every office day after it until a later issue replaces this one. No later issue is attached.

## Opening the clinic day

Anita opens the clinic book at the start of the office day. She writes the date, the site Fraser Burnaby, and the names of the clinician on duty. On a day Priya Shah is on duty, Priya's name is the clinician name. On a day Priya is away and Alex Kim is covering, Alex's name is written as on-call cover. The letter cabinet stays under the clinician rule either way. Anita does not open the clinic book on Saturday or Sunday. She does not open it on a holiday that the clinic has posted as closed, and she does not invent a posting in the margin of this manual.

Before the reception counter is unlocked, Anita confirms three opening facts. The letters that were in the clinician cabinet at the previous close are still in the cabinet. The draft hours pages are not on the reception screen. No page, draft or otherwise, has been marked by a clerk's note as the page this clinic is serving today. If any of the three facts fails, the counter stays locked until Anita has put it right. A clerk does not unlock the counter "just for arrivals" while a draft is still on the screen.

The reception counter is then staffed for members who have a clinic-day appointment and for members who arrive asking whether they are in the right hospital. The counter confirms the member is at Fraser Burnaby. The counter does not confirm a booking at Cedar Ridge. A sentence about Cedar Ridge is referred to the Vancouver desk, not answered from the Fraser book. The counter does not confirm an accept on the Cascade Seattle board. That accept is Jordan Hale's act. The counter says, when asked, that Fraser Burnaby is a different hospital.

The first hour is for arrival. Charts that belong to the day are already with the clinician, or they are fetched to the clinician. They are not stacked on the reception counter for a member to read. A chart that cannot be found is reported to Anita before the member is told that the visit will proceed without it. Anita decides whether the visit waits. The clerk does not invent a chart from a telephone note.

Opening includes the hours pages as objects in the room, not as a script. Anita looks at the draft long enough to see that it is marked draft, then closes it. She does not read it to the room. She does not read it to herself aloud. A silent check that the page is the draft is allowed. A performance of its times is not. Published pages may also be in the room. Opening does not include choosing one of them. That omission is stated again in the section on the hazard, because clerks feel the omission most sharply at the moment a member asks.

## What may be read aloud

The list of what may be read aloud is short. The clerk reads from this list and from nowhere else when the member is listening.

The clerk may read the member's name back, as the member gave it, to confirm the person at the counter. The clerk may read an appointment from the clinic book when the clinician has written that appointment in the clinic book: the office date and the time written there. The clerk may say that the member is at Fraser Burnaby. The clerk may say that a draft page is not being read. The clerk may say that this manual does not name a published page. The clerk may read back a telephone number the member has just given for a callback. The clerk may say that the callback will be on an office day, Monday through Friday.

If Anita is in the building and a clerk has asked her, in person, about a question this manual does not answer, the clerk may read aloud only the words Anita just spoke on that occasion. The clerk does not add a time Anita did not say. The clerk does not treat that spoken answer as a standing choice written into this manual. The next member does not inherit it. The next morning does not inherit it. The Vancouver desk does not inherit it as a card.

Priya may speak to the member in the clinic room about the care she is giving. That speech is the clinician's. It is not a reading of an hours page, and it is not a reading of the letter into the reception record. The reception clerk does not repeat Priya's clinical words at the counter afterward. Alex, when he is the on-call cover, is under the same limit. A letter is not read aloud at the counter. A letter is not read aloud on the telephone to Noel Park or June Lau. A letter is not read aloud to Morgan Ibarra. Screen text at the Vancouver desk is that desk's screen. It is not a script this counter borrows in order to sound informed.

The draft hours page is not on the list. No sentence of its body is on the list. Its opening time is not on the list. Its closing time is not on the list. A published page the clerk prefers is not on the list. An old close time is not on the list. A card the Vancouver desk has been reading to callers is not on the list. A figure on the 2024 assistant is not on the list. A close time a member remembers from a previous season is not on the list.

Example. A member asks when the clinic closes. The clerk says that a draft page is not being read, and that this manual does not name the published page. The clerk offers to take a name and a telephone number. If Anita is free, the clerk asks Anita in person and waits. If Anita answers with words on that occasion, the clerk reads those words and stops. The clerk does not then open the draft "to see if Anita was right."

Example. June Lau calls from the Vancouver desk and asks for a sentence the desk can repeat. The clerk does not read the draft into the call. The clerk does not read an old close time into the call. The clerk says what this manual allows: the draft is not being read, and the manual does not name the page. June may be disappointed. Disappointment is not an extra sentence.

Example. A member asks the clerk to read "whatever is on the screen." The screen is showing the draft. The clerk closes the draft before speaking. Then the clerk says that a draft page is not being read. The clerk does not turn the screen so the member can read the draft while the clerk looks for a different page. The clerk does not leave the draft face up on the counter. The clerk does not hand the member a print of the draft.

## Draft hours pages

A draft hours page for Fraser Burnaby exists. Other draft hours pages exist in the document room for other sites. A draft is marked draft. Its presence is a fact. It is not a script.

The draft must not be read to a member. The clerk does not read it because the member is in a hurry. The clerk does not read it because the line is long. The clerk does not read it because the published pages are slow to open. The clerk does not read it on a Saturday in order to clear a callback list. The clerk does not read it because Cedar Ridge is filing that night and someone wants Fraser to sound equally definite. Anita does not authorize an exception for a long weekend.

Staff may open a draft only to confirm that the page in front of them is the draft, so that they do not read it by mistake. That check is silent. The member is not told the words that were on it. Bobbi Tran keeps logs for Northline and is not asked, by this manual, to read a draft to a member or to place it on a spoken script.

If several drafts are open, the clerk closes all of them before speaking. One draft is not "more current" than another inside this manual, because this manual does not rank drafts and does not rank published pages. A clerk who sorts the drafts by date and reads the newest has left the manual. Newest is a fact about paper. It is not a permission to read aloud.

A draft that has been copied onto a card is still a draft. The card does not become readable because it is short. A draft that has been copied into a desk binder at Vancouver is still a draft. Morgan's bay does not convert it. If Anita learns that a draft sentence has been read aloud, she treats the reading as a break of this manual. She does not adopt the sentence afterward to avoid embarrassing the clerk. The member, if still reachable on an office day, is told that a draft was read and that this clinic is not standing on the draft. Anita still does not, in that callback, name a replacement document. She offers the callback path in this manual, which is her own later speech on that occasion, or a further callback, not a page crowned in the issue.

## The hazard of an old close time

Anita names the hazard of an old close time.

An old close time is a closing hour that is still printed, still on a card, still on a screen, or still in a person's mouth, after the clinic's papers have moved on in ways this manual will not sort. The hazard is that a clerk, wanting to be kind and quick, reads that old close time aloud. The member goes home with a closing hour. The member plans a trip, a ride, or a meal around it. Later the member finds the building shut, or finds the building open and thinks the clinic lied. The harm is ordinary and it is enough. Anita has watched staff reach for the nearest time because a silence felt worse than a wrong hour. She is writing the hazard down so the nearest time is not the remedy.

The hazard has a second face. A copy of an hours page can fail to match itself. The check stored with the copy and the body of the copy can disagree. A clerk who trusts the copy because the check looks official can read an old close time off a body the check does not support, or can trust a check and then read a different body. Anita names that face of the hazard as part of the same hazard. She does not say which copy is the sound one. She does not say which body to believe. She says that a mismatch is a reason to stop reading, not a reason to pick the time that feels familiar.

She does not say which document is the one to serve. She does not name a page in this manual. She does not point a clerk at a binder tab, a version, a season, a site's hours sheet, or a close time and call that the page to use. A clerk who wants this issue to settle the question will not find the settlement. The omission is deliberate. Anita has left the sentence out. Naming the hazard is not naming the document. If she named the document, the hazard would become a hunt for a winner, and the clerk would read the winner aloud and call the reading compliance. That is the thing this section refuses.

Fraser Burnaby may have more than one published hours page. Published pages are the pages that are not the draft. This manual does not list them, and it does not rank them. Anita does not say which published page is current. When a member asks when the clinic closes, or when a service at Fraser Burnaby ends, the clerk does not answer from the draft, and the clerk does not choose a published page on Anita's behalf. The clerk tells the member that the closing time is not being read from a draft, and that this manual does not name the published page. The clerk offers the callback. The callback is made on an office day.

A member who brings a printed page from home is not told that the brought page is the current one, and is not told that it is retired. The clerk takes a copy of the brought page for Anita, does not read it aloud as the clinic's answer, and does not compare it aloud with the draft. The comparison, if Anita later makes one, is Anita's later act. It is not a sentence this manual already contains. The brought page may be an old close time in the member's hand. The hazard applies. The clerk does not defeat the hazard by reading a different old close time from the clinic's own pile.

If the Vancouver desk has been telling members a close time from a card of its own, that card is the desk's card. This manual does not adopt the card, and it does not correct the card by crowning a different card. Noel Park's question about what Fraser will stand behind is answered with the draft rule, with the list of what may be read aloud, and with Anita's silence on the document to serve. The desk is not given a Fraser close time by these pages. The 2024 assistant may show a close. That showing is a screen. It is not the document this manual serves, and Anita does not name it as the document to serve. She does not name any other document in its place.

People argue about hours. They argue about which page a screen is allowed to use. This manual does not award that argument. It does not award a clock to a screen. It names the hazard of an old close time so the clerk will stop. Stopping is the procedure.

## Through the middle of the day

After arrival, the member is seen by the clinician on the book. The clerk walks the member to the clinic room and does not carry the letter in view. The clinician conducts the visit. When the visit ends, the member returns to the counter for the ordinary close of the visit: the next office-day appointment if the clinician has written one in the clinic book, or a statement that no further appointment was written. The clerk reads the appointment from the clinic book. The clerk does not read it from a draft hours page. The clerk does not invent an appointment to fill a silence. The clerk does not invent an appointment so that an old close time will have somewhere to land.

A member who asks about Harbor Coverage is not given a coverage answer from this clinic. The Fraser counter does not say yes, no, or any other coverage word. The counter takes the referral id, if the member has one, and tells the member the question belongs with the payer and with the Vancouver desk.

A member who asks about a person id or a previous caller is not answered from this manual. If the member's chart and the member at the counter do not match, the clerk stops and calls Anita. The clerk does not continue the visit on a chart that belongs to someone who was here earlier.

During the middle of the day the draft stays closed. A clerk who opens it to settle an argument with a colleague has begun a reading, even if the member is not at the glass yet. The colleague is not an audience this manual adds. The clerk closes the page and asks Anita if a spoken answer is needed. Anita may refuse to speak a close time. Her refusal is an allowed outcome. The clinic can finish the day without a close time having been read aloud.

## Closing the clinic day

At the end of the office day Anita closes in a fixed order.

She confirms that letters that were in use are back in the clinician cabinet. A letter that was on a desk in a clinic room goes back before the room is left. A letter does not go home in a coat pocket. A letter does not go into the night pouch. A letter does not go to Cedar Ridge because that clinic is about to file. Priya returns the letters she used. Alex, if he was the cover, returns the letters he used. Anita confirms the cabinet herself. A clerk's nod is not the confirmation.

She confirms that every draft hours page is off the reception screen and off any screen in a clinic room that a member could have seen. Prints of drafts made during the day are in Anita's tray, not in the night pouch and not in a clerk's bag. She does not read the prints as she gathers them. Gathering is not a reading.

She confirms that no published page has been marked, by this manual or by a clerk's note, as the page being served. If a clerk has written "use this one" on a tab, Anita removes the note. She does not replace it with a better tab. The close does not smuggle in the choice the open refused.

She confirms the callback list. Each name on it has a telephone number and the office date of the next call. No callback has been completed by reading a draft after the member left. No callback has been completed by reading an old close time to the answering machine. Callbacks wait for an office day, and they wait for the rule on what may be read aloud.

The Friday close uses the same checks. The clinic day then stays shut until Monday. A question about hours that was left for callback is called on the next office morning. It is not answered from the draft on Saturday in order to clear the list. Cedar Ridge may release a file on Friday night, on Saturday night, and on Sunday night. Those releases do not reopen Fraser. They do not authorize a clerk to come in and read a close time "while the building is quiet." Quiet is not an exception.

The last act of the close is the clinic book. Anita writes the date closed, the site, and that the three confirmations were done. She does not write a close time in that line. A close time written in the clinic book would be a served time, and this manual does not name the document that would justify it. The book records that the clinic day ended. It does not record an hour taken from an old page.

## Exceptions

If the fire alarm or a similar interruption ends the day early, the same close is done as far as it can be done. Letters go back if they can be reached safely. Drafts are closed if the screens can be reached safely. Anita does not, in the parking lot, shout a close time to members who are leaving. The hazard of an old close time includes the time a person remembers under stress. She takes names for callbacks if she can do it without inventing an hour.

If a member is already in the building at the ordinary end of the day and the visit is unfinished, the clinician finishes or reschedules in the clinic book. The reception clerk does not extend the day by reading a later close time off a draft. The building's remaining minutes are the clinician's decision about this visit, written as an appointment or as a statement that no further appointment was written. They are not a new hours page.

If two clerks disagree about whether a page is a draft, they do not read it while they disagree. They ask Anita. If Anita is gone for the day, they treat the page as not readable. The safer act is silence and a callback. Silence does not name a document to serve.

If Morgan Ibarra sends a card and asks Fraser to confirm the sentence on it before Monday, the card waits in Anita's tray. It is not read to a member over the weekend. On Monday Anita may look at it. Looking is not adoption. This manual, even after she has looked, still does not name the document to serve. A sentence she speaks to Morgan on that Monday is an occasion, and it is not a revision of this issue unless a new issue is written.

If the 2024 assistant is on a screen in the room and shows a close, the clerk closes that screen before the member is brought to the counter, or turns it away and does not read it. Riley Chen is not called to interpret the figure. The figure is not what may be read aloud. The hazard applies to a close time on a tool as much as to a close time on a card. Anita still does not name a different document for the tool to serve instead.

A member who needs care the clinician judges urgent is seen under the clinician's judgment. That judgment is not a reading of an hours page and not a release of a letter to the desk. The clerk does not fill the wait with an old close time.
