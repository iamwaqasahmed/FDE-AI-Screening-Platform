# How Cascade's board is filled

Northline Access Collaborative
Cascade Seattle, hospital, Washington
Time zone of this records office: America/Los_Angeles
Issue date: 1 October 2026
Author: Jordan Hale, records
Procedure owner: Jordan Hale

## What this manual is

This manual is the operating procedure for the board Jordan Hale keeps at Cascade Seattle. Jordan wrote it on 1 October 2026. Cascade Seattle is a hospital in Washington. The records office that fills the board works Monday through Friday. Saturday and Sunday are not days on which this office accepts a referral or moves the board. Omar Farouk is the night supervisor. Omar watches the night tray. A packet that arrives after the Friday close, or on Saturday, or on Sunday, stays in that tray until the next office morning. Omar does not accept the referral. Omar does not add to the board and does not take away from the board.

The Vancouver desk keeps an office week of Monday through Friday in America/Vancouver. That week belongs to the desk. This manual does not move Cascade records onto the Vancouver counter, and it does not move the Vancouver counter onto this board. Dana Okonkwo directs operations at that desk. Morgan Ibarra supervises it. They may ask what this office has accepted. The answer is given by the steps below, and the answer names the copy it was read from.

Cedar Ridge releases a nightly file, including on weekends, and has no slot API. A Cedar Ridge file is not, by itself, a line on this board. Fraser Burnaby keeps letters with clinicians. A letter is not a line on this board. This manual describes the board this office fills. It stops at that board.

## The packet that may be accepted

A referral reaches this office as a packet. Jordan reads the referral id on the face of the packet before any other step. A packet that does not name a referral is not accepted. It goes back to the tray with a note that the id was missing, and it does not touch the board.

Jordan confirms that the packet is for Cascade Seattle. A nightly file from Cedar Ridge is a file from a rural Washington clinic. It can mention a referral. Mentioning a referral does not accept the referral into this hospital's record. This office does not invent a slot API for Cedar Ridge in order to treat that file as a Cascade accept. Inlet Medical merged into Cedar Ridge on 1 September 2026. The old site id still appears on some files. An old site id on a file is not a Cascade packet. Jordan does not accept an Inlet-marked file into the Cascade Seattle record because the merger has made the file easier to confuse. He notes the site named on the file and leaves the file out of the board.

A sentence spoken on a phone at the Vancouver desk is not an accept. A form typed on the Vancouver web intake is not an accept. A row someone describes as already on a facility mart is not an accept. Jordan has heard each of those offered as if they were the same act. Under this manual they are not the act. A reply from Harbor Coverage is not an accept. This manual does not describe Harbor's papers. It says only that those papers are not the mark in the Cascade working book.

If the packet is for another site, Jordan does not accept it into the Cascade Seattle record. Fraser Burnaby is not Cascade Seattle. Cedar Ridge is not Cascade Seattle.

## What accepted means to records

Accepted, in this office, means a records act on an office day. Jordan writes the referral into the hospital record at Cascade Seattle and marks that record accepted. He writes the referral id, the site name Cascade Seattle, and the office date of the accept in the working book he is writing in that day. The book is the working book. The mark is the accept. Until that mark is in the working book, the referral is not accepted, whatever a caller, a file, a mart row, or another office has already said.

The order is fixed. First the packet is identified. Then the site is confirmed as Cascade Seattle. Then the mark is written in the working book. Then, and only then, the board may change. Jordan does not change the board first and write the mark later. He does not accept by telephone and leave the book for the afternoon. If he is interrupted after the mark and before the board, the accept stands and the board is brought into line before he leaves the desk. If he is interrupted before the mark, there has been no accept, and the board is left as it was.

A correction later the same office day is a correction of the record, not a second accept. Jordan strikes the wrong line in the working book, writes the corrected line, and records that the referral was already accepted. He does not add a second accept for the same referral because a name, a clinic wording, or a date on the packet was amended. If the accept itself was made in error and Jordan reverses it the same office day, he marks the reversal in the working book and the board step below reduces the count by one. A reversal is a reversal. It is not a quiet omission.

Omar Farouk does not perform this section on a night shift. The night tray holds the packet. The accept waits for the office day. A Monday accept of a packet that sat in the tray on Saturday is a Monday accept. The office date on the mark is Monday. The Saturday arrival is a tray fact. It is not the accept.

Accepted means that mark. It does not mean that a member used the word accepted on a phone. It does not mean that a clerk at another desk hopes the hospital has the referral. It does not mean that a display somewhere shows a number. People outside this office use the word for other events. This manual uses it for the working-book mark at Cascade Seattle, made on an office day, by records, after the packet has been identified and the site confirmed.

## How the board is filled

The board in this records office counts referrals this office has accepted and has not reversed. The unit of the count is the accept. One referral accepted once adds one. A second conversation about the same referral does not add one. A Cedar Ridge file that mentions the referral does not add one. A call answered by Noel Park or June Lau does not add one. A figure Morgan Ibarra might read at the Vancouver counter does not add one. A packet sitting in Omar Farouk's night tray does not add one.

When Jordan has marked the accept in the working book, he adds one to the board for that referral if the referral was not already on the count. He writes the new count in the board book with the office date. He writes the referral id beside the change so a later morning can see which accept moved the count. He does not copy a count from the Vancouver desk into this board. He does not copy a count from another hospital into this board. Chris Pell may ask, from finance, what number should sit beside a later display. This manual answers only for the board Jordan keeps: the count of accepts not reversed.

When Jordan has marked a same-day reversal, he subtracts one if the referral was on the count. He writes the new count and the referral id of the reversal. A reversal on a later office day is still a reversal. He does not wait for a round number. He does not leave the count high because the morning is busy.

The board count is a count kept in this office. Staff at other offices have described other counts, begun on other events, and Jordan has heard those descriptions. This manual states what this board counts. It does not name a clock for a screen. It does not say that the accept is the instant any other display is allowed to use. It does not choose among the desk, the mart, the file night, and this board. A person who wants that choice will not find it in these pages. Jordan will not add it in the margin. This manual does not award a clock to a screen.

## The copy the desk saw yesterday

This office keeps the working book, and it keeps a copy that is one version behind the working book. Staff here call that copy the copy the desk saw yesterday. The name means the version the desk was shown on the previous office day, still sitting one version behind the book Jordan is writing in today.

An accept marked this morning is in the working book. It is not yet on the copy the desk saw yesterday. A correction marked this morning is in the working book. The copy the desk saw yesterday still shows the earlier version, including a clinic name or a line that the working book has since changed. A clerk who reads only that copy is reading yesterday's version. The clerk is not reading the working book, even when the two sit on the same table.

When the Vancouver desk asks what Cascade has accepted, the clerk says which copy the answer came from. If the answer came from the copy the desk saw yesterday, the clerk says those words, and says that the copy is one version behind the working book. The clerk does not describe that copy as if it were the book Jordan has open. The note that goes back to Morgan Ibarra or Dana Okonkwo names the copy in the first sentence.

If the two copies disagree, the clerk writes both readings on the note and writes which reading came from which copy. A clinic name on the copy the desk saw yesterday may differ from the clinic name in the working book. The clerk records each name with the copy it was read from. This manual does not say which name prevails. This manual does not tell the desk to show one of them on a screen. The disagreement stays visible because the copy is one version behind, and the procedure is to name the copy.

The copy is refreshed when Jordan sets the day's working book aside at the close of the office day and the next office morning's side copy is taken from it. Until that refresh, the side copy remains the copy the desk saw yesterday. Omar Farouk does not refresh it at night. A Friday close leaves Friday's version as the copy the desk will still be looking at until Monday's office work moves the working book. Weekend tray packets are not accepts and do not appear on either copy until Jordan accepts them on an office day.

## What the mart shows

The facility mart is a table other offices read. Jordan has been shown rows from it. This section says what those rows show. It does not adopt the mart as the board this office fills.

A mart row, on the sheets this office has been shown, carries the referral, a booking date, and a wait figure. The wait figure on those rows is 0. The 0 sits beside the booking date. Jordan has seen that shape more than once. He writes it here because clerks in this office are asked to "just read the mart" as if the mart were the working book. The mart shows the referral, the booking date, and a wait of 0. That is what it shows. It does not show the accept mark. It does not show whether Jordan has written the referral into the Cascade hospital record. It does not show whether the packet is still in Omar's tray. A wait of 0 is a figure on the mart. It is not an accept, and it is not a count of accepts.

The board is filled from the working book, by the steps above. The mart is not a source the clerk adds from. If the mart shows a booking date for a referral this office has not accepted, the board does not gain one. If the mart shows a wait of 0 for a referral this office has accepted, the board does not change its count to 0. The count on the board remains the count of accepts not reversed. The two displays can sit on the same table and disagree. The clerk writes down what the mart shows, writes down what the working book shows, and does not sand them into one sentence.

Jordan does not say that a screen may use the mart's 0. Jordan does not say that a screen may use the office date of the accept. He has heard both requests. He has not granted either. Hearing a request is not a line on the board. Chris Pell's questions about a figure for finance are answered with the board count when the question is about this board, and they are answered with "that is the mart" when the question is about the mart. The two answers stay apart.

Example. A packet for a referral arrives in the night tray on Thursday. The mart already shows that referral with a booking date and a wait of 0. On Friday morning Jordan has not yet marked the working book. The board does not include the referral. The mart still shows the 0. Omar does not add the referral overnight to make the board match the mart. On Friday, if Jordan identifies the packet, confirms Cascade Seattle, and marks the working book, the board gains one. The copy the desk saw yesterday does not gain that one until the next refresh. A clerk who is asked on Friday afternoon what the desk was shown yesterday reads the side copy and says so. A clerk who is asked whether records has accepted the referral reads the working book.

Example. The working book shows an accept. The mart row for the same referral is absent. The accept stands. Jordan does not reverse the accept because the mart is quiet. He does not phone the Vancouver desk and ask them to type a row so the mart will look full. The board was filled from the mark. The mart shows what it shows, including when it shows nothing for that referral.

## What the clerk says in this office

A member does not receive the board count as a promise about another office's display. If a person at Cascade asks whether a referral has been accepted, Jordan or the records clerk looks at the working book, not at the copy the desk saw yesterday, and not at the mart, and answers yes or no from the mark. The answer is about the hospital record at Cascade Seattle. It is not a reading of a Cedar Ridge file. It is not a reading of a letter held by Priya Shah at Fraser Burnaby.

If the question is what the desk was told, the clerk uses the copy the desk saw yesterday and says so. If the question is what the mart shows, the clerk says the mart shows a referral, a booking date, and a wait of 0 when that is the row in front of them, and says that this is the mart and not the board. The answers are allowed to differ. The clerk does not smooth them into one sentence. The clerk does not pick a time of day and declare it the time a screen should carry. The working book has an office date on the accept. The copy has the office date of the version it still shows. The mart has the booking date it shows. All three are written when all three are asked for. None of them is awarded to a screen by this manual.

## Exceptions

A packet with two referral ids on its face is not accepted until Jordan has confirmed which id the packet is. He does not accept both to be safe. He does not accept the id that appears on the mart and ignore the id written on the packet. The packet is the packet. If the two ids cannot be reconciled on the office day, the packet returns to the tray with a note, and the board is unchanged.

A same-day duplicate packet for a referral already accepted does not add a second one to the board. Jordan notes the duplicate on the working book as a duplicate. The count stays. A duplicate is not a reversal, and it is not a new accept.

A reversal requested by telephone, with no packet and no mark, is not a reversal. The caller is told that the working book has not been marked. The board stays until an office-day mark reverses it.

If the side copy and the working book have been physically swapped by a clerk in a hurry, Jordan stops the conversation with the desk, identifies which book has today's mark, and names that book the working book before he speaks a count. He does not guess from the mart's 0 which book is live.

A question that asks this office to name the event a screen label requires is not answered here. A question that asks which clock a board is allowed to use, meaning a board other than the count of accepts this office keeps, is not answered here. The board this manual fills is the count of Cascade accepts not reversed. Other arguments in the building can continue. This manual does not award them.
