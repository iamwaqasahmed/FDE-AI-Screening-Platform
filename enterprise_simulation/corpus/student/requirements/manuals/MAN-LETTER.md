# Who may see a letter

Northline Access Collaborative
Fraser Burnaby, hospital, British Columbia, and the Vancouver desk
Time zone: America/Vancouver
Issue date: 1 July 2026
Authors: Priya Shah, MD, clinician, Fraser Burnaby, and Evan Cho, privacy officer, Vancouver
This is a joint manual. Both authors wrote it. Neither author's name is a courtesy signature.

## What this manual is

This manual is the operating procedure for a hospital letter. Priya Shah and Evan Cho wrote it together on 1 July 2026. Priya is the clinician at Fraser Burnaby. Evan is the privacy officer at the Vancouver desk. The desk is in Vancouver, British Columbia. The office week at the desk and the clinic day at Fraser Burnaby are Monday through Friday. Saturday and Sunday are not office days. A question about a letter that arrives after the Friday close waits, with the letter unmoved, until the next office morning.

A letter is a file. The audience mark on the letter is clinician. That mark is part of the file. This manual says who may see the file, how screen text differs from the file, and what is done when someone wants a tool pointed at the file. The manual is a desk and clinic instruction. It cites no statute. It describes no external filing. The names in it are the synthetic names already used in the Northline document room. No real patient, member, or employee is described here.

Dana Okonkwo directs the Vancouver desk. Morgan Ibarra supervises it. Noel Park and June Lau are clerks there. Alex Kim, MD, is the on-call physician. Riley Chen owns the 2024 assistant. Harper Singh left in March 2026. This manual does not assign Harper a change to a program, and it does not say a live system will block a click. The policy is the rule. A screen or a later job may do something else on a given day. When that happens, this manual remains the rule, and the other behavior is a separate fact.

## Who may see a letter

The person who may see a letter is the clinician responsible for it. At Fraser Burnaby that clinician is Priya Shah when Priya is the clinician named for the letter. The letter stays in the clinician's record. Priya reads it because she is the clinician and the audience mark says clinician. She does not read it aloud at the reception counter. She does not dictate it to a clerk. She does not paste it into a mailbox so that a thread will have the text.

Alex Kim, MD, may see a letter when he is the on-call physician and the opening is break-glass. Break-glass is an opening of a letter file by the on-call physician. It is the same sentence on an office day, at night, and on a weekend. Cedar Ridge may file at night, including Saturday and Sunday. The Vancouver office week does not drop the sentence on those nights. Break-glass requires a named reviewer. The reviewer is a person whose name is written as reviewer on that opening. The physician's name records who opened the letter. The name written as reviewer is a separate place on the desk-book line. A reason sentence, a referral reference, a site name, or a blank does not fill that place. The physician's own name, written only as the physician, does not fill that place by itself. This manual does not choose the reviewer's name in advance. Being listed on this page is not itself a review of a later opening.

Alex writes the opening in the Vancouver desk book at the time of the opening. The line carries the physician's name, the reviewer's name, the letter file that was opened, and the time on the America/Vancouver clock. Morgan Ibarra reads the book on the next office morning. When the reviewer's name is missing, Morgan asks Alex for the name and writes the answer beside the original line, with the morning's date. The original line stays, so the missing name remains visible until a name is written. That follow-up is a desk practice. This manual does not say a program rejects the opening or inserts a reviewer.

Evan Cho may see the rule. He may see the audience mark. He may see that a letter exists as a file with a clinician audience. He does not ask for the letter text to be pasted into a thread so that he can file the words in a mailbox. The people on a mail thread are not the audience marked on the document. If a sender has already put letter text into a thread, Evan tells them to stop, and the text is not forwarded onward to widen the audience. Evan's seeing the rule is not a grant to see the file.

A clerk may see screen text. Screen text is defined in the next section. Noel Park, June Lau, and any other person sitting the clerk chair may read screen text during the office week for the work they have open. Morgan may read screen text when she is supervising the clerk's screen. Dana may read screen text when she is directing the desk from that same screen. That grant is a grant to see screen text. It is not a grant to see the letter.

The following people do not see the letter under this manual. Riley Chen does not see it by owning a tool. Chris Pell does not see it by asking what a product costs. Jordan Hale does not see it by keeping Cascade records. Sam Nguyen does not see it by filing a night at Cedar Ridge. Ruth Alvarez and Lena Brooks do not see it by recording a coverage answer. Noel and June do not see it by being helpful on the phone. Morgan does not see it by supervising the desk. Dana does not see it by directing operations. A member at the counter does not see it by asking what it says. A member on the phone does not hear it. Inlet Medical, on the date of this manual, is still its own Washington clinic. A letter held at Inlet is still a letter. The audience mark is still clinician. A merger that has not happened is not a reason to move the file, and a merger that later happens would still not, by itself, move the audience.

If a member asks what the letter says, the clerk says that the letter stays with the clinician and offers to ask Priya, or Alex if Alex is the on-call cover that day, whether the clinician will speak with the member. The clerk does not preview the answer. The clerk does not quote a phrase in order to be helpful while the clinician is in another room. Anything Priya chooses to say to the member in the clinic room is Priya's speech as the clinician. It is not a release of the file to the reception record, and it is not a release of the file to the desk.

## Screen text and the file

Screen text is the text shown on a display for the work a person has open. A referral id on a screen is screen text. A short badge on a screen is screen text. A figure on a screen is screen text. A string of characters on a screen is screen text. The 2024 assistant has a screen. Text shown on that screen is screen text. Standing at the screen does not turn a file into screen text.

The letter is a file. Words inside the file stay in the file. The audience mark is part of the file. A screen line that mentions the same referral, the same member, or the same desk does not change the file into screen text. A clerk who may read the screen is not, by that grant, allowed to open the letter file, copy the letter file, or carry the letter file off the desk.

The two objects can sit in front of the same person and still be two objects. Example. The screen shows a referral id and a badge. The file is the letter stored on the hospital side, with the audience mark clinician, and with the words Priya is responsible for. The badge is screen text. The words in the file are the file. Pointing at the badge is not pointing at the file. Reading the badge aloud is not reading the file aloud. Quoting the badge to a member is quoting screen text. It is not quoting the letter. A clerk who says "the screen says this" is describing the screen. A clerk who says "the letter says this" has left this manual, unless that clerk is the clinician reading in the clinic room under the section above, or Alex under break-glass with a named reviewer.

Example. A printout of the screen is still screen text. Putting the printout in a folder does not make it the letter file. A printout of the letter file is the letter leaving the place the record keeps it. This manual does not permit that printout to go to the desk, to finance, to a night file, or to a tool. If a printout of the file has already been made, Priya or Evan is told, the printout is returned to the clinician cabinet, and it is not read onto a phone.

Example. The screen and the file disagree. The screen shows a short word. The file contains the clinician's text. The disagreement is not repaired by editing the file to match the screen, and it is not repaired by pasting the file onto the screen. Priya does not grade the file from the screen. Evan does not treat the screen as the audience mark. The file remains the file. The screen remains screen text. This manual does not say which of them a later display is required to prefer, because that sentence would be a different argument, and this manual does not award it. This manual says who may see the file, and it says the screen is not the file.

Ordinary clerk work stays on the screen. When the work cannot be finished from screen text, Noel asks Morgan the same office day, on the Vancouver desk clock, before the desk closes. Morgan decides whether the next step is a supervisor reading on the same screen or a request that stays inside this rule. The answer on this page stays the same: a clerk may read screen text, and a letter file is not screen text.

## The assistant

The 2024 assistant is a tool. Riley Chen owns it. Some people want to point that tool at the letter. They want the tool to read the file so a screen will have something on it when the clinician is not standing at the chart. They want the weekend covered by the tool. They want a badge, or a string, or a word, taken off the letter and placed where a clerk can see it without opening the file. Priya has heard the request. Evan has heard the request. The request is real. People will make it again.

This manual does not permit that. The assistant is not pointed at the letter. The letter is not given to the assistant. The letter is not given to Riley so that he can point the assistant at it. A wish to fill a screen is not a grant. A wish to be helpful on a thin weekend is not a grant. Fraser being short of clinicians on a Sunday does not move the file onto a tool. If Priya is not in the building, the letter waits for a clinician. It does not get a reading from the assistant so the screen has something to show.

Evan's line is the same line. If the assistant can only read the letter by taking it off the hospital record, the answer to the read is no. He has not been shown a design in which the read and the move are different things. Until he is, this manual treats the ask as a move. The move is not signed. Please do not put the letter text in the request. The people who want the tool pointed at the letter are not the audience marked on the document.

Priya's line is the clinical line beside that one. She will read the letter. She is the clinician. A tool does not read it for her under this manual. A cheaper tool does not read it for her. Chris Pell may have a cost on a sheet. Price is not the question this manual answers. Priya will not sign a grade produced by the assistant and she will not adopt one after the fact because a panel looked empty. The refusal is joint. Either author may say it. A clerk does not shop between the two authors for a softer sentence.

If the assistant's screen already shows text, that text is screen text under the previous section. Its presence does not permit a further pointing of the tool at the file. A clerk who can see the screen does not open the file to "check the tool." Checking the tool against the file would be opening the file. The clerk asks Priya if a clinician reading is required, or asks Evan if someone is proposing to move the file. The clerk does not perform the comparison alone at the counter.

Harper Singh is not available to build a narrower connection. He left in March 2026. This manual does not appoint a replacement in order to permit the pointing. Bobbi Tran keeps the three logs. She is not asked to feed the letter into the assistant. Anita Desai, clinic manager at Fraser Burnaby, is not asked to approve the pointing. If Anita is asked, the answer on this manual is still no, and Anita tells the asker to speak to Evan without putting the letter text into a message while she waits.

## Through the clinic day and the desk day

Anita opens the Fraser clinic book on an office morning and writes the clinician on duty. On a day Priya is on duty, the letters of that day stay with Priya. On a day Alex is the on-call cover named in the book, break-glass is the path if a letter file has to be opened, and the named reviewer is still required. Charts are not stacked on the reception counter for a member to read. A letter does not go into the reception folder. A letter does not go into a pouch for the Vancouver desk.

At the Vancouver desk the clerk works from screen text. The clerk does not fetch Priya's record to the counter. The clerk does not fetch it to the phone. The clerk does not fetch it because a member is upset, and does not fetch it because a supervisor wants one number before a weekend. The letter stays where the hospital record keeps it. Cascade Seattle, Cedar Ridge, and Fraser Burnaby are the same rule on that point. Their letters stay where the hospital record keeps them. Cedar Ridge has no slot API. A night file is not a letter and does not carry a letter out of the clinician's record.

At the end of the clinic day Priya returns letters to the clinician cabinet. Anita confirms the cabinet. A letter that was on a desk in a clinic room goes back before the room is left. A letter does not go home in a coat pocket. A letter does not go to the night file at any other site. The Friday close uses the same cabinet check. Monday begins with the letters still in the cabinet, not with a stack that spent the weekend at the Vancouver desk or in an assistant's store.

## Exceptions

A member in the clinic room may hear what Priya decides to say about their care. That speech is the clinician speaking. It is not a copy of the file handed to the member to take away, and it is not a warrant for the desk to repeat the speech later as if it were screen text. If the member asks the desk to repeat what Priya said, the desk does not reconstruct the letter. The desk offers a callback to the clinician on an office day.

Break-glass without a reviewer name is not a completed opening under this manual. Alex still writes the line he wrote. Morgan still asks for the name on the next office morning. The missing name is not papered over by a busy night. Cedar Ridge filing that night is not the reviewer.

A screen that already shows a word is not an emergency grant. The clerk does not open the file because the screen looks urgent. Urgency on a display is screen text. The file remains the file. If a clinician needs to see the file, the clinician sees it under this manual. The clerk does not.

Someone who says the assistant has already read the letter is reporting a thing that may have happened. This manual does not permit it, including after the fact. Priya does not adopt the reading. Evan does not sign the move because it is already done. The letter is not pointed at again to "finish" the reading. The next request gets the same refusal.

A request to show the letter to a vendor, a cheaper model, or a cost comparison is a request to move the file. Chris may keep a number on a sheet. He does not receive the letter. Evan does not approve an attachment. Priya does not grade from a vendor's output.

If the audience mark on a file is missing, the file is not treated as screen text in order to fill the gap. The clerk stops. Evan is asked. Priya is asked if the file is one she holds. Nobody invents an audience of "whoever is at the counter." Until the mark is known, the file stays unmoved and unpointed.

This manual does not award a clock to a screen. It does not say which instant a display may use. It does not name an event a screen label requires. People may argue about tools and about weekends. The argument does not replace the page. The assistant is not permitted to be pointed at the letter.
