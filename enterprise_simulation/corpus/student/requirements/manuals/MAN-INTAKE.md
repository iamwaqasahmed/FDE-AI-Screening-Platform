# Phone and web intake at the Vancouver desk

Northline Access Collaborative
Vancouver desk, British Columbia
Time zone of this desk: America/Vancouver
Issue date: 1 October 2026
Author: Morgan Ibarra, desk supervisor
Procedure owner: Morgan Ibarra

## What this manual is

This manual is the operating procedure for phone intake and web intake at the Vancouver desk. Morgan Ibarra wrote it on 1 October 2026. The desk is the Northline desk in Vancouver. The office week is Monday through Friday. Saturday and Sunday are not office days. A phone call that arrives after the Friday close waits for Monday. A web form that arrives after the Friday close waits for Monday. Noel Park and June Lau follow this manual on every office day they take a phone intake or a web intake. Any other clerk assigned to the desk follows the same steps. Dana Okonkwo receives the manual as operations director and does not change a step during a live intake. Evan Cho is called when a member token has been typed for the wrong person. Bobbi Tran keeps the three logs. This manual does not ask Bobbi to change a program. Harper Singh left in March 2026. This manual does not assign anyone a change to a program.

The counter procedure for a member standing at the glass is a different issue. This manual is the phone and the web. A clerk who has the member on the handset, or who has a web form on the intake queue, works from the sections below. A clerk who has the member physically at the counter does not pretend the counter is a phone call in order to skip a factor. The two kinds of approach can happen on the same office morning. They are still two approaches.

## The office week, and what the desk does not open

The desk opens when Morgan opens the counter on an office morning and closes when Morgan closes it. Monday's first intake is a new intake. It does not continue Friday's last call and it does not continue Friday's last web form. Cedar Ridge, the rural clinic in Washington, releases a nightly file, including on Saturday and on Sunday. That file is not a reason to open this desk on a weekend, and it is not a reason to treat a weekend web form as if the counter were open. Cedar Ridge has no slot API. Cascade Seattle keeps its own records office. Harbor Coverage keeps its own answers. Fraser Burnaby keeps its own clinic day. This manual is the Vancouver intake.

A member who speaks about Cedar Ridge, about Cascade, about Fraser, or about a payer is still on a Vancouver intake if the phone or the web form reached this desk. The words they use are captured here. Those words do not move the desk onto another site's calendar.

## What the clerk types on a phone intake

The clerk types. The application mints. The member speaks. Those three acts are different, and the clerk keeps them different while the call is open.

When the call is answered, the clerk types the channel as phone. The clerk types the desk region. The clerk types the office date and the time of day the desk took the call, read from the desk clock in America/Vancouver. That time is the time the clerk writes on the intake. This manual leaves that time on the intake. It does not hand the time to a screen, a board, a mart, a nightly file, or a payer row.

The clerk then types the utterance. The utterance is the member's words, typed as the member spoke them. The clerk does not improve the grammar. The clerk does not shorten the sentence into a slogan the clerk prefers. The clerk does not add the word booked if the member did not say it, and the clerk does not remove the word booked if the member did say it. After the utterance is typed, the clerk reads the utterance back to the member. The reading has to match the words on the row. If the member corrects a word, the clerk corrects the utterance and reads it back again. The call is not closed on a reading the member has rejected.

The clerk types the handset result. The result is delivered or not delivered. Delivered means the handset message for this call reached the handset the member is using for this call. Not delivered means it did not. The clerk does not type delivered in order to clear the queue. The clerk does not type not delivered and then tell the member that a later clinic step has been completed. If the handset result is not delivered, the clerk says so to the member in plain words, types not delivered, and tells Morgan if the member is still waiting for a message that has not arrived. The intake can still hold the utterance. The missing handset does not become a visit.

The clerk types the member token only after identification on this call. The member token is the token subject for the member. It names the member. The clerk asks for the identification this caller is giving now. The clerk types that token. The clerk does not look at the previous call and continue its token onto this one. If the caller does not give an identification the clerk can type, the member token stays blank. A blank token is not filled from the previous caller, from a clinic name, from a file id, or from a sentence in the utterance.

The clerk does not type the session id. The session id is minted by the desk application for this approach. Minting is the application's act. It is not a string the clerk chooses from the morning list. It is not a string the clerk copies out of the member token. If the application does not mint a session id, the clerk does not invent one. The intake is not treated as a completed session. Morgan records the failed approach, and the member is asked to begin again.

The clerk does not type a booking. The clerk does not type a file id from Cedar Ridge. The clerk does not type an accept for Cascade Seattle. The clerk does not type a coverage word in place of the utterance. The clerk does not type a wait figure. A question about how long the member has waited is not answered by typing a number into the intake. The clerk tells the member that the intake has been taken, and the clerk refers the wait question to Morgan. This manual does not choose a figure for a screen.

If a screen code is used as a factor on the phone, the code is the code for this call. The member reads it from the display that belongs to this approach, or from the message that belongs to this approach. The clerk types that code. The clerk does not type a code remembered from an earlier call. The section on a screen code reused, later in this manual, is part of the phone path as well as the web path.

## What the clerk types on a web intake

A web intake begins as a form. The member types the form. The words the member typed are the utterance. The clerk does not retype those words into a cleaner sentence. A cleaner sentence, if a clerk writes one at all, is a clerk note. The clerk note is not the utterance. The utterance remains the member's text.

The clerk types the channel as web. The clerk types the desk region. The clerk types the office date and the time the form was taken up at this desk, in America/Vancouver. The clerk types the member token from the identification on this form, under the same rule as the phone: this form, this person, no token carried forward from the previous form. The clerk types the message result for any handset or web message this form required, delivered or not delivered, and does not type delivered to make the queue look finished.

The clerk does not type the session id on a web intake. The application mints it for this form when the factors for this form are complete. A form that arrived on Saturday is not given a session id on Saturday. It is taken up on Monday as its own intake, with its own utterance, its own member token, and, if the factors complete, its own minted session id.

The web page may show a screen code for this approach. The clerk types the code the page is showing for this form. The clerk does not type the code from the form above it in the queue. The clerk does not type a code the member pasted in from an old email if that code is not the code this page is showing now. If the page is blank where the code should be, the clerk does not invent a code. The form waits until a code is shown or Morgan ends the approach.

## What an utterance is

An utterance is the member's words, held on the intake as words. On the phone, it is what the clerk typed from the member's speech and read back. On the web, it is what the member typed on the form. The utterance can be one sentence or several. It can name a clinic. It can name a night. It can say that a visit is booked. It can say that a visit is not booked. It can be uncertain. The utterance is still the words.

The clerk points at the utterance when a later reader asks what the member said. The clerk does not point at the utterance when a later reader asks whether Cedar Ridge has released a visit. Those are different questions. The first question is answered by reading the utterance aloud, matching the row. The second question is not answered from this desk, because Cedar Ridge has no slot API and the nightly file is not typed here.

A training sentence used on this desk is the shape "booked at Cedar Ridge tonight." Clerks hear that shape. Clerks see that shape on a web form. The shape is an utterance when it is what the member said or typed. The training sentence is useful because it tempts a clerk to treat speech as a visit. The temptation is the reason the next section exists.

## What is not a booking

The utterance is not a booking. Typing the utterance is not a booking. Reading the utterance back is not a booking. A web form that says the member wants tonight is not a booking. A phone sentence that says the visit is booked is not a booking. The word booked, sitting inside an utterance, does not create a detail row at Cedar Ridge. It does not hold a chair. It does not call a slot API, because Cedar Ridge has no slot API. Sam Nguyen releases a file at night, including weekends. Until a visit is a detail row in a file he has released, this desk has not watched a booking land, and this desk does not type one into existence.

The session id is not a booking. The member token is not a booking. The screen code is not a booking. The handset result is not a booking. The time the desk took the call is not a booking. A referral id the member already had, typed so the intake can be found later, is an identifier. It is not a booking. A clerk who types the referral id is not confirming that a visit exists at the clinic named in the utterance.

Cascade Seattle's records are not made by this typing. Jordan Hale accepts a referral in that office under that office's book. A sentence on a Vancouver phone is not his mark. Fraser Burnaby's clinic book is not made by this typing. Anita Desai's hours pages are not made by this typing. A coverage answer is not made by this typing. Lena Brooks's unit records yes, no, or timeout on its own row. The clerk does not type those words onto the intake in order to finish the call.

If the member asks "so am I booked," the clerk answers from what this desk has actually done. The desk has taken the intake. The desk has the utterance. The desk has not booked a visit at Cedar Ridge. The clerk says that. The clerk does not soften the utterance into a promise, and the clerk does not scold the member for the word they used. The word stays in the utterance. The booking stays untyped.

## Session id and the member token

The session id and the member token are two strings. They do different work. The clerk keeps them in different fields.

The session id identifies one approach at this desk. One phone call that completes its factors gets one session id, minted here. One web form that completes its factors gets one session id, minted here. A second call by the same member later the same office day gets a new session id if the factors are completed again. The clerk does not type the session id. The clerk does not choose it from a list of ids used earlier the same morning. The clerk does not repair a blank session field by pasting the member token into it. The clerk does not repair a blank session field by pasting yesterday's session id into it. Familiarity is not a mint.

The member token identifies the member. The clerk types it. It is the token subject obtained on this intake. A member who calls on Monday and again on Thursday can have the same member token on both intakes, because the token names the person, and only after the clerk has obtained the identification again on Thursday. The Thursday session id is still a new session id. The match of the token is not a match of the session. A note that names the session id names that session id and no other. A note that names the member token names the token obtained on this intake and no other.

The mix-up this manual forbids is a paste in either direction. The member token pasted into the session id field makes a person look like an approach. The session id pasted into the member token field makes an approach look like a person. Both pastes have a tidy look on a screen. Both are wrong. If the clerk has already pasted one into the other, the clerk stops, clears the field that received the paste, and types or mints again under this section. The clerk does not finish the call on the grounds that the two strings "belong together."

Example. A morning call is minted as a session. The token subject typed from the caller's identification is the token for that caller. Forty minutes later a different person calls. The application mints a new session id. The token field on the new intake is blank until this caller is identified. The clerk types this caller's token. If this caller's token and the earlier token differ, that difference is expected. If a clerk leaves the earlier token in place, the new session is carrying the previous person. That intake is not released. Morgan is told. If the person now on the line has already been spoken to under the previous token, the clerk stops the conversation and Morgan tells Evan Cho.

Example. The same member calls twice. The token matches because the clerk obtained it again and the person is the same person. The session ids do not match. The clerk writes a short note that the token was obtained again on this call. A match that was produced by leaving the old token untouched is not that note.

## A screen code reused

A screen code belongs to one approach. The code on the display, or on the page, is the code for the approach that display or that page is showing now. When that approach ends, the code ends with it. The next approach needs its own code. Likeness of a code does not join two approaches. An exact repeat does not join them either.

A screen code reused is a thing that has happened at this desk. Morgan Ibarra has seen it. A code that had already been used for one approach was entered again for a later approach. She has seen it on a web queue, where the code from the form above was typed onto the form below. She has seen it on a phone intake, where a code read to the previous caller was read again to the next caller because it was still on the clerk's card. She saw the pair. She is not guessing at a story from another site. The desk book names the office date and the two approaches. Noel Park and June Lau read that record when they open the queue on Monday.

The manual says a screen code reused should not happen. Seeing it does not make it allowed. A code that has already gone out on a second approach is pulled back. The clerk who typed it, or Morgan if that clerk has left the chair, strikes the reused code, obtains the code that belongs to the approach now waiting, and types that code. The intake is not released while it still carries the earlier approach's code. If the member has already been taken through a later step under the reused code, the clerk stops, tells Morgan, and does not finish the approach "since it has already started."

A fresh code that happens to look similar is still typed only if it is the code this approach is showing. The clerk reads the code as a string. The clerk compares it with the previous approach's code. If the strings are the same, the clerk stops and treats the match as the failure in this section until a new code has been obtained for the approach now waiting. The clerk does not write a note that blesses the reuse. There is no note that makes a reused screen code correct.

The same rule applies at the Friday close and at the Monday open. Friday's last code is not left on the card for Monday's first caller. Monday's first approach starts with the code Monday's display or Monday's page shows. A weekend web form opened on Monday is a Monday approach. It does not inherit Friday's code.

## Worked example, phone

On an office morning Noel answers a call. He types the channel as phone. He types the desk region. He types the time the desk took the call. The member says the visit is booked at Cedar Ridge tonight. Noel types that sentence as the utterance. He reads it back. The member agrees that those were the words. Noel types the handset result. On this example the handset was not delivered. He types not delivered. He does not tell the member the visit is booked. He tells the member the desk has the words, the handset message has not arrived, and Cedar Ridge is not booked from this call. He obtains the member token from this caller and types it. He does not type a session id. The application mints one. He does not type a file id. He does not type a wait. If the member asks how long, Noel refers that question to Morgan and does not invent a number while he waits.

June, taking the next call, sees Noel's code still on the card. She does not read it to the new caller. She clears the card. She uses the code for the call she has now. If she had read Noel's code, that would be a screen code reused, which has happened here, and which this manual says should not.

## Worked example, web

A form sits in the Monday queue. It was submitted on Saturday. The member's text says they want to be seen at Cedar Ridge tonight, and the word tonight in a Saturday form is already a day that has passed by Monday. June does not rewrite tonight into a Monday sentence. The utterance stays the member's text. June types the channel as web, the desk region, the Monday time she took the form up, and the member token from the identification on the form. She types the screen code the page shows for this form. She does not type the screen code from the Saturday-looking draft that was still open in another tab. The application mints the session id. June does not type a booking to make the stale word tonight true. Sam's weekend file, if he released one, is his file. It is not this form.

## Exceptions

If the caller will not give a member token, the intake is not released. The clerk tells the caller that the intake cannot go forward without the identification, and the clerk tells Morgan. The call may be ended or held at the same clerk. It is not released with an empty token, and it is not released with someone else's token.

If the application fails to mint a session id, the clerk does not borrow one. The approach is unfinished. A later successful mint is a new approach, not a repair that reuses the failed approach's screen code.

If the member and the clerk disagree about the utterance after two readings, the clerk types the words the member confirms on the second reading, notes that the first typing was corrected, and does not keep both sentences as if both were the utterance. One intake has one utterance.

If the member names two clinics in one utterance, the clerk types both names inside the utterance. The clerk does not split the call into two bookings. The clerk does not choose a clinic and delete the other from the words.

If a supervisor from another office asks the clerk to type a visit so a display will have something to show, the clerk refuses. Morgan supports that refusal. The utterance can sit on the intake. The booking is not typed. This manual does not award a clock to a screen. People in the building argue about counts and about starts. This manual records the intake. It does not pick a winner among those arguments, and it does not name an event a screen label requires.

A handoff of the call to another clerk uses a fresh identification for the person now on the line. The utterance already read back may be read again by the receiving clerk. The receiving clerk does not replace the utterance with a summary. The receiving clerk checks the member token against the person now speaking before speaking about that person's matter.
