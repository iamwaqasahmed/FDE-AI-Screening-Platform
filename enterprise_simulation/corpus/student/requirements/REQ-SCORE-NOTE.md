# Note on the calibration export

Chris Pell, finance, Vancouver. 14 January 2027.

Riley Chen left a calibration export on the shared drive before the assistant work paused. I opened it because Dana asked whether we could say the new wording was better than the old wording before the long weekend. I am not a model person. I can read a table.

The sheet has a handful of rows. Each row has two scores, a count, an arm letter, and a column headed pass. I did not ask for that column. It sits on the row the way a total sits on an invoice after the line items, which makes me think it was copied from the answer rather than measured in the room. I do not know who typed it. Riley is not here this week.

Two of the rows are small. One has the same score on both arms and a count of twelve. Another has scores that differ in the fourth decimal and a count of eight. Dana read those two as a win for arm A because the pass column says so. I said I would not sign a slide until someone writes down, before looking at that column, what size of difference we were willing to call a change. Dana said the column already is that writing. I do not think a column that arrived with the sheet is the same thing as a rule we wrote first.

There is also a channel note from the desk, not on this sheet, that mentions scores 0.5002 and 0.5000 with a count of nine. It has no pass column. I have not lined that note up with the sheet. They might be the same comparison written twice, or they might be a different afternoon. Nobody labeled them as the same study.

Morgan Ibarra asked if the sheet could decide the assistant's urgency wording. I said I can read the column and I can read the counts, and I am not ready to treat them as the same kind of fact. That was the end of the conversation.

Open items, still open:

- Who added the pass column, and from which file.
- Whether the desk note with n 9 is this sheet or another one.
- What we would have decided if the pass column had been absent.

I am leaving the export where it was. This note is only to say that I saw the column.
