# What member services must be able to say on the phone

Northline Access Collaborative
Vancouver desk
Member services
Business requirements
Date: 7 January 2027
Author: June Lau, member services

## Purpose

This is a business requirements note from member services. I am June Lau. I take the phone at the Vancouver desk. I am writing it on Thursday, 7 January 2027, which is an office day. The office week at this desk is Monday through Friday. Saturday and Sunday are not days on which this bay is open, and they are not days on which I am asking anyone to invent a sentence so a closed counter can sound open. The requirement in this note is about the sentence a member can hear on an office day, from this phone, and can repeat afterward without me standing there to explain it a second time.

Member services must be able to say, on the phone, what the member has asked to hear when the member asks how the matter stands. That is the work. It is not a new product. It is not a new screen. It is the speech the bay is already expected to give, and it is speech I cannot give honestly while three different numbers are being quoted around the same member. I am writing the requirement because I have been on the calls. I am not writing a sentence that settles them. I need one sentence. I do not receive it in this document.

## The desk this note is about

The desk is the Vancouver desk of Northline Access Collaborative, in British Columbia. The time zone of the office is America/Vancouver. Dana Okonkwo directs the desk. Morgan Ibarra supervises it. Noel Park sits the clerk chair with me. Evan Cho is the privacy officer. None of them is the author of this note. I am not signing their names under a sentence they have not written. I am telling them what the phone requires, and I am leaving the sentence itself unwritten because I have not been given it.

Cedar Ridge is a rural clinic in Washington. It files at night, including weekends, and it has no slot line I can query while a member is still on the phone. Cascade Seattle is a hospital in Washington. Jordan Hale keeps the records there. Fraser Burnaby is a hospital in British Columbia. Priya Shah is a clinician there. Alex Kim is the on-call physician. Harbor Coverage is the payer. Ruth Alvarez is a clerk on that side. Chris Pell is finance at Northline. Sam Nguyen files the Cedar Ridge night. I name them because a member on my phone has usually already spoken to one of their offices, or has been told a figure that came from one of their offices. I am not moving their work onto my script. I am saying that my script still has a hole where one sentence should be.

Riley Chen owns the 2024 assistant. Harper Singh left in March 2026. Wes Calder left in 2020 and wrote a clock page in 2019. I am not asking the assistant to speak for me, and I am not asking a page from 2019 to become the sentence. Bobbi Tran keeps three logs. I can ask Bobbi what characters are on a row I name. I cannot ask Bobbi to finish a sentence the row does not contain. Omar Farouk watches the night at Cascade. Anita Desai manages the clinic day at Fraser Burnaby. Lena Brooks supervises Harbor Coverage in Seattle. Their nights and their clinic days are not my phone. My phone is the office day at this desk.

## What the phone already has to do

On an office day, Monday through Friday, a member calls this bay. I answer with the name of the desk. I take the member's name and the referral id they have, if they have one. I do not read a clinician's letter into the call. I do not read a draft hours page into the call. I do not tell the member a visit is booked at Cedar Ridge because the member hopes it is, and I do not tell them it is booked because another office used the word tonight. Cedar Ridge does not give me a slot to confirm. A file id is not, by itself, a booking I am willing to upgrade into a spoken promise. If the visit is not something I can point at, I say I cannot confirm it. That limit is already the way I work. This note does not relax it.

I also do not read a coverage row I have not been given in a form I can repeat. Ruth records Harbor's answers. I am not Ruth. If I do not have the payer dates, I do not invent them. If I have heard the word timeout about some other check, I do not borrow that word onto the member who is actually on the line, and I do not borrow a window from a different referral to make this one sound finished. A guess I make because the member is upset is still a guess. I will not stand on it. The phone can carry a refusal to guess. The phone cannot, today, carry the one sentence this note is asking for, because that sentence has not been given to me.

When the member asks what happens next, I can say that the desk has the referral if the desk has it, and that I will call back on the next office morning if the question arrives after the Friday close. A Saturday call, if one reaches a recording, is not an office-day answer. I do not come in on Saturday to clear a list by reading a number I would not read on Friday. The office week is the boundary of the speech. Inside that boundary I am still missing the sentence the member asks for when the member asks how long.

## The three numbers people quote

People quote three numbers. I am listing them because they arrive on the same calls, sometimes in the same hour, and a member hears them as if they were one fact with three outfits. They are not one fact in my ear. I am not ranking them. I am not dropping two of them so the third can become the sentence. The list is the reason I need one sentence. The list is not the sentence.

The first number people quote is the mart. They mean the facility mart. A figure sits there, and someone has read it aloud, or a member has seen it, or a clerk has been told that the figure on the mart is the figure the desk should trust. I have heard the mart quoted as a wait. I have not been told, in a sentence I can repeat, that the mart is the only number the phone may use, and I have not been told that the mart is a number the phone must refuse. Dana has talked about one number. I have heard that talk. Talk is not the sentence on this page. I am not writing the mart figure into a script, and I am not striking the mart figure out of the list. It is the first of the three numbers people quote.

The second number people quote is the desk count. Morgan's bay has a count that starts when the desk takes the call. Clerks quote it. Members repeat it back to me as if I had been the person who started it. Sometimes the desk count and the mart are close enough that a tired reading treats them as the same digits. Sometimes they are not close. I am not measuring the gap in this note, and I am not deciding that a gap means the desk count wins or that the absence of a gap means the mart wins. The desk count is the second number people quote. It is a number I hear. It is not a sentence I have been handed.

The third number people quote is the hospital accept. They mean a figure that arrives with the news that Cascade has accepted a referral into the hospital record, or with the news that Cascade has not. Jordan keeps that record. People quote an accept as if the accept were a wait the member has already spent, or as if the absence of an accept were a wait that has not started. I do not keep Cascade's board. I do not mark an accept from this phone. When a caller quotes the hospital accept, I can hear that they are quoting a third number. I cannot turn that quotation into the one sentence this bay is supposed to say, because I have not been given that sentence, and because I will not build it by preferring the hospital accept over the mart or over the desk count.

Those are the three. Mart. Desk count. Hospital accept. A member who has heard all three in one week will ask me which one I am standing on. That question is the phone call this note is about. I can list the three. A list is not a choice. I have told members I would not join competing accounts into a calmer story. I am telling this note the same thing. I need one sentence. The sentence would be the thing I can say once, on the phone, on an office day, that a member can repeat. It is not in this document. Nobody has written it underneath my name. I do not receive it here.

## What I am asking for, and what I am not asking for

I am asking that member services be able to say one sentence on the phone. The sentence has to be sayable by me and by Noel without a supervisor standing at the chair to pick a number in the moment. It has to survive a callback on the next office morning, so Friday's speech and Monday's speech are not two inventions. It has to be one sentence, not a paragraph that offers the mart and then apologizes with the desk count and then mentions the hospital accept so that every office feels included. Inclusion of every office is how the member gets three numbers. I am not asking for a paragraph.

I am not asking for a caption under a screen. I am not asking anyone to name the event a label on a screen requires. I am not asking which clock a board is allowed to use, and I am not awarding a clock to a screen. People in this building argue about clocks. Wes's page is still in a binder. I have heard the argument. This note does not award it. If a later reader wants a time painted on a display, that reader will not find the paint here. The phone is speech. The sentence I need is speech. A clock is a different argument, and I am not settling it in order to sound definite on a Thursday.

I am not asking finance to pick a currency so the phone can sound finished. Harbor's sheet can carry more than one currency word. Chris has not handed me a word to say, and I am not choosing one in this note. I am not asking records to define how long the member has waited. Jordan's accept is the third number people quote. It is not a definition I am importing. I am not asking Sam to hold a Saturday file until Monday so my sentence can be easier. His night is his night. The office week here remains Monday through Friday. A weekend file does not become the sentence.

I am not asking Evan to review a new disclosure inside this note. The sentence, when it exists, still may not be a reading of a letter. The audience of a letter stays with the clinician. I will not receive a sentence that is really a letter read aloud. If the only way to sound informed is to read a letter, then that way is not available, and the missing sentence is still missing. I would rather say I cannot give the sentence than read the wrong paper into a member's ear.

## How a call goes when the sentence is missing

I want the missing sentence to be visible as a gap, not smoothed into a tone of voice. This is the shape of the call I am already taking.

The member says they were given a number. I ask which number. They say the mart, or they say a count from the desk, or they say something a hospital told them about an accept. Sometimes they say two of those in one breath. I write down what they say they heard. Writing it down is not agreement. I tell them I have the referral if I have it. I tell them I am not able to give them one sentence that joins the numbers. I offer a callback on an office day if we are at the edge of Friday. I do not fill the pause with a fourth number I composed while they were talking. A fourth number would be worse than the three, because it would be mine, and I do not have authority to mint one.

Noel has the same hole. If he and I each invent a sentence so the queue will move, the member will eventually hear both inventions and will call them a fourth and a fifth number. Morgan can hold the line. A holding line is not the sentence. Dana can want one number on a screen. A want is not the sentence I can say today, on 7 January, into a handset. I am uneasy when I speak a figure I do not understand. I have been uneasy on calls already. This note is not a confession that I will keep speaking figures anyway. It is a requirement that the bay be given one sentence, and a record that the sentence did not arrive with the requirement.

If the member asks me to pick the mart because it was the shortest figure, I do not pick it. If the member asks me to pick the desk count because they remember the minute they called, I do not pick it. If the member asks me to pick the hospital accept because a hospital sounds more final than a desk, I do not pick it. Final is not a test I am allowed to apply from this chair. The three numbers stay listed. The sentence stays unreceived.

## What success would look like, without pretending it has happened

Success, if it ever occurs, is a later page. It is not this page. Success would be one sentence, written by someone whose job is to own it, that member services can say on the phone during Monday through Friday, that does not require the clerk to choose among the mart, the desk count, and the hospital accept while the member is listening. Success would leave the clerk able to say that sentence twice, on two office days, without drifting. Success would not be a screen caption. Success would not be a clock. Success would not be a currency. Success would not be a definition of a wait that records has refused to give me.

I cannot mark success on 7 January 2027. I have listed the three numbers people quote. I have written that I need one sentence. I have not received it. There is no reply clipped under this note. There is no sentence in the margin. There is no spoken line I am willing to put in quotation marks and call the answer. If a reader searches this document for the sentence, the reader will find the need and will not find the sentence. That emptiness is the status of the requirement. I am not ashamed of the emptiness. I would be ashamed of filling it.

## Out of scope

Clinic hours at Cedar Ridge, Cascade Seattle, and Fraser Burnaby are out of scope. The nightly file is out of scope. A quotation, a contract fee, and any figure Chris journals are out of scope. Letter handling is out of scope. A privacy review is out of scope. French wording is out of scope. A second screen at Cascade is out of scope. Whether a Saturday file belongs on a clinic tally is out of scope. Coverage words on a payer row are out of scope. I will not smuggle those subjects in by calling them part of the phone sentence.

The wiring between a mart and a display is out of scope. I am a voice on a phone. I am not specifying a path through a system. A design discussion that does not change the sentence I can say is not required before this note can be true. The note is already true: the sentence is required, and the sentence is not here.

## Open point

The open point is the sentence. I am not closing it by silence, and I am not closing it by choosing a number in the last section so the document can look finished. Mart, desk count, hospital accept: those are the three numbers people quote. I need one sentence. I do not receive it in this document.
