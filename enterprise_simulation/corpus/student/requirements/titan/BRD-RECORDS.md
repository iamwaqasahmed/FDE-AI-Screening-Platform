# What accepted means in Cascade records after the Inlet merger

Northline Access Collaborative
Cascade Seattle
Records
Business requirements
Date: 15 September 2026
Author: Jordan Hale, records

## Purpose

This is a business requirements note from Cascade records. I am Jordan Hale. I am writing it on Tuesday, 15 September 2026, which is an office day. Cascade Seattle is a hospital in Washington. The time zone of this records office is America/Los_Angeles. The office week is Monday through Friday. Saturday and Sunday are not days on which this office accepts a referral into the hospital record, and they are not days on which this office moves the board. I am writing because Inlet Medical merged into Cedar Ridge on 1 September 2026, and old ids are still arriving on paper that people want me to treat as an accept. I am writing what accepted means in this office after that merger. I am not writing what a member has been waiting.

The Vancouver desk of Northline Access Collaborative has its own office week, Monday through Friday, in America/Vancouver. Dana Okonkwo directs that desk. Morgan Ibarra supervises it. June Lau and Noel Park answer the phone there. Their week is their week. I am not moving Cascade records onto their counter, and I am not moving their counter onto this board. Sam Nguyen files the night at Cedar Ridge, including weekends. Cedar Ridge has no slot line. Pat Morales was the office manager at Inlet Medical. Omar Farouk is the night supervisor at this hospital. He watches the night tray. He does not accept a referral. A packet that arrives after Friday close, or on Saturday, or on Sunday, stays in that tray until the next office morning.

## What accepted means here

Accepted, in Cascade records, means a records act on an office day. I write the referral into the hospital record at Cascade Seattle and I mark that record accepted. I write the referral id, the site name Cascade Seattle, and the office date of the accept in the working book. The mark in the working book is the accept. Until that mark is in the working book, the referral is not accepted in this office, whatever a caller, a nightly file, an old site id, or another office has already said.

The order is the order I already keep. First the packet is identified. Then the site is confirmed as Cascade Seattle. Then the mark is written. Then, and only then, the board may change. I do not change the board first and write the mark later. I do not accept by telephone and leave the book for the afternoon. If I am interrupted after the mark and before the board, the accept stands and the board is brought into line before I leave. If I am interrupted before the mark, there has been no accept, and the board is left as it was.

A correction later the same office day is a correction of the record, not a second accept. I strike the wrong line, write the corrected line, and record that the referral was already accepted. I do not add a second accept because a clinic name on the packet was amended. If the accept itself was made in error and I reverse it the same office day, I mark the reversal in the working book and the board count goes down by one. A reversal is a reversal. It is not a quiet omission, and it is not a way to retire an old id that never became an accept.

That is what accepted means in this office. It is a mark in a book on an office day, for a packet that is for Cascade Seattle. It is not a mood. It is not a phone call. It is not a file name that survived a merger. It is not a figure someone hopes a member will hear as a wait.

## What the merger did not change

Inlet Medical was a Washington clinic. On 1 September 2026 it merged into Cedar Ridge, which is a rural Washington clinic. The merger is a fact I was told. The merger is not an accept into Cascade Seattle. Cedar Ridge is not this hospital. A nightly file Sam releases is not, by itself, an accept into this hospital's record. The merger did not give Cedar Ridge a slot line, and it did not give this office permission to invent one so that a Cedar Ridge file can be treated as a Cascade accept. Fraser Burnaby is not Cascade Seattle. A letter that stays with a clinician at Fraser is not an accept here. A sentence spoken on a phone in Vancouver is not an accept here. A row someone describes as already on a facility mart is not an accept here.

I have heard each of those offered as if they were the same act as the mark in my working book. Under this note they are not the act. The merger made that confusion louder, because paper that used to say Inlet now sits in a Cedar Ridge cabinet and still says Inlet on some of the slips. Loud paper is still paper. It is not the mark.

Omar does not perform the accept on a night shift, including a night after the merger. The night tray holds the packet. The accept waits for the office day. A Monday accept is not backdated onto Sunday because the packet was in the tray while the building was quiet. Quiet is not an accept. The office date of the accept is the office date I write when I make the mark.

## Old ids

Old ids still appear. I am writing that as a fact of the paper, not as a decision to keep them and not as a decision to retire them. The old site id I have been shown is inlet-medical. It still appears on files after 1 September 2026. I have not retired it in this note. I have not been given a rule, from an authority over both clinics, that tells me the old site id vanished at midnight after the join. Pat Morales wrote, before the join, that historical file ids were an open question. I am not closing her question from Seattle. Sam wrote, on 2 September, that two file names were in one jacket. I have been shown those names. I am not striking either of them.

The names I have been shown for that jacket are inlet-2026-11-02, still carrying the old site id inlet-medical, and cedar-r3a-2026-11-02, carrying the site id cedar-ridge. The referral on the jacket is R3A-MERGE. Sam clipped both names and did not stamp a winner. I am not stamping a winner in this note. I do not know which id a payer uses. Harbor Coverage is the payer named on face sheets that came with Inlet paper. Their id is not a thing I can see by looking at my working book, because the referral is not accepted here merely by being named on a slip that crossed a merger.

An old id is not an accept. A new id printed beside an old id is not an accept. Both ids on one jacket are not two accepts, and they are not one accept. They are names on paper that arrived after a merger. If a packet for Cascade Seattle later comes to this office and I mark it accepted in the working book, that mark will say Cascade Seattle and will carry the office date I write. The mark will not consist of the string inlet-medical. The mark will not consist of the string cedar-ridge. Those strings can remain on the packet as the history of where the paper lived. History on a packet is not the accept. I will write the old id in the note that describes the packet, so the next clerk can see it. Writing it in the note is not accepting it, and it is not deleting it.

I have heard two sentences since the join. One sentence says the old site id must keep working for historical files. Another sentence says the old site id was retired on the merger date. I am not choosing between those sentences. Neither sentence, on its own, tells me that Cascade has accepted a referral. The accept is still the mark. The sentences about the old id are sentences about the old id. I leave both of them standing because I was not given the choice, and because this note would become a different document if I pretended I had been.

## The board

The board in this records office counts referrals this office has accepted and has not reversed. The unit of the count is the accept. One referral accepted once adds one. A second conversation about the same referral does not add one. A Cedar Ridge file that mentions the referral does not add one. An old Inlet id on a slip does not add one. A call answered in Vancouver does not add one. A figure on a facility mart does not add one. A packet sitting in Omar's night tray does not add one. A merger date does not add one.

When I have marked the accept in the working book, I add one to the board for that referral if the referral was not already on the count. When I have marked a same-day reversal, I subtract one if the referral was on the count. I write the new count in the board book with the office date. I do not copy a count from the Vancouver desk into this board. I do not copy a count from Harbor Coverage into this board. I do not copy a clinic tally from Cedar Ridge into this board. Chris Pell may ask what number should sit beside a later display. This note answers only for the board I keep: the count of accepts not reversed. It does not answer for a journal. It does not choose a currency word. Harbor's sheet can carry more than one currency word. I am not signing one of them because a board exists.

This office also keeps a copy that is one version behind the working book. Staff here call it the copy the desk saw yesterday. An accept marked this morning is in the working book. It is not yet on the copy the desk saw yesterday. When the Vancouver desk asks what Cascade has accepted, the answer names the copy it was read from. If the two copies disagree, both readings are written, and each reading is tied to the copy it came from. This note does not say which reading prevails. This note does not tell the desk to show one of them on a screen. The disagreement stays visible because the copy is one version behind.

The copy is refreshed when I set the day's working book aside at the close of the office day. Omar does not refresh it at night. A Friday close leaves Friday's version as the copy the desk will still be looking at until Monday's office work moves the working book. Weekend tray packets are not accepts and do not appear on either copy until I accept them on an office day. The merger did not create a weekend accept. Inlet paper that arrives on a Saturday is still tray paper until an office morning.

The board starts, for a referral, when the referral is accepted into the hospital record in the sense this note has already given: the mark in the working book. I am describing this board. I am not describing a Vancouver screen. I am not describing a facility mart. I am not describing Morgan's desk count. I have not adopted those objects. Hearing them is not the same as moving this office. I will not say what a screen may use. I am not awarding a clock to a screen, and I am not telling a reader which clock a board is allowed to use. People argue about clocks. The argument is not awarded by the existence of this board.

## I do not define the member's wait

I do not define the member's wait. I need that sentence in its own section because the rest of this note will be misread if I leave it as a hint.

The member's wait is not the accept. The member's wait is not the board count. The member's wait is not the office date on the mark. The member's wait is not the gap between a merger date and an office morning. The member's wait is not the age of an old id. The member's wait is not the difference between inlet-2026-11-02 and cedar-r3a-2026-11-02. The member's wait is not a number I will speak so that June has something to say on the phone. June may need a sentence. She does not receive that sentence from this note. I am not supplying it under another name by defining a wait.

If a member asks this counter whether a referral has been accepted, the answer is yes or no from the mark in the working book, and the answer is about the hospital record at Cascade Seattle. The answer is not a reading of how long the member has waited. If the question is what the desk was told, the clerk uses the copy the desk saw yesterday and says so. Those two answers may differ while the copy is one version behind. I do not smooth them into a wait. I do not pick a time of day and declare it the time a screen should carry. The working book has an office date on the accept. The copy has the office date of the version it still shows. Both dates can be written when both are asked for. Neither date is a definition of the member's wait, and neither date is awarded to a screen by this note.

Dana may want one figure on a Vancouver screen. Morgan may start a desk count when the desk takes a call. A facility mart may hold a figure. I am not saying those practices are wrong, and I am not saying they are right. I am saying they are not this board, and they are not a definition I am writing for the member's wait. This document does not define the member's wait. A reader who needs that definition will not find it in these pages. I will not add it in the margin on 15 September.

## Worked packet, not a worked wait

Take the jacket Sam described for R3A-MERGE. Two file names. One old site id still printed. One newer site id printed beside it. The member region on the face sheet has been described to me as British Columbia. The building the paper sits in now is in Washington. None of that is an accept at Cascade. I do not add one to the board. I do not start the board for that referral because a merger week is noisy and a noisy week wants a number. I do not tell Vancouver how long that member has waited. I do not know the member's wait, and I am not going to learn it by staring at two file names.

If, on a later office day, a packet for this hospital is identified, the site is confirmed, and I write the mark, then the referral will be accepted in the sense of this note, and the board will count that accept. The old ids will still be written in the packet note if they are still on the paper. The member's wait will still be undefined by this document. Acceptance will not have become a wait by occurring. That is the whole of the example. I am not picking which of the two file names is the live file. Sam did not pick it. I am not picking it for him.

## Out of scope

Coverage answers at Harbor are out of scope. A currency word is out of scope. A journal line is out of scope. What member services says on the phone is out of scope, except that this note does not hand them a wait. Clinic hours at Fraser Burnaby are out of scope. Whether Sam counts a Saturday file on a clinic tally is out of scope. I archive a trailer page when he releases a file and the count matches. I do not assign his file id. I do not turn his Saturday into an accept.

Letter text is out of scope. I do not read a clinician's letter in order to decide an accept. Evan Cho's questions about letters stay with Evan. The audience of a letter is not this board.
