# What people said

You spend the next two days in the building. These are the notes. The files, which you open in the next note, are what you can count. Where a person and a file disagree, show the disagreement. Do not average it away.

Roles in brackets are the plain-language reminder from [Health language](../../health_language.md).

**Director of Screening Programs** (runs the public screening programs). Breast screening meets its coverage target. Cervical screening is close. Diabetic eye screening is the program they cannot defend in front of the ministry. They have been saying the registry holds about 84,000 people with diabetes, that coverage is about 41%, and that rural coverage is around a quarter. They do not have a source for the rural figure. It is the number the briefing keeps repeating. Photos go to three graders. One of them has been away. Results go out by letter. Ophthalmology says too many people are referred, and also that the urgent people wait too long.

**Ophthalmology clinical lead** (the eye specialist who would receive referrals). Do not send a longer list. The routine queue is already past fourteen weeks. A draft note talks about seeing sight-threatening disease within 14 days. Nobody has issued that note. Last year's model marked a large share of hospital photos as positive. Specificity was not on the slide. A grader must remain accountable for any result that changes care. A score can sort a queue. A score cannot discharge a patient or create a referral.

**Nurse at a Hill Counties primary-care site** (takes the photo at a local clinic). Patients travel a long way, longer from the river towns. The nurse takes the photo and does not know if it is usable. The file goes in a shared folder called `EYE SCREEN 2024 final final`. If the picture is no good, the patient has already gone home before anyone says so. The nurse can keep someone for another ten minutes if something on the screen says to retake the photo. The nurse cannot invent a specialist appointment.

**Grader** (reads the retinal photo and records the code). The queue is the workbook. Blank results do not sort themselves. The clinic that calls gets opened first. Some sight-threatening cases are written as a code, and some are written only in the notes. Mild referable disease and sight-threatening disease still leave on one referral list.

**Data protection officer.** A previous pilot stored images outside the authority network and was paused. The written rule is the one they will enforce. They will review a bounded service with a data map. They will not review one release that covers breast, cervical, and eye screening.

**IT manager.** The electronic health record team can start an integration in about fourteen months. What exists now is the extract you are about to open: a registry file, a workbook, a hospital manifest, a shared-folder listing, letters, and referrals. There is no API. Hospital captures are on the PACS manifest. Primary-care and outreach captures are on the shared-folder listing. The cover note tells you how to join the files. IT did not write that note.

**Breast screening program lead.** That program meets its standard. Two readers already review every set. A shared AI platform creates a safety case they did not ask for. Leave that program out of the first release.

**Vendor.** On 1,200 hospital images, sensitivity for referable disease was 93%. Those images came from dilated patients on hospital cameras. Dilated means the pupil was widened with drops so the photo would be clearer. Specificity is "available under NDA." The model returns nothing when it judges an image ungradable. The contract in procurement is a regional license for eye, breast, and cervical screening.

**Finish.** You can separate the Chief Digital Officer's goal from what the grader, the nurse, and the ophthalmology lead will not accept.
