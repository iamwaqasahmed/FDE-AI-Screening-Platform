# The public line

**Days 2 and 3. Be the consultant, then the product manager.** The query has four questions. The line answers the ones the files support and refuses the ones they do not. Two paragraphs. Plain English. Suitable to publish.

## Who signs it

`raci.csv`, the row for press and ministerial responses. Responsible drafts. Accountable approves. Consulted is read before it is drafted. Informed is told, not asked to approve. The agent may draft. It may not send.

## What the line can say

- Screening results are still decided by trained graders. No AI system makes decisions about anyone's care.
- Any AI pilot needs a named clinical safety owner, a signed safety case, and data protection approval first.
- A letter bureau problem between the two dates on the Week 4 request file is being reconciled, and affected people are being contacted.
- Anyone worried about an appointment or a letter should contact the screening programme.

Those four sentences are the line. You may split them across the two paragraphs. Do not add a fact the files do not support.

## What it cannot say

Anything about one person, including whether someone lost sight. A pilot date, either the one requested or the one on the roadmap. That the system is approved, certified, or in use. Blame on staff, graders, or a named supplier. A count of people harmed. That every letter has been accounted for. An internal upper bound, or any count from the chase list, presented as the number of people waiting.

The query names a clinic and describes a reader. The line does not repeat either.

## Stop. Write the line.

Use [the press-line template](templates/press-line.md) and save it as `press-line.md`. Exactly two paragraphs, separated by a blank line. No heading.
